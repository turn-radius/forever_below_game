# The look

Spline Armory's renderer sets the house style: banded toon light, ink outlines, and wear in the shader. Keep the style and raise the fidelity (better meshes and normals, real light and shadow from carried light sources, richer materials). Everything here is read from `reference/spline_armory_engine.js` and `reference/forever-below-plate.html`. Search those files for the names in `code`.

## Toon shading

Materials are three.js `MeshToonMaterial` with a 1-pixel-tall gradient map sampled with nearest filtering, so light falls off in hard bands.

- Spline Armory (`makeGradient(n)`): band `i` of `n` has value `0.12 + 0.88 * i/(n-1)`. Default `n = 3` (`S.render.bands`), giving 0.12, 0.56, 1.0.
- The plate uses `0.16 + 0.84 * i/(n-1)` with `n = 3`, giving 0.16, 0.58, 1.0, a little lighter in the shadows.

In Bevy, use a custom material (WESL) or an `ExtendedMaterial` on `StandardMaterial` that quantizes the diffuse term into these bands. Keep the band count and values as material parameters.

## Ink outline

This is an inverted hull, drawn for every mesh unless that part opts out.

- A second mesh shares the geometry and is drawn with **back faces only**. Its vertex shader pushes each vertex out along an **outline normal** by width `w`, then writes a flat ink colour.
- Outline normals are **smoothed across hard edges**: weld vertices by position (rounded to 1e-4) and average the face normals (`withOutlineNormals`). Without this, faceted parts crack open at the corners. Surfaces that are already smooth use their own normals (`smoothOutline`).
- Ink colour: `#1a1f22` in the Armory, `#1c1814` (a warm near-black) in the plate.
- The width is in world units, rescaled to the view so the line reads the same at any zoom. In the Armory, `w = outline * clamp(viewHeight/9, 0.45, 2.2)` with `outline = 0.032`. Jewellery, food and bags use a floor of 0.02 instead of 0.45. In the plate, `w = 0.028 * clamp(frustum/9, 0.18, 2.2)`.

For first person this becomes a function of distance to the camera. Keep the same feel: a line about 1.5–2.5 px wide on screen, thinner on small things.

## Wear, rust and grime (the decay shader)

Each material carries a **type** and an **amount**. Its fragment shader mixes in procedural decay from 3D value noise (4-octave fbm) in item space, so the pattern stays fixed to the object as it turns (`DECAY_HEAD`, `DECAY_BODY`, `decayAmt`).

| type | material | what decay does |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | iron (`metal`) | dulls, rust blooms, pitting |
| 2 | brass (`accent`) | tarnish, verdigris |
| 3 | wood | grain, silvering, rot, moss |
| 4 | leather, string | darkening, scuffs, grime |
| 5 | gems and stones | clouding, fractures |
| 6 | gold | soft dulling, grime in hollows |
| 7 | silver | tarnish, warm toning, black patches |
| 8 | bone | yellowing, stains, hairline cracks |
| 9 | stone | weathering, lichen |
| 10 | cloth | fading, grime, moth holes |
| 11 | paint over wood | fading, chips showing the boards |
| 12 | food | browning, going stale, mould |
| 13 | skin and hair (`fb_body`, ours) | the character's dirt: a dull film, then smudges pooling low on the body |

The amount is `cond.wear` times a per-material multiplier (`cond.rust`, `cond.patina`, `cond.rot`, `cond.fray`). The same shader also draws fabric patterns (stripes, checks, tartan, lozenges, quilting, mail rings, scales, rivets, heraldic divisions) and etching (scrollwork, border bands, knotwork, chevrons, diaper, runes) as dark engraving or gold inlay.

The plate adds a character-level **dirt** and **wear** on its own body and clothing materials: grime pools low on the body, clothes fray and get moth holes. Both are driven by the character's conditions.

## Light

- The Armory lights from the camera: a key light of 0.78 at azimuth −40°, elevation 42°, plus ambient `#dfe6ee` at 0.42.
- The plate uses a directional key of `#fff1dc` at 0.62 from (−6, 10, 9), plus ambient `#b6b0c8` at 0.42.
- A torch is a warm point light (`#ffb36a`, intensity 0.55, range 16, quadratic falloff) with flicker `f = .88 + .08 sin(13.1t) + .05 sin(7.3t + 1.2) + .03 sin(23.7t)` and an additive halo sprite.

In the abyss, carried light is the main light. Ambient should be very low so torches, lanterns and glowcaps matter (the light radius is a game stat).

## In the Bevy build

Where the Rust renderer (`game/src/render/`) departs from the prototypes:

- Bands are applied per light, to `N·L * 0.5 + 0.5` as three.js toon shading does, so each carried light adds its own hard steps. A fragment in shadow drops to the lowest band.
- Point and spot lights fall off with distance in hard steps of their own, on top of the `N·L` bands (`toon.wesl`, `reach_attenuation` and `reach_step`; `light::reach_falloff` mirrors it). The falloff is Brian Karis's windowed inverse square from [Real Shading in Unreal Engine 4](https://cdn2.unrealengine.com/Resources/files/2013SiggraphPresentationsNotes-26915738.pdf) (SIGGRAPH 2013, eq. 9), `saturate(1 - (d/r)^4)^2 / (d^2 + 1)`, which Bevy's `getDistanceAttenuation` also uses without the `+1`. Our own choices on top of it: the `+1` becomes `(0.4 r)^2`, so every light has the same shape across its reach `r` and none climbs without bound at its source; each light's display strength is capped at full and rounded up to quarters, so a wall beside the flame is full light rather than burnt white, the light steps down across its reach, and its dimmest step ends at `r`. A light is set by its strength close up (`light::peak_lumens`) and its reach.
- Flames flicker in the toon shader, after their strength is cut into reach steps (`flame_flicker` in `toon.wesl`), not by scaling `PointLight::intensity` on the CPU. Scaling the intensity, as the prototype did with its torch, moved every step boundary as the strength swung between 0.72 and 1.04 of full, so rings of light swept across walls and floors and whole walls popped a step at a time; under a steady camera at seed 1207's start, up to 47% of the screen jumped a full step between two consecutive frames. Now each light's intensity is steady, the steps hold still, and only their brightness wavers by the prototype's curve, `f = .88 + .08 sin(13.1t) + .05 sin(7.3t + 1.2) + .03 sin(23.7t)` (`Torch::flicker` mirrors it), with `t` the view's time (`globals.time`, which Bevy wraps every hour) plus a lag from the light's position (`FLICKER_LAG`, 7.1 and 5.3 ms per unit along x and z), so neighbouring flames waver out of step and a carried light's flicker stays smooth as it moves. The steps now sit where the light's full strength puts them, rather than wandering around its average. Bevy 0.20's clustered light struct (`ClusteredLight` in `bevy_pbr-0.20.0/src/render/mesh_view_types.wesl`) carries no free per-light field, so a flame's flicker depth (0 steady, 1 the full curve, `LightAnchor::flicker`) rides in `PointLight::radius`: Bevy reads `radius` only for specular highlights and percentage-closer soft shadows, neither of which the toon shader draws or this build enables. Glowcaps and the debug light keep radius 0 and stay steady. The channel and the lag are our own convention (`light::Torch`, `light::flicker_depth`).
- A lit item's light reaches twice its `LightAnchor` radius: the radius is bright light and dim light runs as far again, as a torch's does in the [D&D 5e SRD 5.1](https://media.wizards.com/2016/downloads/DND/SRD-OGL_V5.1.pdf) ("Adventuring Gear", Torch: "bright light in a 20-foot radius and dim light for an additional 20 feet"). A torch lights the floor out to about five cells and leaves the dark beyond it.
- Every light in the level casts shadows: braziers, glowcaps, the carried light, and lit things lying on the floor, so no light reaches through rock into the next corridor. A lit thing on the floor casts no shadow itself (`Propagate(NotShadowCaster)` on its root), and flames and glowing caps on fixtures cast none, or they would black out their own light. Bevy 0.20 renders a cube shadow map (six faces at `PointLightShadowMap::size`, 1024 by default) every frame for each shadowed point light, up to `max_texture_array_layers / 6` of them (`prepare_lights` in `bevy_pbr-0.20.0/src/render/light.rs`), and each face draws only the meshes inside the light's range and that face's frustum (`check_point_light_mesh_visibility` in `bevy_light-0.20.0/src/lib.rs`). Seed 1207 has about ten shadowed lights, short-reaching except the braziers, and the rock is drawn in 16-cell chunks, so each face draws a few chunks. If many more lights come, lowering `PointLightShadowMap::size` or shadowing only lights near the player are the next steps. Clipping each light to the cells it can see was the alternative; shadows also keep a pillar or a doorway's jamb from being lit on its far side.
- Every item part has a `ToonMaterial` of its own (its wear seed, pattern and etching differ), so the toon extension is bindless where the device allows it, as `StandardMaterial` is (`#[bindless(index_table(range(100..101), binding(102)))]` on `ToonExtension`, after Bevy's `AsBindGroup` docs and its `extended_material_bindless` example): all the parts' uniforms sit in one array behind one bind group, found by the material slot in the mesh uniform (`toon_of` in `toon.wesl`). Bevy's shadow pass binds the material of anything whose material has a prepass shader, which `StandardMaterial` and so every `ExtendedMaterial` on it does (`prepass_reads_material` in `bevy_pbr-0.20.0/src/material.rs`), and draws by bind group. Without bindless, seed 1207's 700 or so part materials were 700 bind groups, nothing batched, and every face of every shadow cube drew the parts one at a time: about 50 ms a frame at the start in a debug build, 17 ms (the 60 Hz display's own) with it (#148).
- Every view draws its meshes directly, not through Bevy's GPU-built indirect draws (`render/indirect.rs`, #163). Bevy 0.20 picks its GPU mesh preprocessing mode from the device (`GpuPreprocessingSupport` in `bevy_render-0.20.0/src/batching/gpu_preprocessing.rs`). In `Culling` mode, this M5's default, the bins of each multidraw stay on the GPU from frame to frame and compute shaders unpack them, give each draw its instance slots and build the indirect draws (`unpack_bins`, `allocate_uniforms` and `mesh_preprocess` in `bevy_pbr-0.20.0/src/render/`), fed by sparse uploads of the mesh input buffer (`render_resource/sparse_buffer_vec.rs`). With the toon extension bindless, a few hundred parts share one multidraw, each part its own bin, and on that path draws now and then took another part's instance data for whole runs of frames while the view turned: a dwarf in another's hat, straps in a crate's colour, and point light shadow maps holding parts in the wrong places, so a brazier's shadow came and went every other frame or the whole floor went dark. In 16 runs of 40 frames turning slowly in seed 1207's brazier room with three dwarves, 3 runs showed it (101 frames where the floor's brightness jumped). We capped the mode at `PreprocessingOnly`, the mode Bevy already uses where indirect first-instance is missing: mesh uniforms are still built in compute, but binning and batching are on the CPU and draws are direct. Point light shadow views take `NoIndirectDrawing` from that mode (`create_point_shadow_maps` in `bevy_pbr-0.20.0/src/render/light.rs`), so the camera-level `NoIndirectDrawing` alone would not reach them. After the change, 16 runs showed none, and frame times are the same or a little lower (see the commit). How we narrowed it down: a non-bindless toon extension shows none of it (each part its own multidraw, but #148's cost comes back). Turning off GPU preprocessing completely (`PbrPlugin::use_gpu_instance_buffer_builder: false`) draws a black level with this renderer. A serial uniform allocation in place of the three-step scan still shows it. Bevy's CPU-side bins stay consistent every frame, and no bin points at a stale input slot. Full uploads instead of sparse ones make it rare but do not remove it. We did not find the exact line in Bevy. This is our own workaround, and worth dropping once a Bevy release fixes the GPU bin path.
- One lower leg of a folk archer drawn solid black in a brazier room (#151) is not in the fitted geometry: on every person of seeds 1 to 16 and 1207 each fitted part passes `Part::validate`, winds with its shading and outline normals over at least 75% of its area (the worst is 91.5%, smooth normals leaning past thin faces at folds), and is drawn in a finite colour well above black (`folk::tests::every_fitted_folk_part_faces_out_and_is_coloured`). Close captures of every archer standing by a brazier on seeds 1, 4 and 1207, from all sides, show both legs lit. The report matches the indirect-draw fault above, where parts took another part's instance data and shadow maps held parts in the wrong places; it came before that fix, and one 60-frame turning run with the cap lifted did not show it again (the fault showed in 3 of 16 runs). Our reading, not a reproduction.
- The crash after a while of play (#171) came out of Bevy 0.20's GPU light clustering, and capping GPU preprocessing at `PreprocessingOnly` (the item above) is what stops it. GPU clustering reads the size of each view's light index list back from the GPU and grows the list to the next power of two of it, unchecked (`ViewClusteringReadbackData::update_from_metadata` in `bevy_pbr-0.20.0/src/cluster/gpu.rs`). In `Culling` mode the count read back for the player's camera was now and then garbage: a patched Bevy logging each readback over 2^20 saw 16 in four soaks of up to 20 minutes (`FB_SOAK`), from 1,062,885 to 2,323,105,500 indices for one to five lights in 17 by 9 by 24 clusters, where every light in every cluster would be at most 3,672 per light. Above 2^31, `u32::next_power_of_two` overflows inside the readback's buffer map callback ("attempt to add with overflow" at `core/src/num/mod.rs:1290:5`) while it holds the view's mutex, and the next lock panics the render thread with a `PoisonError`; between 2^30 and 2^31 it asks for an 8 GiB index buffer, which wgpu refuses. All four `Culling` soaks crashed one of these ways within 2 to 13 minutes, and four soaks in which the autopilot never fought (so no flash re-specialized creature parts every frame) ran 20 to 23 minutes clean. With the cap, four 20-minute soaks with fighting logged no resize of an index list (so no count past its 65,536 start) and did not crash. Our reading, not traced to a line in Bevy: the GPU bin path the cap avoids also wrote over the clustering's counts, as it wrote over other parts' instance data. Eight more `Culling` soaks with the cap taken out on top of it, stopped after 5 to 10 minutes, crashed twice at about 5 minutes with the same overflow and then the `PoisonError` (at `bevy_pbr-0.20.0/src/cluster/gpu.rs:1157`). The soak's report line gives the render world's preprocessing mode, which must read `PreprocessingOnly`, and `indirect`'s own test keeps the cap. Lights are also clustered on the CPU (`render::cluster_lights_on_the_cpu`), through `GlobalClusterSettings::gpu_clustering = None`, Bevy's documented switch (`bevy_light-0.20.0/src/cluster/mod.rs`), so the unchecked readback (`bevy_pbr-0.20.0/src/cluster/gpu.rs:403-406` and `419-421`, under the lock at `gpu.rs:1157`) never runs whatever the preprocessing mode. In seed 1207's brazier room (`FB_DEBUG_POSE=25,14.3`, `FB_FRAME_TIME=240,270,300`, debug build, vsync, three runs each, alternated) frame times were 16.1 to 17.2 ms with GPU clustering and 16.5 to 18.9 ms with CPU clustering, both at the 60 Hz display's 16.7 ms, and captures of the two match.
- Shadowed lights use depth and normal biases set for 10 cm units (`light::cast_shadows`). Bevy's defaults assume metres, and the torch's point shadow with them striped every wall it grazed with acne.
- Wear and light work on the display (sRGB) colour, as three.js r128 did with no output encoding: the prototype's wear colours are display values, and the summed light scales the display colour before it is converted to linear. Lit in linear space, the same band values read as nearly flat on screen.
- Rust, verdigris, gold grime, silver blotches, wood rot and moss cover a set share of the surface instead of a threshold that sweeps the whole item: fbm is rescaled to zero mean and unit spread, then cut at the quantile for the coverage the decay calls for. A grave find keeps about half its iron bare (blooms ringed with stain and pitted) and three quarters of its brass free of verdigris. Wear 0 still draws no decay.
- Wear and etching are band limited by the pixel footprint, as Inigo Quilez describes in [Band limited synthesis](https://iquilezles.org/articles/bandlimiting/): each noise octave, the rust crust, the pits, the verdigris mottle and wood grain fade to their mean once a cell of their lattice spans under four pixels and are gone by two (`detail` in `decay.wesl`, from `fwidth` of the item-space position), and etched lines fade once a repeat of their pattern is under about eight pixels. The prototype drew them at every distance, so far iron, door frames and folk's clothes shimmered with sub-pixel specks whenever the view moved. The pixel windows are our own.
- The Armory turntable (`game/src/turntable.rs`) lights like the Armory (`StudioLight::ARMORY`: a key riding on the camera plus ambient), draws on the Armory's plate colour with no tone curve, and closes in on one item (Enter or F; `FB_CLOSEUP=<slot>` for captures), so items can be judged beside `reference/spline-armory.html`. The grid is seen through an orthographic camera, as the Armory's plate is, so each cell is sized from the page's largest bounds (the width an item sweeps as it turns, and its full height) and the labels get a band of their own in pixels under the items. On the grid an item that lies flat (shorter than 0.3 of the width it sweeps: keys, lockpicks, a coiled rope) is tipped 0.6 rad toward the camera so its face shows, and an item under half the size of the page's largest is magnified to half, so a lockpick beside a bedroll is still readable; each label gives the item's true longest side and any magnification. Both are this build's own choice, not the Armory's. The close-up stays in perspective, level and at true size.
- The doll view (`--doll`, `game/src/doll_view.rs`) draws one dressed body (`game/src/figure.rs`) on the turntable's plate under the same studio light, turning as the turntable's items do, framed in perspective so the whole turning figure fills 86% of the view. Each worn layer and held item keeps its own palette, condition and etching, as on the floor; the skin wears with the character's dirt (0.2 there). A held light adds a warm point light at its flame reaching 0.9 of the body's height, our own tuning so the near side warms and the far side keeps the studio light. The doll is skinned like the folk in the level and can walk in place: W, or the panel at the top right (a walking toggle and a speed slider in multiples of the folk's natural pace; `--walk` and `--speed <n>` start it so).
- Folk are drawn as skinned meshes (`game/src/folk_pose.rs`, `docs/body.md`, "Posing"), so both vertex shaders skin. `toon.wesl` takes Bevy's `skin_model` and `skin_normals` (`bevy_pbr::render::skinning`) under the `SKINNED` shader def, as `bevy_pbr`'s own `mesh.wesl` does; `outline.wesl` takes `skin_model` and has `OutlineMaterial::specialize` lay the joint attributes out at locations 6 and 7. Its ink normal is skinned as a direction (the blended matrix applied and normalised), not with `skin_normals`: the inverse transpose threw ink spikes a unit long off every blended joint in captures of a walking doll. Wear, patterns and etching are painted in item space, which for a still item is the mesh's local space (the fragment shader inverts the mesh transform). A skinned mesh's transform no longer places it, so each vertex also carries where it rests, in the tangent attribute (`xyz`), and its rest normal, octahedral in the second uv set (`part_to_skinned_mesh`, `decode_octahedral` in `toon.wesl`), and the fragment shader takes its item-space position and normal from those, so the wear stays on a limb as it swings and does not swim across it. A skinned part and its ink hull each carry `DynamicSkinnedMeshBounds`, so a swung limb is culled by where it is.
- The outline width is set in pixels at 1080p (2 px by default) and converted to world units at each vertex's view depth, capped at 0.07 units so distant hulls don't balloon.
- Fine parts that opt out of the ink line (strings, chains, claws, small settings) are drawn at least 1.5 px wide at 1080p: the toon vertex shader pushes them out along their normals by the shortfall, estimated from the part's own half thickness (`2V / A`). A bowstring stays a visible line across a room and is untouched up close.
- Bands fall on the item's form, so relief finer than a band should not reach the shading normals. Under hard bands, a surface whose light sits near a band edge breaks into islands wherever small relief tips it over. The gambeson's quilting puffs stay in its silhouette, but the body shades with the normals of the coat without them; the quilting itself is drawn by the `Quilted` pattern: stitched seams and each puff shading down into them, laid out with the same `Wrap` as the puffs (`build::garment::Quilting`), whose scale is fitted so a whole number of cells goes round the plain coat. A drawstring bag's pleats shade with its turned profile the same way, and a faceted ring band shades as a round one: each flat facet crossed a band edge at its own point round the hoop, so the light stepped round the band in a staircase.
- A part sunk inside another, such as a barrel's heads in their chimes, draws no ink line: it never reaches the silhouette, and its outline hull pokes through thin walls around it in dashes. Barrel staves are bevelled at the chime, since a square end's thin rim left the far rim's outline normal nearly level and drew almost no line there.
- Decay noise is in mesh-local space, so item parts keep an identity transform under the item root.
- Paint chips scale with the decay amount; in the prototype they showed at wear 0.
- A part shows its wear scaled by `1 - glow` (`light_fastness` in `render/item.rs`), so a flame (glow 1) never clouds and a worn brazier keeps a bright flame. In the prototype every gem material clouded alike, flames included, and a grave find's flame went pale peach. Graded rather than all or nothing (our own rule) so a faintly glowing stone still clouds with age.
- A flame is a lathed veil and a core inside it, drawn blended and partly see-through so the torch head is seen through the fire (#280). Our own look, not the prototype's opaque teardrop. Each flame's vertices carry its colour and opacity by height (`flame_colour`, `flame_alpha` in `render/flame.rs`): the core a pale yellow-white at its base warming to yellow, the veil from yellow through orange to a deep red-orange tip, the veil 0.4 to 0.45 opaque over its lower half and the core 0.5 to 0.9 (a fainter veil read as a flat shape behind the head). The toon shader reddens a flame toward its silhouette in three steps (the surface's facing to the eye stands in for distance from the flame's axis) and premultiplies it by its alpha. Flames use `AlphaMode::Premultiplied` rather than `Blend` because `ToonExtension::specialize` makes `Blend` write depth for glass, which would hide the head behind its own flame. A torch's flame stands straight up to the head's top, so the burning head sits inside its bulb and inside a core 0.9 of the veil's width (`FLAME_CORE`; upright flames keep a 0.55 core), then leans forward along the flame frame's +Z, easing out of vertical to 55 degrees at its steepest and curling back to vertical at the tip (`spine_offset` in `armory::build::light`, part of the mesh so the generator stays a pure function of the seed). An earlier flame leaned from its base and left the head poking out of its side. At run time `flame.rs` turns it upright about its base and yaws it to lean the way the item faces, or, for a held item, mostly out toward its hand's side and a little back toward the delver (`FlameFacing`, `HELD_OUTWARD`): a lean along the look hides the flame behind the head or drops it below the torch on screen. Lanterns, candles and braziers keep an upright flame: it stands inside glass or a bowl, where a lean would clip.
- A legendary lying on the floor shimmers, the user's call ("a faint shimmer when they're on the ground"): a per-material `Shimmer` on `ToonExtension` (colour and strength, in the bindless uniform), drawn by `shimmer.wesl` on top of the lit colour. Its glow swells and fades every 3.6 s between 35% and full, at `legendary_shimmer` (0.25) times the legendary colour `#f29550` (`ui::rarity_color`), offset in time by where the item lies so no two pulse together; and glints drift through the item (a 12 cm lattice moving through item space, half its cells holding a glint that flares for 0.84 s of every 2.8 s), drawn as hard-edged discs with a near-white core to keep the toon steps crisp. It is self-light only, so it reads in an unlit corner but lights nothing round it; the plate likewise gave legendary metal a faint emissive (`metal.emissive = '#3a2c10'`). Only floor items are drawn with it (`carry::floor_shimmer`), so it stops on pickup and comes back if the item is set down. The rates and sizes are our own.
- Fabric patterns (`pattern.wesl`) are painted on the base colour before wear, as in `DECAY_BODY`. Wrapped patterns (cloth, quilting, mail, scales, rivets) are laid out by `armory::fabric::Wrap` rather than the prototype's fixed planar or cylindrical projection: round a vertical axis through the part, with a whole even number of cells round so the pattern closes at the back; a part off to one side of the item (one sleeve) or a mirrored pair (two sleeves, two leg cloths) wraps each piece round its own long axis instead of round the wearer; and mail and scales take their count round each row from the girth there (a 16-band profile of the part), as mail is narrowed with decreases, so rings keep their size from a coif's neck to its shoulders. Cells finer than about half a pixel fade to the pattern's mean so mail does not shimmer across a room (our own addition). The prototype's quilting stitched channels one way; ours is a grid, to match the puffs.
- Field patterns (heraldic divisions and planks) lie over the part's uv, which runs 0..=1 across a shield's field with the fess point, where the charge sits, at 0.5, 0.5 (the prototype offset a fixed 5% of the height instead). The first tincture goes in dexter and in chief as blazon reads it; the prototype put the second in chief for per fess and per bend. Lozengy fields get their own id (20, free in the prototype's table) rather than reusing the cloth lozenge. Per saltire and barry are ours, as is the slight tone each plank takes so boards read apart from across a room.
- Glass (`MaterialKind::Glass`: bottles, vials, flasks, lantern chimneys) is alpha-blended at the prototype's `glassMat` opacity (0.32), thickening towards its silhouette (a cubic of `1 - |N·V|`, our own stand-in for Fresnel), and casts no shadow, so the drink inside shows lit through it. Bevy 0.20 offers two ways to see through a surface: `AlphaMode::Blend`, drawn in the sorted transparent pass after opaque things, and screen-space transmission (`StandardMaterial::specular_transmission`, [docs.rs](https://docs.rs/bevy/0.20.0/bevy/pbr/struct.StandardMaterial.html#structfield.specular_transmission)), which samples a copy of the opaque pass. Either way an inverted hull, which fills the whole silhouette behind the part, would show through as a dark fill. With blending the hull can be fixed: glass writes depth (`ToonExtension::specialize`; Bevy's blended pipelines leave it off) and draws front faces only, and its hull is drawn in the transparent pass with a small depth bias, which Bevy adds to the sort distance (`TransparentSortingInfo3d::sort_distance`), so it comes just after the glass and only the ring outside the glass passes the depth test. Transmission's copy of the opaque pass would already hold the hull, so blending it is.
- Etching (`etch.wesl`) goes on after the pattern and before wear, on the parts its target covers (`EtchTarget::covers`, the prototype's `etchFor`), projected three ways in item space and blended by the item-space normal as in `DECAY_BODY`, and rubbed out with wear the same way. Inlaid lines take the inlay colour; blackened lines are worked per material, our own split of the prototype's single `c * 0.3`: engraved metal and embroidery thread at 0.3, carved wood and bone at 0.45, tooled leather at 0.55. Embroidery adds slanted stitches along the thread (ours). Jewellery's etching scales by the same factor as its wear (8); the prototype's food factor of 2 is moot, since no food is etched.
- The level's rock (`game/src/level/`) gets no inverted hull: seen from inside a room its edges are creases, not silhouettes. Ink is drawn instead as zero-width ribbons along the floor and ceiling seams and the wall corners, whose two sides carry opposite push directions in their outline normals, so `OutlineMaterial` widens them to a line of set pixel width. Where a ribbon's far half runs into rock it is hidden; at a convex corner it draws the silhouette. Rock ink is near black (darker than the Armory's), so it never shows lighter than unlit stone.
- Rock relief is one smooth displacement field of world position applied to every floor, wall and ceiling vertex, so faces that meet stay closed, and the shading normal is taken from the field rather than the triangles, so coplanar walls shade alike across cell seams. Stone wears with decay type 9 at wear 0.5, its noise scaled to a pattern a few metres across.
- Levels of stone halls (`LevelStyle::StoneHalls`, even depths) draw dressed stone (`game/src/level/geometry/stone.rs`, no Bevy) in place of the rock's relief; level 1 and every odd depth draw exactly as before. Each surface is a flat shell on the cell planes (wall, floor and ceiling share their edge vertices, as the rock's do) with the stone laid over it: wall blocks stand 4 to 8 cm proud with a 2.8 cm chamfer, flagstones 1 to 2.6 cm proud, and the 3 mm joints between them show the shell as mortar, so no two faces ever share a plane. Wall joints are laid along whole runs of wall from a seed taken from the run, not cell by cell, so courses tile across cell and chunk seams (a chunk lays a block when it owns the block's middle) and stop 10 cm short of a wall they meet at an inside corner. Courses are one list per level (eight to the 3 m spring, then on up), also used for the doorway masonry. The ceiling is one height field: flat at 3 m over corridors, a parabolic barrel over each colonnaded hall, crossing barrels in square bays (groins) over other rooms, and it slopes down through a doorway's cell to the corridor, where the doorway's infill hides the join. The wall shell rises to the same field, so the seam is closed, and blocks near the vault are cut to the lowest ceiling over them. Columns (a plinth, a round base, drums with entasis, an echinus and an abacus, chipped and eroded at the foot) are a few shared meshes, one per height and variant, turned by quarters; the vault slopes across a column, so an impost block fills the wedge above the abacus. The prototype had no stone halls: every proportion here is our own. `FB_DEPTH=2` plays them, and `FB_DEBUG_CAMERA=hall|vault` looks down the first colonnaded hall or up at its ceiling.
- The inventory is the dark ledger, not the plate's parchment card over a scrim. The page covers the level, and letting go of an item on the page outside the ledger still sets it down there. Bevy blends the UI in linear light, so a wash over the near-black page reads stronger than the same opacity does in CSS, which blends in sRGB. To make up for it, the seal's own gold glow is 0.07 at its centre rather than the plate's 0.2, and its bands are shown at 70%. Bare stone underfoot shows its empty grid, where the plate showed a dashed box, so there is always a cell to drop on. The warm glow goes on the side of whichever hand holds a lit item, the off hand only when the main hand holds none, and is left out when neither does (`inventory::doll::light_side`). These are our own choices.
- The title screen (`game/src/title/`) stands on the user's concept art, `reference/concept/forever-below-title-card.jpg`, until there is a rendered scene to show instead. The card has its own lettering baked in, so `game/assets/ui/title-card.png` is cropped from it above the letters (1842 by 1036 from x 734, y 0 of the 2576 by 1808 card, about 16:9), keeping the sky, the mountains, the lake and the hillside, so the name never shows twice. The crop covers the screen without stretching: the image node draws only the centred part of the card with the screen's aspect (`title::cover_rect`).
- The title lettering is real text, in Almendra Bold, not a picture of the card's letters. Almendra Display and Almendra SC were the other candidates: Display's letters are drawn as hairline outlines, which leave nothing inside to stripe, and SC sets the lowercase as small capitals where the card has lowercase. The card's letters (diagonal-barred e, wedge serifs, heavy strokes) are Almendra's own shapes at a bold weight. The words are set as on the card, a tall F beside both lines, "orever" beside its top and "Below" tucked under, by a 2D camera drawing white text into an offscreen image. A `UiMaterial` (`title/lettering.wesl`) reads that image's coverage and paints each letter with gold bands, about eleven to the x-height and half gold, and a solid gold rim a twentieth of the x-height wide, both measured off the card. The darkened gaps between bands and the soft shadow behind the letters are our own, for the brighter sky the name sits on here. Bevy's text has no per-glyph fill, so drawing the text into an image is how a shader gets at it.
- The title's buttons are boxes of the ledger's page, ruled in tan, with cream words that turn gold under the mouse, and Settings opens the pause menu's own settings page (`menu::menu_page`) on a panel of the same page. Both are opaque: Bevy blends UI in linear light, where the pause menu's 96% page over the card's white sky comes out mid-grey (`#3d3c3c`), and the cream text on the opaque page is the ledger's tested 4.5:1 and more (`ui::tests::text_reads_on_the_page_at_wcag_aa`). These are our own design.
- The game opens on the title (`launch::AppState::Title`) unless `--seed` or a debug switch that looks at the level is given. The play plugins' per-frame systems are in `play::DelveSystems`, which runs only while delving, and what a delve spawns (the level, the player, the character) is made on `OnEnter(AppState::Delving)`, so New delve builds a level from a fresh seed. The music opens on Forever Below wherever the game opens; leaving the title cuts the track short with a fade over `MusicSettings::fade`, and the level's random music follows the usual silence, never starting with Forever Below again (`audio::MusicTrack::cut_short`).
- The pause menu (Esc, `game/src/menu.rs`) is the same dark page at 96% over the level, which stops while it is open, with the title in the display face and the buttons in the body face, muted until the mouse is over them. Its Controls page is built from the one `Keymap` (`game/src/keymap.rs`) the game reads its keys through, so it cannot drift from the real bindings. Its Settings page has sliders (a bar ruled in tan, filled in muted tan, with a cream knob; hit-tested by hand as the inventory's grids are, rather than through the unstyled `Slider` and `Checkbox` of `bevy_ui_widgets` that Bevy 0.20's default `ui` feature brings, which work through picking) and toggles for music and sound volume, mouse sensitivity, inverted mouse Y and fullscreen, applied the same frame and saved in `settings.json`. The plate has no menu; this is our own design.
- Creatures (`game/src/creatures.rs`) are drawn as items are, one part per material with its own toon material and ink hull, but each part sits under the bone entity that moves it, so a part's local space is its bone's and wear stays fixed to the skin as the bone turns. Only each bone's main surface (fur, a snake's skin, head, jaw, tail, paws) casts a shadow; eyes, whiskers, claws, teeth, markings and bellies lie inside that shadow anyway, and a snake has three parts to a segment, all drawn into each nearby light's six shadow faces. A creature more than 20 m from the player holds its pose, since changing every bone's transform re-propagates and re-extracts each of its parts. At seed 1207, `FB_FRAME_TIME` (debug build, vsync, frames 240 to 300) gave 16 to 19 ms in the rat pack's room and in the constrictor's room on a quiet machine, and up to 25 ms while other builds ran on it; at the start, where no creature stands, the median over four runs of nine samples was 25 ms with creatures and 27 ms without, so their cost is within the noise. Both choices are our own.

## Profiling

Frame and system timings come from [Tracy](https://github.com/wolfpld/tracy). The `tracy` feature turns on Bevy's `trace_tracy`, which is off in ordinary builds.

1. Build with the `profiling` profile (release speed, line tables and symbols kept) and the feature: `cargo build --profile profiling -p forever_below --no-default-features --features tracy`.
2. Install the Tracy profiler, version 0.14 (the release `tracy-client-sys` 0.30 in Bevy 0.20 embeds; the versions must match) and open it.
3. Run `target/profiling/forever_below` from the repo root (any view switch works) and press Connect in Tracy. Bevy's systems, schedules and render passes show as zones.

Do not profile a debug build: it is optimised differently and the timings mislead.

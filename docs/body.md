# Bodies and fitting

This is how the prototype builds and dresses bodies. The Rust version should go further: better hands, faces and cloth. The fitting rules under "Held gear" came from review and still hold. Read alongside `reference/forever-below-plate.html`. Folk parameters are in `data/races.json`. Units are 10 cm, so a human is about 17.2 units tall.

## The body (`bodyDims`)

- Height `h = race.h * hs` (hs is 0.95–1.05). Proportions scale with `s = h / 17.5`, and girth `ws = s^0.72 * race.girth * (0.88 + frame * 0.3)`.
- The torso is a spline of cross-sections (hip, waist, chest, shoulder, neck). Each section is a superellipse with exponent 0.77 (`tSurf`), giving a slightly boxy, carved look. The belly and bust bulge only at the front.
- Arms (`mkArm`) and legs (`mkLeg`) are tubes swept along Catmull-Rom splines through shoulder, elbow and wrist, or hip, knee and ankle, with radius profiles that taper. Each arm root starts inside the chest, so sleeve openings never show skin.
- The head (`headFn`) is an ellipsoid with a narrower jaw, a chin, and a flatter face. Each folk adds its own features: snout, pig nose, long ears, frill, tusks.
- Hair, brows and beards are offset surfaces of the head with lumpy noise. Long hair drapes over the shoulders and clears the clothing.

### In the Rust build (`crates/fb_body`)

`BodySpec::roll(folk, seed)` rolls the plate's body fields (`C.form` is `figure`, `C.hs` is `stature`); `Body::new(&spec)` measures it as `bodyDims` does, in body space (soles on y = 0, face +Z, the wearer's left +X). Departures, all our own:

- **One body for skin and clothes.** The body yields the Armory's forms sized to itself (`torso_form`, `lower_body_form`, `leg_form`, `hand_form`, `head_form`) and where each sits (`Placement`s), so the garments, trousers, boots, gloves and hoods already built on those forms fit it unchanged. `TorsoForm` gained `Bulge`s (belly, bust and seat) that `TorsoProfile::point` adds, so cloth drapes over a belly instead of through it.
- **Rounder trunk sections.** `TRUNK_EXPONENT` is 0.9, nearly an ellipse; the plate's 0.77 read as a carved block. Garments are built on the same form, so they round with it.
- **Body shape fields** (`BodySpec::muscle`, `slender`, `bust`, `bust_round`; `BodyShape` with `stature`, `frame` and `figure` is what the creator's sliders set). They are rolled after the plate's fields, so those still roll as they did. Slenderness scales the girth (`ws` by `1.12 - 0.24 * slender`) at the same height. Muscle broadens the shoulders, chest and neck, narrows the waist, and fills the deltoids, upper arms, forearms, thighs and calves. Weight (`frame`) adds a belly and fills the waist. The creator's height runs 0.9 to 1.1 of the folk's (`STATURE`); rolls stay within 0.95 to 1.05.
- **A chest, a seat and the small of the back.** The plate swells one shelf across the front past figure 0.55 and leaves the back flat. Here the chest is two domes either side of the breastbone (`Bulge::pair`, `Bulge::dome`): the breast muscles, more with muscle, and a bust over them. Each dome has a definite edge and a crease under it, a full slope from the collarbone down to its crest and a short underside that rounds as `bust_round` rises (`Bulge::upper`, `Bulge::power`, `Bulge::fall`), sitting a little lower when round. Flat, the domes are squarish plates with a crisp lower edge (`Bulge::square` 4), the pectorals; as the bust grows they round (`square` 2), widen and reach round toward the sides. Every bulge swells out from the body's axis rather than only deepening the section, so a breast rounds out sideways too. A first pass swelled a Gaussian there, which blended down into the belly and read as a gut, and narrow domes that only deepened the section read as cones. The seat is two lobes too (with a cleft, and rounding off above the crotch), the belly rounds off above the groin, and the small of the back draws in above the seat (a `Bulge` of negative depth, skin only), so the side view has an S. Cloth is built on the same bulges as cloth drapes (`Bulge::bridged`): drawn across the cleft, and hanging from a bust's crest before it falls back to the body, rather than tucking under it.
- **Limb radii** run through their knots on a smooth cubic (Catmull-Rom slopes) rather than the plate's piecewise-linear `lerp3`, with a deltoid cap over the shoulder and a forearm that fills out below the elbow (the plate's was a thin tube). The forearm flattens toward the wrist to the hand form's wrist (wide across the palm, thin through it), so arm and hand meet without a step. The thigh's top reaches the trunk's own widest point at the hips, whatever the figure, so wide hips flow into the thighs instead of standing out over them as a ledge, and it stays full a third of the way to the knee before tapering; its root inside the trunk stays narrow. The back of the thigh's top fills out under the seat, so the buttock rounds into the thigh in a fold. Under the hips the trunk tucks inside the thighs at the sides (0.62 of the hips' width) and keeps its depth behind for the seat. Legwear keeps out on the hips toward each point's own direction from the axis, so its back seam does not fold into the seat's cleft. Legs swell at the calf behind (more with muscle) and the knee in front.
- **Hands** are the glove builder's palm and digit tubes ([`armory::build::hand`]): four fingers and a thumb that curl joint by joint (relaxed, or closed round a grip with each joint bent by phalanx length over grip radius), the ball of the thumb as a pad, and nails. Their length is the plate's `handL`; the knuckles widen with the wrist, so dwarf hands are broad and goblin hands slender. The plate's hand was an ellipsoid and a thumb.
- **Feet** are the boot builder's `LegForm` foot, sized from the plate's `footFn` (length `0.148 h * race.foot`), turned out 0.12 rad. Pigfolk keep the plate's darker trotters; lizardfolk get three bone claws per foot.
- **Heads** (`Body::head_mesh`, `Body::face`) are the plate's `headFn` on `HeadForm`, with a little relief added to the skin only (hoods and helmets still fit the plain form): a brow ridge, eye sockets the eyes sit in, cheekbones, and a muzzle and chin so the profile is more than a flat face and a nose. Human eyes have an upper lid, so they read at doll size and hold an expression close up. Eye whites, irises, pupils and the mouth carry no material (a tint only), so the character's dirt never clouds them. Ears are cupped sheets with a rolled rim instead of ellipsoids and extruded blades. A lizard's snout has a jaw line with a row of teeth, and its frill is spines joined by a membrane (drawn with the head whatever its hair, in its hair colour). The pig's snout disc is deeper than the plate's, and its flop ears are broader and fold further. Ear size, eye spacing and mouth width get small seeded nudges on top of the spec's jaw, brow and nose.
- **Hair, brows and beards** (`Body::hair_mesh(HairFit)`) follow the plate's `buildHair`, brows and `buildBeard`, with a little thickness on every sheet and seeded value noise over the plate's ripples, so two heads of one style differ. Long hair hangs from behind the ears only (the plate started it beside the face, which wrapped a mantle over the chest) and is pushed clear of the trunk and the upper arms, outward along the ray from the body's axis, by `HairFit::clearance` plus a margin; a ponytail rests on the back at the same clearance. A beard's sides by the ears stay short and it narrows as it hangs, so it falls from the chin and jaw instead of over the shoulders, and it rests on the chest: pushed straight forward until it clears what is worn on the front of the chest (`HairFit::chest`), not out along rays from the axis by the thickness worn anywhere round the shoulders, which fanned a long beard out across a cloak like a shelf. Dressed, both lie on the outermost worn layer itself (`HairFit::rest`, a `Rest`: how far the worn surface reaches from the body's axis by height and direction over the chest, shoulders and neck, measured by `fit_worn` when the hair goes on). A beard falls from the chin and lies on what is on the chest, straight forward or back, drawing back toward it at no more than 1.5 per unit of drop (`HANG_TUCK`), and along the jaw it lies on a collar or cowl; only within 55 degrees of straight ahead, so by the ears it keeps its own line (pushed out by mail and pauldrons round the shoulders, a cleric's beard fanned across them like a flat plate). What is worn on the head (an aventail, a hood's cape) is left out of the surface: it hangs over hair and beard, not under them. A ponytail rests on the back as worn. Each strand of long hair falls until it meets the surface and is moved onto it along the surface's normal: out where the surface is steep, up where it is a shelf (a cloak over the shoulders), and between the two as it tilts. A strand that was lifted slides back off the shelf along the slope (down it, and back) in proportion to how much it was lifted rather than being pushed out to its edge, and below the shoulders it is drawn back onto the back. Choosing between pushing out and lifting outright was a step in the hair's position, so two neighbouring strands could land either side of a cloak's shoulder and the sheet between them cut through it. The surface is drawn from the worn triangles themselves into the rest grid (256 directions by 96 heights; each cell the farthest any triangle reaches across it, then the most of the cells about it, so the grid never falls inside a surface that bulges between its nodes; a row beside a cell counts only as far as it reaches less one unit of reach per unit of height, `DILATE_SLOPE`, so a surface running gently between rows is not widened by the slope times a row's height, which held hair 2 cm off a sloping shoulder), with an edge standing well clear of the surface's own run (a cloak's front edge, a hood lying down its back) holding hair off for a few degrees beside it (the surface's own run is the slope across the column from both sides, since a cell held up by the row beside it reads as flat from one side and made the next column look like an edge); and it is spread a little upward over anything standing out, so hair parts round a cowl above it. Taking the surface's vertices at their crests several degrees either side instead held the edges of a curtain of hair up to 2 cm off a cloak where it curves round the shoulders; long hair now lies within 1.2 cm of a cloak all round. Long hair has 72 columns across the back (36 before) and 36 rows down its length (18 before that), so the sheet between two strands follows a cloak's curve over the shoulders instead of cutting across a rim between them. Our own; `HairFit::clearance` and `HairFit::chest` remain for bare bodies. `HairFit::covered` (under a helmet or hat) drops the cap, ridge and topknot, as the plate does; `Body::hair_volume` is the plate's `hairVol` for sizing a helmet over the hair. Hair is `MaterialKind::Hair`, which dirties as skin does.
- **Skin** is its own material (`MaterialKind::Skin`, decay type 13): it wears with the character's dirt, a dull film and smudges pooling low on the body. Lizardfolk skin carries the Armory's `Scales` pattern.

## Worn gear

Clothing is not modelled per item. Each garment is an **offset of the body surface** (`garmentGeo`), shaped by the Armory state: style, length, fit, flare, folds, sleeves, neck, trim and pattern. That lets one Armory tunic fit a gnome and a pigfolk. Layers stack outward with a running `maxOff` (shirt, legs, armour, neck, cloak, belt, then back). Boots extend the foot surface; gloves and gauntlets wrap the hand and cuff. Helmets are the Armory helm itself, resized so its radius matches the head. Amulets use the Armory pendant, with the chain drawn on the body surface from the back of the neck down to the pendant. Bags are the Armory bag on the back, with straps pushed onto the worn surface (`pushOut`, `strapCurve`).

### In the Rust build (`fb_body::worn`)

`fit_worn(&body, &[(Slot, Item)])` returns a `WornFit`: each item fitted in body space (`layers`), the `HairFit` the hair should take under it, and which bare skin it hides (`Covered`); `Body::dressed` puts skin, head, hair and gear together. Each item keeps its own Armory shape, built by the Armory's own builders on the body's forms, so one tunic fits a gnome and a pigfolk. Departures from the plate, all our own unless noted:

- **Order.** Slots go on legs, feet, shirt, body, hands, belt, neck, head, cloak, back. Trousers go under the shirt (the plate's offsets put the trousers' seat inside the shirt as well); the belt goes under the amulet and the cloak, so a cloak hangs over a belt instead of being strapped down by it; and a hood's cape goes under the cloak, which hangs over it as over armour. Long hair hangs under a hood and its cape, and otherwise over everything but a pack.
- **Measured layering instead of `maxOff`.** After each item the fitting measures how far its mesh stands out past the bare trunk (gridded by height and direction), past each arm and past each lower leg. The next item is built on the torso form widened just enough that, once its own fit eases it, its surface clears every measured point: the nine sections are fitted to the measurements, each pass widening the two sections either side of a point still inside. Sleeves and glove cuffs are eased over what is on the arm, boot shafts over the trousers. Whole parts are sorted onto the trunk or the arms (a skirt hanging by the hands stays on the trunk). A belt's pouch and frogs, and footwear, are not measured: they stand out on one side only, which nine sections round the trunk cannot follow.
- **Cloth pressed under cloth.** Under a closed garment a skirt hangs straight and its folds lie flat, and under a cloak it hangs straight; sleeves under sleeves or gloves do not bell; a garment's own belt gives way to a worn one; a cowl gives way to a hood on the head (the plate draws no hood roll then); trousers tuck into boots with a shaft. The Armory flares a skirt as a share of the form it hangs from, so a cloak over arms keeps the flare it would have on the body alone.
- **Cloaks and hood capes** hang over the arms and whatever is on them (sleeves, gloves, pauldrons), and their shoulder section moves up to the top of the arms, so they rest on the shoulders and still close round the neck. They are measured over the upper arms always, and over the forearms and hands only while those hang down by the body: a forearm raised to hold a staff or a torch comes out through the cloak's front, as from a real cloak, instead of pushing the cloak out in front of it. While a forearm is raised, only what lies on the shoulder's side of the elbow counts: a wide sleeve sags below a bent elbow, and a mantle hung over the sag stood out from the arm in a corner. **The shoulder is found by its reach, not its height.** The section's top is the highest thing standing out at the shoulder's own width (past halfway from the shoulder section's width to the shoulder top's), so a yoke, a sleeve cap or a pauldron counts and a collar round the neck does not. It was cut off at the neck's height, which left whatever rose above that showing over the cloth, and widened the top section to meet the arms below it: a flat shelf with a corner at each shoulder, like a tent. The neck section now rises with it (a quarter of the wearer's scale above the shoulder top at least), so the cloth domes over the shoulders and falls from them. A hood's lower rim leaves the head higher up where its cape stands that high (a small head over pauldrons), so its neck rises from the cape to the rim instead of folding back on itself, and a cowl dips low over the chest only (the fourth power of the cosine of the angle from straight ahead, not the square), so it stays up over the front of the shoulders.
- **A cloak drapes from the shoulders.** It is built on the bare trunk's form and hangs over everything else through an underlay (`garment_parts_fitted` with `Fitting::over`, `DrapedSurface::over`): a fine grid of how far the bare arms, the hands and every worn surface reach (the forearms, hands and what is on them only while they hang under it) from the body's axis, by height and angle. In each horizontal slice the cloth takes the convex hull of what it rests on, so it bridges from the back to the arms rather than dipping between them, with its fold valleys kept clear; down each meridian it falls past the arms and draws back toward the legs below them at `UNDERLAY_TUCK` (0.15 per unit of drop). Its flare does not spread it out from what it hangs over (the fullness is in its folds), and a skirt under a cloak loses its flare as under any other outer garment. The plate widens its cloak past a fixed arm line; the first Rust fit eased sections all round over the arms, which stood out like a tent over bell sleeves.
- **Sleeves and pauldrons follow the body's arms** (`garment_parts_on_arms`, `SleeveForm::on_arm`): short sleeves end halfway down the upper arm, long ones at the wrist (the plate's `saGarment` shares); pauldrons sit over the shoulder joint down the upper arm, as the plate's do. Lengths in an item's state scale with the wearer, and a hem stays clear of the ankles (the plate's `yBot`). A robe is cut to its wearer instead: its hem hangs 0.15 of the wearer's scale above the ankle (`ROBE_CLEARANCE`, 1.5 cm on a human), just clear of the feet, on every folk. Scaled from the Armory's length, the longest rolled robe ended at mid-shin on a human, and none could reach below 19 cm off the ground.
- **Boots and gloves** are built on the body's foot and hand (its toe and width from the boot, the hand in its pose, curled per finger by `hand_parts_curled`) and replace the bare feet and hands; bracers leave the hands bare. Boot shafts stop at the knee, glove cuffs at most of the forearm. A shaft is eased over the trousers under it by `LegForm::ease`, measured from the knee to mid-shin and from there to the ankle, and added after the calf's swell. The first fit added the most the trousers stood out anywhere on the lower leg to the shin before the calf multiplied it (by up to 1.45), so a shaft stood up to twice the trousers' reach off the leg and ballooned. The calf now swells where the leg's own calf is (0.72 of the knee's height), not at 0.55 of the shaft's height. A cuff is drawn close over what is worn on the forearm under it (`hand_parts_fitted` with a `CuffUnderlay`): the sleeve is measured in the hand's own space, by how far below the wrist and by angle round the forearm, and the cuff's inner face follows it 2 mm off all along, so a bracer lies on a mail sleeve instead of standing off it by the most the sleeve stands out anywhere under it. A plate cuff keeps its flare over a sleeve; cloth, leather and mail are drawn in. Trousers, breeches and leggings are built along the body's own legs (`LowerBodyForm::sections`, `Body::leg_sections`): sixteen horizontal sections of the leg as drawn, from the ankle to the crotch, give the cloth the leg's line forward at the knee, the calf behind and the knee in front, where the Armory's own legs are straight tubes off the boot form. On a body the seat starts where the trunk's skin rounds under between the thighs, the cloth keeps out on the hips' own outline as it blends from leg to seat, and the hips are eased 5 mm wider at the sides (our own allowance), so no skin shows through even close leggings. Trousers and leggings still hide the bare legs, which they now wholly cover.
- **Helms** are resized with `HelmState::sized` to `1.12 * head half width + hair volume` (the plate's `saHelm`), every length of bowl, brim, cheeks, aventail, comb and crest with it; a head much deeper than it is wide (a lizard's) is sized by `0.96 * half depth` instead, so the back of its skull stays inside. **Hoods and hats** go on the head form grown by the covered hair's volume.
- **Features under headgear** (`WornFit::head`, a `HeadFit`; `Body::head_mesh_fitted`, `Body::under`). Under a hood the ears fold back flat along the side of the head, and a pig's flop ears fold under anything on the head; a lizard's frill folds into a low ridge along the crown under anything. The headgear is grown by the folded features' thickness (`Body::feature_volume`) as it is by hair. Standing ears (a goblin's or a gnome's) come out through a helm (`Body::standing_features`, `Openings`): `Helm::build_with_openings` cuts a hole in the shell where they cross it, or a notch up from the rim when they cross near it, and breaks the brow and eye bands and drops the rivets there. Every other part of the helm gives way too: a brim, an aventail or a visor gets a hole where a feature crosses it, a slot cut in from the nearer edge when the crossing reaches one, or a gap right across it when it reaches both (an aventail opens back past an ear at its front edge); cheek plates move forward of an ear, up to 0.4 of the bowl's radius from the front, and are trimmed if that is not enough. A lizard's face (its snout, jaw and teeth, its eyes and the ridges over them) lifts the rim instead (`Openings::lift`): the shell is notched up from the rim over it however high it crosses, so a sallet, bascinet or great helm opens over the snout, a barbute's T opens out to take it, and a hounskull's visor is notched; a nasal, a comb, spangen bands, a great helm's cross and any slits or breaths in the way stop short of the notch. The crossings are found by sampling each part's own surface and taking the convex hull of where a feature comes within reach of it, grown by the margin. The plate draws ears and frills through its headgear.
- **Tails through clothes.** Where a lizard's tail (`Body::tail`) comes within reach of a garment's skirt (a cloak, a robe, a tunic, a hauberk), the garment is rebuilt with a vent cut up from its hem at the back (`Notch`, `Drape::hem_at`), arched at its top (a half ellipse; the first vents were pointed, which needed twice the width at the hem) and wide enough at every height the tail's surface, all round it, passes through; the hem trim follows the vent's edges. Trousers, breeches and leggings get a hole in the seat round the tail (`LegHole`, `legwear_parts_holed`): each leg's cloth is built as the cloth below the hole and the cloth above it, meeting either side, round the smallest ellipse that takes in where the cloth comes within reach of the tail. A pig's curled tail tucks in under anything over its seat (`Covered::tail`). All our own: the plate's tails pass through its clothes.
- **A skirt flares by how far it hangs** (our own): the Armory's flare is a share of the radius at the hem, which belled a tunic ending just below the hips out like a peplum; it now takes its whole flare only over 40 cm of drop below the hips (`FULL_FLARE_DROP`), and a quarter at least.
- **Belts over a robe** leave their pouch off (our own): it hung where the robe's skirt falls.
- **Amulets** take the pendant without its chain (`pendant_parts`) and lay the necklace (`necklace_parts`) round the base of the neck and down to the pendant on whatever is worn there, the pendant drawn a little over true size as the plate's is.
- **Bags** lose their own carrying straps (the `carry` part) for straps laid onto the worn surface, drawn in as well as pushed out so they lie on what they cross, and rest against the back as it actually is under them.

Known limits: in mid-stride the leading shin still shows a little through the front of a robe that hangs to the feet, where the cloth between the legs follows both of them alike; a kettle hat's brim over a lizard's snout loses most of its front, since the brim lies along the top of the snout; the bottom of a great helm or barbute can still meet a lizard's shoulders; a vent for a lizard's thick tail root can reach up to the belt of a robe and the small of the back of a cloak; and for some folk and seeds outside the sweep (goblins and pigfolk especially) the outer strands of a long curtain of hair can still stand up to 2 cm off a cloak over the shoulders.

## Posing: the rig, skin weights and the gait (`fb_body::rig`, `skin`, `gait`)

The prototype's bodies were static. Folk now have a skeleton and walk. All of it is plain Rust, deterministic (a pure function of the body, so the same seed gives the same rig, the same weights and the same pose), and is our own: the prototype had none.

- **Rig** (`Rig::of(&body)`). Sixteen joints taken from the body's own limb joints: pelvis (the hips' centre), spine (the waist), chest, head (the base of the neck, where the head already turned), and for each side shoulder, elbow, wrist, hip, knee and ankle. Each rests with an identity rotation, so a joint's frame is body space moved to its origin: a rotation about +X swings a limb in the vertical plane (negative swings a hanging limb forward), about +Y turns, about +Z rolls. A `RigPose` is a local rotation and an offset per joint (the pelvis's carries the bob); `Rig::skin_frames` gives each joint's move from rest, which is what the renderer's inverse bind poses undo.
- **Skin weights** (`Skinner`, `PartSkin`). Every vertex of the bare skin and of every worn layer gets up to four joints and weights, by where it rests and the region it is in (`SkinRegion`), never by its mesh, so gear fitted a little off the skin gets almost the weights of the skin under it and stays on it however the rig is posed. Along a limb the weights blend smoothly over each joint (trunk into thigh or upper arm over the hip or shoulder, then shin or forearm over the knee or elbow, then foot over the ankle; a hand is stiff and follows the forearm). A vertex follows a limb as closely as it hugs it, its distance to the limb's centre line over the limb's radius. Across the body the trunk's weights follow height (pelvis, spine, chest) and the neck gives way to the head. Cloth that hangs loose (a cloak, a long coat's skirt, a robe, baggy trousers) follows the pelvis and chest and takes a share of the legs that grows toward the hem, to `HEM_SHARE` (0.75; it was 0.4 while skirts ended at the knee) at the ankles, and splits across the midline, so it neither tears between the legs nor stays still as they pass. Sleeves, gloves, boots and trousers are weighted by the slot they are worn in; a hood and the head inside it ride the chest, as they turn with the body rather than the neck. What a hand holds has no weights: it rides the wrist joint rigidly (`Joint::wrist`), a lantern at the hip the pelvis. Weights are capped at four joints per vertex; a joint dropped gives its weight to its nearest kept ancestor.
- **Gait** (`Gait::new(&body, &rig)`). `walk(phase, speed)` is the walk cycle, `idle(cycle, t)` the idle, `pose(phase, speed, cycle, t)` the two blended by speed. The phase runs 0 to 1 over two steps and is advanced by distance (`phase += distance / stride_length()`), so the stance foot is planted on the floor and held flat, moving back through the body's frame at exactly the body's speed: it does not slide. The swing foot lifts and carries through, leaving and meeting the floor at the stance's own speed. Legs are solved by two-bone IK to those foot targets with the knee forward, so the bob, the knee bend and the reach follow from the leg's own length: a step is 0.7 of the leg's length, so a dwarf's short legs take short steps and a goblin's long ones long, and the natural pace (`natural_speed`) is 1.8 steps a second. The pelvis turns, rolls and sways over the stance leg, the spine counter-twists, the head stays level, and the arms swing opposite the legs: an arm that holds something swings about a third as much, and with both hands full neither swings, so what is in them stays in step with the chest. The idle replaces the whole-figure sway the folk had before they had a skeleton: the chest rises and falls with the breath (15 a minute, each person's own rate and phase from `IdleCycle::of(seed)`), and about every ten seconds the pelvis shifts across to one leg and the other knee relaxes, feet planted.
- **No two-handed grips yet.** `fit_hands` fits each hand's own item, so no hold has both hands on one thing. When both are full, neither arm swings; when a two-handed hold comes, both wrists will already move as one with the chest.
- **Weights and the seam between the legs.** Baggy legwear (the fighter's trousers and puttees on seed 1207) has surface that runs across the midline between the thighs. Two legs going opposite ways cannot both hold it, so there a few edges stretch (the specs allow the worst edge of a walk 25 times its rest length, and three in four edges under 1.6 times). Everywhere else gear stays on the limb it covers (`skin_tests`).

### Why folk are skinned and creatures are not

The creatures (`fb_creatures::rig`, `game/src/creatures`) are a rigid bone entity per body part, with no skinning, and that suits a rat: it is a few lumps that overlap at the joints. A folk has knees, elbows and clothes. A rigid shin and thigh open a wedge-shaped gap at the knee as it bends, an arm and its sleeve gape at the elbow, and a cloak cannot follow a stride at all. So folk are drawn as Bevy skinned meshes (`SkinnedMesh`, `ATTRIBUTE_JOINT_INDEX` and `ATTRIBUTE_JOINT_WEIGHT`, one entity per joint and `SkinnedMeshInverseBindposes` of the joints' rest places) in `game/src/folk_pose.rs`, and posing costs only the sixteen joints' transforms a frame; the meshes are built once. See `docs/rendering.md` for what skinning needed of the toon and ink shaders.

## Held gear (`saHeld`, `fitGrip`)

These rules came from review and are deliberate:

- **Grips fit the hand.** The effective grip radius (shaft radius × grip thickness × wrap) is capped at `clamp(arm_radius * 0.27, 0.07, GRIP_MAX = 0.13)`, about a torch handle. Blades and heads keep their size.
- **One-handed weapons** (sword, dagger, mace, club, hand axe) stand perpendicular to the forearm, through the fist. The weapon arm bends at the elbow.
- **Swords and daggers** turn `BLADE_TURN = 55°` about the grip so an edge leads, and draw at `BLADE_SCALE = 0.78 × sqrt(s)`.
- **Hand axes** turn 90° about the haft so the bit faces forward.
- **Bows** close the fist on the riser (the bow's origin), stand upright with the string back toward the archer, and are capped at `BOW_MAX = 0.62` of the archer's height.
- **Shields** strap at the fist with the forearm along the back, held clear of the hand, and are capped at `SHIELD_MAX = 0.42` of the bearer's height.
- Staves and spears stand upright in the hand.

All constants are in `data/constants.json`.

### In the Rust build (`fb_body::held`)

`fit_held(&body, side, &item)` returns a `HeldFit`: the `Pose` with that arm set and its fingers closed on the grip (`ArmPose::holding`), the item's state as drawn, its mesh in body space, and the scale and `Placement` it is drawn at (`HeldFit::to_body` maps an item-space anchor, such as a torch's light, onto the body). Draw the body with `Body::posed(&spec, fit.pose)` so hand and item meet. Every rule above has a test with a rolled Armory item. Departures, all our own unless the plate is named:

- **The grip is measured, then thinned in the state.** The radius checked against `Body::grip_radius` is measured on the built item: the farthest any handle part (grip, wrap and its bindings, bare shaft, bands, a torch's thong, a bow's riser, a crossbow's stock) stands from the line through the fist, across the fist's width. When it is too thick, the state's handle is thinned and the Armory rebuilds the item, as the plate's `fitGrip` thinned `shaft.radius` (the wrap's cord, pommel and bands by `sqrt(k)`, as there). Scaling the built mesh round the grip would have squeezed guards, langets and head necks that overlap it, and scaling the whole item would have shrunk the blade or head too. Where a head sits on the shaft (the top, or an axe's eye) the shaft is tapered instead of thinned evenly, so the head still fits its neck; the flare stops at a taper of 2.5, past which a haft reads as a club's swelling. A club's swelling widens to keep its girth. The plate thinned only swords, daggers, maces, staves and spears; here clubs, axes, torches, bows and crossbows fit too.
- **Hafts longer than 0.45 of the bearer's height** (a long axe, a long-hafted mace or club) are held upright like a staff, not as one-handed weapons. The plate had only one-handed clubs and hand axes.
- **Upright holds are solved.** The grip channel runs across the fist, perpendicular to the forearm, so a staff, spear, bow or torch stands upright only when the forearm is level. The forearm's bend is searched for that, instead of the plate placing the item upright whatever the hand did. An upright haft is held no higher than keeps its butt cap 0.15 off the ground (the plate's `a.hand.y - .15`).
- **Torches stand upright** in a level fist, the flame at shoulder height, carried beside the body: the arm spreads out 0.5 rad (`TORCH_SPREAD`), and further, up to 0.8, until the flame clears the head's outline from the front by 0.15; where spreading only lifts the flame along the head's side (past a goblin's long ears) the torch leans out from the fist instead, up to 0.4 rad. At the plate's 0.14 the flame burned across a gnome's face in the doll and its portrait. The plate leaned torches forward from a lower hand.
- **Lanterns hang plumb** below the fist, which closes round the carrying ring's wire; the arm is spread a little wider than the plate's off hand (0.32) so the lantern hangs clear of the leg.
- **Crossbows** are carried by the waist of the stock, 0.6 of the way from the butt, deck up.
- **Shields** stand upright: the face is level and perpendicular to the forearm, so it faces out from the body, where the plate turned it out and forward and so leaned the board whenever the forearm sloped. The shield arm is raised toward level (bend 0.85 for the plate's 0.5). The board's back clears the measured hand and forearm by 0.07 (the plate's clearance), and boss grips are still held by the strap rule.
- **Both hands** are fitted together by `fit_hands(&spec, main, off)`: the main hand is the right (the plate draws `C.items.mainhand` on arm `'R'`), the off hand is fitted on the body already posed for the main hand, and `Hands::body` is posed for both, so `Body::dressed` and `fit_worn` on it lay sleeves along the arms that hold things. Any held item fits either hand: a weapon in the off hand is fitted to the left fist as the main hand fits it to the right, mirrored, and the view model mirrors its hold to the left (`view_hold`).
- **A lantern at the hip** (`fit_hip`, our own; the plate drew none) hangs plumb from the worn belt on the off hand's side, 0.6 rad round the waist toward the front so it clears an arm hanging at the side, its carrying ring over the belt's band (the belt runs through the ring as the fingers do in a fist) and the top of the ring at the middle of the band. Its body stands out far enough to clear the hip ring's ellipse plus 0.12 below the belt, where trousers and a tunic's skirt lie. In first person the same fit is moved so the body's eye is the player's, and hung from the player's body.
- **Bows** keep the Armory's handedness (the arrow rests on the item's -Z side), so a bow in the left hand carries its arrow on the right of the riser. Placements never mirror an item.

### In first person (`game/src/view_model.rs`)

The level's view model draws each hand's item as rolled, in its own proportions, so it looks as its inventory icon and the floor show it, but 1.4 times its size about its grip (`HELD_SCALE`, the user's call), since at true size it read too small. Only the doll's turn in the fist is kept (`fit_hands`: the blade's 55° turn, an axe's bit forward, a bow's string back, a shield strapped along the forearm). The doll's grip thinning and its `BLADE_SCALE` and bow and shield caps are not applied: they fit the item to a drawn fist, and no fist is drawn in first person, so there they only made handles skinny and blades small (most of all on gnomes and goblins, whose grip is at the 0.07 floor against a human's 0.11 to 0.13, and whose blades the doll draws at about 0.6 of their size). The doll still thins and scales, as its own rule above. The item, turned as in the doll's fist, is taken from body space into the view by half a turn about the vertical (body +Z ahead becomes the view's -Z, the wearer's left stays on the left), the point of it the doll's fist closes on moved to where that kind of hold sits in the view and given a further turn there (`view_hold`); the off hand's is the main hand's mirrored. Thinning narrows a handle about its axis, so that point is the same on the rolled item as on the doll's thinned one. Fist positions are the same for every folk, and items are drawn at their own size, so a sword looks the same in a gnome's view as in a human's; only where the doll's hand closes on a long haft (higher up a staff for a short bearer) differs. Moving a small delver's fists nearer the eye, as their shorter arms would, made a torch loom over a gnome's view. The view model camera also sees through a fixed 46-degree vertical field of view, narrower than the world's 70 (it was 60), as Bevy's `first_person_view_model` example and Unreal Engine's First Person Field of View do (https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/first-person-rendering): with the scale, held items are drawn about 1.9 times as tall as before, for every folk alike. The held light's stage light keeps its offset from the flame, scaled with the item. The holds were moved for it (our own tuning against captures): fists sit further below the bottom edge so long blades still end inside the view, and every item stays clear of the eye and the crosshair. Departures from the doll, all our own tuning against captures:

- **One-handed weapons** stand low on the right like the carried torch, spun 0.7 rad about their length so a blade shows some of its flat and an axe its bit rather than an edge-on line, and tipped a little away from the eye.
- **Staves, spears and long hafts** lie low across the view, rising toward the other side, where the doll stands them upright.
- **Bows** are canted in and turned 0.7 rad so the limbs' curve shows; **crossbows** are turned from the doll's stock-up carry to point ahead.
- **Shields** keep the doll's board, its face out toward the edge of the view, so its back is seen low in the corner.
- **Torches** keep the view model's old lean in and away; **lanterns** hang plumb as on the doll.
- Nothing held comes nearer the eye than 3 units or into a circle of 0.3 of the half height about the crosshair (`NEAREST`, `CLEAR_CENTRE`), for every folk.
- No hands are drawn yet. The grips sit just below the bottom edge, so the missing fist reads as out of frame. Drawing them is a small step, though a drawn fist would want the doll's thinned grip back, or a fist sized to the item's own handle: `Hands::body` is already posed with each hand closed round its grip, `hand_meshes` builds the hand in that pose and `Body::hand_placement` places it in body space, so the same body-to-view move as the item puts it round the grip; gloves would come from `fit_worn`'s hands layer on the same posed body.

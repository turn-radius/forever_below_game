# Forever Below: design

A copy of the seed design doc from the "Procedural Ultima" project, as of the handoff.

## Premise
A first-person, stylized-3D dungeon crawler in the spirit of Ultima Underworld. You come through a door into a Stygian abyss and it closes behind you. There is only one way out, and the abyss is infinitely deep. The way out is a rumor the world keeps feeding you, told differently by every culture.

## Core loop
Delve until you find a level worth inhabiting. Gain favor with its factions and make it your base for a time. Faction quests lead to rare items. You can keep a quest's reward instead of completing it; the quest expires and your standing drops. Then the abyss pulls you deeper.

## Systems
- Every character, you and NPCs alike, has skills, stats and an inventory, and is simulated: hunger, thirst, sleep, warmth, light, dirt, sickness, disease, limb health, pain, breath, encumbrance.
- Items persist in the world and carry history. Legendaries from earlier runs come back as found objects.
- Item stats are Diablo-style: rarity, plus prefixes and suffixes that can raise or lower any parameter.
- Lockpicking, sneaking, and breaking doors and containers by material and stats.
- Bartering depends on stats, skills, faction, personal relationship, scarcity, alignment and appearance.
- Quests are generated from NPC needs, alignments and goals.
- Folk: goblins, gnomes, humans, lizardfolk, dwarves, pigfolk. Enemies: slime cubes, skeletons, beholders, demons, ghouls, rats, snakes, bats, scorpions, spiders, giant hornets, worms, dragons, vampires, werewolves, tentacle monsters, liches, devils, ghosts.
- D&D coinage. No mining: resources come from what is in the abyss.
- Classes: Fighter, Ranger, Mage, Cleric. Freshly fallen and Scavenger are starting looks.

## Visual direction
- Spline Armory's renderer is the style guide (see `rendering.md`).
- Ultima Underworld's portrait-and-doll lineup is the character reference, at higher fidelity (`reference/concept/`).
- The inventory follows the parchment concept, restyled as a dark ledger so it does not glare over the abyss: engraved item icons in footprint grids, an underfoot drop zone, prepared outfits on invisible mannequins, and the doll inside a runic seal with slot boxes around it.

## Folk in the level (since the handoff)

Our own proposal; neither the seed doc nor the plate places anyone in a level.

- Each level holds three to ten folk (`fb_level::FolkSpot`), in groups of two to four, one faction to a room. A group stands round the room's brazier if it has one, otherwise round a point inside it, facing in as if talking. Nobody stands in the starting room, in a corridor, in a doorway or beside one, or on the loot.
- The factions are `data/factions.json`. Each leans toward a folk and a pair of outfits (`fb_level::FACTIONS`): the Lantern-keepers human clerics and mages, the Mudfoot clan goblin scavengers and fighters, the Brothers of the Last Door human fighters and clerics, the Scale Court lizardfolk fighters and rangers, the Hogsmeet traders pigfolk rangers and scavengers, the Gnome Guild of Gears gnome mages and scavengers, the Ash Choir dwarf clerics and mages. Three in four members are the faction's folk; the rest are any folk.
- Folk come from a third stream of the level seed, after the layout and the loot, so they never move a wall or an item.
- In the game (`game/src/folk.rs`) each spot is a dressed `fb_body` figure. Who they are is a pure function of the spot: a body from its folk and seed, a name from `data/syl.json`, and one of the faction's outfits rolled through `fb_items::outfit` (`data/outfits.json`, the plate's `preparedSet`, which also gives a bare-headed gnome a pointed hat). Anyone the outfit leaves empty-handed carries a torch. They are dressed by the same `game/src/figure.rs` as the doll (`fit_hands`, then `fit_worn`), with the head, hair and headgear split off to turn about the neck. A lit torch or lantern in hand lights the room.
- Standing, folk breathe (about 15 breaths a minute, each a little faster or slower) and now and then shift their weight. Without a skeleton the whole figure moves a little about its feet. Within 4 m they turn their head toward the player, up to 70 degrees either side, about the neck; under a hood, whose cape lies on the shoulders, the body turns a little instead. Each stands in a circle the player cannot walk into (`play::Obstacles`, `player::slide_around`).
- Looking at someone within reach (2 m, as for picking things up) shows a card under the crosshair in the item card's style: their name, folk and faction ("Dwarf of the Ash Choir"), and what they wear and hold, each item's name in its rarity's colour. The item card wins when both are in view.
- Folk can be struck (`game/src/folk_combat.rs`). A folk's abilities are their folk's scores in `data/races.json` and their class is their outfit's; hit points are a 1st-level character's as the delver's are (the class's hit die at its highest plus the Constitution modifier, SRD 5.1 "Hit Points at 1st Level"), and a scavenger or someone freshly fallen, who has no class, has a commoner's 1d8 at its average, 4 (SRD 5.1 "Commoner"), plus the modifier. Armor Class is the delver's sum over everything they wear and hold. A swing finds a folk at the circle they stand in and rolls to hit first, as every attack does (see "Attack rolls" under Creatures). A blow that lands flashes them, knocks them back as a Medium creature and thuds; at 0 they fall onto their back about their feet, the standing figure turned as a whole (our own simplification, no ragdoll), and "Name is slain" shows. The body stops its routine, turns its head no more, blocks nobody and shows no card.
- Folk fight back once given cause (`game/src/hostility.rs`, our own rules). Every folk starts friendly (`Hostility`), and nothing a friendly folk does hurts the delver, however near they come. Cause is a `Provoked` message any system may send: a swing aimed at a folk sends one whether or not it lands (`combat::swing::SwungAt`), and catching the delver stealing or a talk gone wrong can send one later. A provoked folk turns hostile together with the kin who see it: the folk of the same faction in the same room (within 8 cells when out in a corridor) with a clear line to them (`hostility::who_turns`); other factions stay friendly, and "The Ash Choir turn on you" shows. Hostile stays hostile while the delver stays in sight, and the world record keeps each folk's hit points, place and hostility (`record::FolkRecord`). A hostile folk who goes 25 s without sight of the delver (`folk.lose_interest_after` in `tuning.toml`, our own) loses interest: they turn friendly again and walk back to their spot to take up their routine (`Routine::head_home`), and striking them again provokes them again. Creatures already give up a chase sooner (3 s once at the place they last saw the delver, 9 s at most, `creatures::behaviour::LOSE_AFTER`), and go back to wandering their room. A hostile folk leaves their routine and comes at the delver as a creature does (straight in sight, else along `find_path`, sliding round everything), at twice their walking pace (the SRD's Dash, p. 93), facing them, and in reach swings their main-hand item with the delver's own `Weapon` rules (`folk_combat::Combatant`: an arm to the wrist of their folk's height plus the item's length, a fist an arm's length, a torch an improvised 1d4), a creature's swing time for the weapon (0.9 s for a dagger to 1.5 s for a mace) with 0.8 s between swings. The blow lands as the arm comes down (the main arm drawn up over the shoulder and brought down, `folk_pose::swing_turn`) if the delver is still in reach and in sight, and rolls to hit against the delver's Armor Class; a hit does the weapon's dice plus the Strength modifier, sent as an `AttackOnDelver` (its species is `None` for a folk), which takes the delver's hit points as any creature's blow does, and a miss whooshes past.
- A slain folk leaves everything they wore and held where they fell (`belongings::drop_belongings`, the one system every slain character drops through): as they start to fall, each item is put into the inventory as a loose item and set down round the body (`inventory::underfoot::set_down_around`, the ring a skeleton's gear is set down in, now shared), as the real item with its rarity, affixes and wear, ready to pick up and wear. The body is drawn again bare and marked `Looted` at once, so the floor changes are in before the record could read the floor and nothing is set down twice; the record keeps the body (`FolkRecord::dead`), and a level laid out again brings it back bare and lying where it fell, with no routine and nothing to drop.

## Props in the level

Our own proposal; neither the seed doc nor the plate furnishes a level.

- Each level stands containers and scenery along the walls of its rooms (`fb_level::PropSpot`): Armory chests, barrels, crates and amphorae (`Family::Chests` styles, each with its own seed and a loot seed for what it holds), and rubble, bones and pillars (`armory::build::scenery`). Strongboxes and coffers stand among them now and then. No container lies about as loose loot: the floor's loot table (`fb_level::generate::LOOT`) has no chests.
- Containers are drawn 1.6 times their true Armory size (`fb_level::CONTAINER_SCALE`, the game's `container_scale` tuning), so a chest is about a metre wide rather than the prototype's 60 cm; the user's call, since at true size they read as small from standing height. Their floor shapes, the room a chest's lid needs behind it and their look bounds grow with them. Placement keeps to the constant, not the tuning, so a seed lays out the same level whatever the tuning file says; a square container is also set off its walls by as far as its small turn (`CONTAINER_TURN`) swings its corners. A tuned scale well above 1.6 can push a container into the rock.
- Props come from a fourth stream of the level seed, after the layout, the loot and the folk, so they never move a wall, an item, a light or anyone standing about.
- A room holds about one prop per nine cells of floor, at most five, and one room in seven stands bare. Each stands with its back to a wall, or in a corner against two, far enough off the grid's wall face for the rock's relief and, for a chest, for its lid to swing open. Pillars stand only in corners. The first container in every level is a chest, and every level has at least one container.
- None stands in or beside a doorway, in a corridor, on the straight way in from the entrance, or near an item, a light or a folk. Treating every cell with a prop as rock, every other open cell is still reachable from the start, and every cell with a prop borders one that is, so a prop against its wall never closes the way.
- In the game (`game/src/props.rs`) a container is its Armory item, shut, drawn in its own palette, condition and etching like any item; scenery is drawn as the level's fixtures are. Scenery is a set of builders in `armory::build::scenery` rather than an Armory family, since it is never an item: no state, noun, turntable page or loot entry. Rubble is a heap settled in layers of roughened, faceted stone chunks with now and then a slab of fallen ceiling; bones are a skull with its eye sockets and nose pressed in (`geom::carve_hollows`), long bones turned on a lathe and curved ribs; a pillar is a faceted column with a slight swell on a stepped plinth, either whole with its capital bedded in the ceiling's relief or broken off short with a ragged top and chips of it lying on the plinth.
- Each prop rests at the lowest point of the drawn floor under it and blocks the way (`play::BlocksTheWay`, gathered into `play::Obstacles` with the folk): a circle for round things, a rectangle turned with it for chests, crates and pillars. Things set down from the inventory stop short of props as they do of rock.
- Looking at a container within reach and pressing the pick-up key opens it rather than picking it up: the inventory opens with the container's grid ahead of what lies underfoot, and a chest stands open in the level from then on, its lid raised (the Armory's `chest.open`). Every container is in the `Inventory` from the start, loose, filled from its loot seed, so what is left in it stays for the rest of the run (`docs/items.md`, "Containers").
- `print_level` marks containers `c` chest, `b` barrel, `x` crate, `a` amphora, `s` strongbox and `f` coffer, and scenery `%` rubble, `~` bones and `o` pillar.

## Creatures

Rats and snakes, something simple to fight. Their shapes, sizes, colourings, numbers and behaviour are our own proposal; their combat numbers are placeholders from the [D&D 5e SRD 5.1](https://media.wizards.com/2023/downloads/dnd/SRD_CC_v5.1.pdf) (CC-BY-4.0) until the character simulation is designed.

- `fb_creatures` (plain Rust) rolls a typed state from a seed: `rat(seed)` gives a rat or a giant rat (one in five), `snake(seed)` a poisonous snake or a constrictor (one in three), and `Creature::of(species, seed)` one of a chosen species. A rat has a length, build, tail and ear size, whisker length, a colouring (agouti, black, blue, fawn, albino) and a pattern (plain, Berkshire, hooded, variegated), named after the American Fancy Rat and Mouse Association's [standards](https://www.afrma.org/rstndrd.htm). A snake has a length, girth, head width, a colouring (earth, ash, moss, coral, sand, night), markings (diamonds, saddles, bands, a stripe or none) and the hand it coils to.
- Each state builds a `Rig`: named bones, each carrying its own `armory::ItemMesh` built from Armory geometry in the bone's space. A rat is a body, a head, four legs of three bones (upper leg, shin, paw) and a four-bone tail; a snake is a head with a jaw and a forked tongue, and 22 spine segments. Bones overlap where they meet (a snake's segments are rounded past their joints), so the body stays whole as it bends without skinning. Long parts run along their bone's +Y, so the Armory's wrapped patterns run round them.
- Rats have fur: the body's colour with tufts standing out along the back and flanks, combed back. Ears are cupped, thin and bare; paws are bare with toes and claws; the tail is bare and scaled; whiskers fan from the muzzle; the incisors are orange, as rats' enamel is. Snakes carry the Armory's `Scales` pattern; their markings are patches laid over the skin with three unused anchor vertices, so the pattern's wrap (measured from a part's vertices) matches the skin's and the scales run on across a marking. The belly has broad scutes. A viper has a broad head, brow scales, heat pits and fangs; a constrictor a narrower head and rows of small teeth; both have slit-pupilled eyes and a pale mouth lining that shows when the jaw drops. Every eye has a glint that glows a little, so eyes catch torchlight.
- Sizes depart from the SRD's (p. 92), by our own choice, so rats and snakes read as a threat across a room: each is drawn a size up from its stat block (`drawn_size`), and only the drawing changes; hit points and bites stay the SRD's. A rat (SRD Tiny) is 45 to 60 cm nose to rump, a giant rat (SRD Small) 1 to 1.5 m with its back at a delver's shin or knee, a poisonous snake (SRD Tiny, a short coil on the floor) 1.5 to 2.5 m and thick as a forearm, a constrictor (SRD Large) 4 to 6 m and thick as a thigh. A rat's body fits its drawn size's space and, tail and all, overfills the size below; a snake coiled fits its drawn space. Every creature's collision circle stays under half a cell, so the biggest still passes a one-cell doorway or corridor.
- `fb_creatures::pose` turns a motion into bone placements, so the rules are tested without Bevy. A rat trots, diagonal legs together, each paw planted flat on the floor through its stance and lifted through its swing, the leg bent to reach it by a two-bone solve; its body bobs, its tail swings in a wave and drags on the floor rather than passing through it, its head sniffs, turns and lunges to bite. A snake's spine is laid along a curve given by its heading along its length: a serpenoid curve (heading a sine of the distance along the body, after Shigeo Hirose's *Biologically Inspired Robots*, 1993) travelling back down the body to slither, an Archimedean spiral to coil, the front raised in an S to rear, which a strike throws straight. Rearing, the neck climbs in a column no steeper than about 72° (so the body keeps its length) and the head is held level over it, at least at a human's knee (0.285 of stature, Drillis and Contini's proportions as tabled in Winter's *Biomechanics and Motor Control of Human Movement*). Coiling and uncoiling slide each point of the body from where it lay to where it is going, rather than blending headings, which swept the body round through walls. Laid out, the neck leads from the creature's root and the body follows the trail the head left (`Trail`, a `SnakePose::bend` along the body), so a 5 m constrictor turning a corner keeps to the floor it crossed; coiled, the coil's middle is on the root. A snake's collision circle is its coil, capped below half a cell. The belly rests on the floor; the neck lifts to carry the deeper head, a straight segment on a bending line is lifted clear of the floor, and the jaw drops only as far as the floor lets it.
- Each species carries its SRD stat block's hit points, hit dice, bite damage, speed, senses and page (`Species::stat_block`): rat 1 (1d4 − 1) hit points and a 1-point bite (p. 387); giant rat 7 (2d6), bite 1d4 + 2 (p. 378), found only from the second level down, since a pack of them is too much for a fresh delver (`fb_level::GIANTS_FROM_DEPTH`, our own); poisonous snake 2 (1d4), bite 1 plus 2d4 poison halved on a DC 10 Constitution save (p. 386); constrictor 13 (2d10 + 2), bite 1d6 + 2 (p. 369; its Constrict is left for later). Speeds are the block's feet per 6-second round (p. 90).
- Skeletons (`fb_creatures::skeleton`, #288) are a human's bones, rolled from a seed (`SkeletonState`): stature (0.93 to 1.07 of a human), build, bone colour from bleached through yellowed to dark, cracks, pitting, and missing pieces (the jaw, ribs, fingers, teeth). The rig is 18 bones (pelvis, spine, ribcage, neck, skull, jaw, and an upper arm, forearm and hand, a thigh, shin and foot a side) built from the Armory's bone primitives (`armory::build::bone`: long bones with knobbed ends and condyles, vertebrae, ribs on a half ellipse, a skull carved with sockets and a nose with teeth, a separate jaw, a pelvis, hands and feet of separate bones) on the joints of the `fb_body` human the skeleton is measured on (`SkeletonDims::body`). Every bone's rest rotation is the identity and its origin is on its joint, so a pose is a turn about a joint in body axes; the hand and foot are built in `fb_body`'s frames (`SkeletonDims::hand_frame` is `Body::hand_placement`, a left hand's `x` unmirrored, and the mesh mirrored), so worn and held gear can be fitted to the bones as to a body (#289). Poses (`pose::skeleton`): standing, a walk whose planted feet rest on the floor exactly (each leg solved to its ankle; the pelvis rides at the height the lower reach allows, rounded where the weight changes legs; arms swing against the legs, the ribcage against the hips; a step is 0.7 of the leg), and a swing of the right arm (guard, wind-up over the shoulder, blow, follow-through) with both feet planted and the weight shifting. Its stat block is the SRD's skeleton (p. 346): Medium undead, Armor Class 13 (armor scraps), 13 (2d8 + 4) hit points, speed 30 ft., 10, 14, 15, 6, 8, 5, vulnerable to bludgeoning, immune to poison, exhaustion and being poisoned, darkvision 60 ft., a shortsword (+4, 1d6 + 2 piercing) and a shortbow (+4, 1d6 + 2, 80/320 ft.); the stat blocks gained Armor Class, ability scores, damage and condition immunities, a ranged attack, challenge and XP. `--skeleton` opens the creature view on a skeleton (Left and Right roll seeds, M stands, walks and swings it). Bone colours, the skull's and pelvis's shapes and every amplitude are our own.
- Each level holds one to three packs of two to five rats, each pack one species (one in four giant rats) round a point in a room of its own, and up to two snakes (one in three a constrictor), each alone in a room no folk stand in (`fb_level::CreatureSpot`, with the pack it belongs to). None is in the starting room, a corridor, a doorway or beside one, near an item, a light or a folk, or against a prop or another creature. They come from a fifth stream of the level seed, after the props, so they never move a wall, an item, a light, a folk or a prop. `print_level` marks them `r` rat, `R` giant rat, `v` poisonous snake and `S` constrictor.
- In the game (`game/src/creatures.rs`) each creature is a root on the drawn floor with one entity per bone carrying that bone's Armory parts, drawn by the `ItemSpawner` in the house style (toon bands, ink, wear; skin and fur with a little grime). Each frame the root's movement (pace and the distance walked, which advances the step cycle or the wave so feet and body do not slide) and what the creature means to do (`Intent`: coil, rear, strike, jaw, tongue, a head turn, eased at their own rates) become a pose that is copied onto the bones. Snake segments and the head rest on the floor's relief under each. Standing about, rats sniff and sway their tails; snakes lie coiled, raising their heads now and then and flicking their tongues every three seconds or so.
- Skeleton gear (`fb_items::undead::skeleton_outfit`, `fb_creatures::gear`, #289). A skeleton rolls its gear from its seed through `roll_item` like loot, so every piece has a rarity, affixes and a history, and then wears it hard (wear 0.55 to 0.95, iron rusted, cloth and leather rotted, edges nicked). Two in ten are kitted (helm, body armour, gloves or gauntlets, boots, a sword, axe or mace and nearly always a shield); four in ten are part dressed, each piece by chance, with a tattered tunic where there is no armour; four in ten are bare-boned, usually with a weapon (a spear, bow or club too) and sometimes nothing at all. Worn gear is fitted by `fb_body::fit_worn` to the human the bones are laid on, as to any folk, so it keeps the shape of the man who wore it and hangs off the ribs and limbs; held gear by `fb_body::fit_hands`. The rig is rigid, so a fitted mesh is cut by triangle and each piece rides the bone it lies nearest (headgear the skull; a torso garment the ribcage, its sleeves cut off along the arms below the ball of the shoulder; gloves the hands; a boot's foot the foot and its shaft the shin; held gear the hand), stored in its bone's own space, much as plate armour is jointed. Gloves and boots replace the bare bony hand and foot. An arm that holds something is posed by the fitting (the forearm raised to a level grip), where the rig's arms hang at rest, so its `ArmStance` is the turn of upper arm, forearm and hand that brings the bones to the fitted arm and the gear is stored for the bones in that stance (`pose::skeleton_armed`; a holding arm does not swing as it walks, the right arm's blow still drives its upper arm and forearm and the left arm braces its shield). The bony hand of an armed arm is closed into the grip `Body::grip` assumes (`armory::build::bone::hand_gripping` lays the fingers along `fb_body`'s digit joints at the curl a gripping hand takes), so a held weapon sits in the fingers. Tests across 200 seeds (the sweeps are `#[ignore]`d with a default-run `_smoke` twin): the same seed gives the same gear; no more than 3% of a piece's vertices come inside a long bone's shaft, the spine or the skull (a sleeve's collapsed root lies on the arm's axis), none is further than 3.2 units from a bone, and a held weapon passes within 0.3 of the fist's grip.
- Skeletons in the game (`game/src/creatures/skeletons.rs`, #290). A skeleton is drawn in its fitted gear (`SkeletonGear::roll`, each piece a child of the bone it rides) and posed with its stance. Everything fixed about it is worked out once at spawn into a `SkeletonKit` on its `Critter` (the bones' measures and height, the weapon's reach, dice and swing time, Armor Class, the shield's chance to block), so no frame rebuilds a body. Its weapon sets what it does: a blow reaches an arm to the wrist and the weapon's length beyond it, as the delver's does (`combat::Weapon::in_hand`), so a spear outreaches a sword and a sword a dagger; it rolls the weapon's dice and damage bonus; a swing takes 0.9 s with a dagger to 1.5 s with a mace and lands as the arm comes down (0.62 of it), if the delver is still within a seventh again of its reach; with nothing in hand it claws for 1d3 at an arm's length, and a bow is swung as an improvised 1d4 (nothing shoots yet). Armor Class is the delver's sum (10, the stat block's Dexterity 14 modifier, each item's armour) when it wears anything, else the stat block's 13. The delver's swings roll against it, and its own blows add its weapon's ToHit to the stat block's +4. A shield turns a blow from within 60 degrees of its front with a chance of 0.2 plus 0.08 for each point of the shield's armour, at most 0.65 (our own); bludgeoning (a mace, club, staff or fist: `Weapon::bludgeoning`) does it double damage and nothing is poison. Struck down it falls onto its back with its knees drawn up and its arms flung out (`pose::skeleton_fallen`, `feedback::laid_back`) and, as it starts to fall, every item it wore and held is put into the inventory as loose items and set on the floor round it (`FloorChange::SetDownAt`), as real items with the rarity, affixes and wear they had, once: the body is marked `Looted` as it dies and the drop fires at once, so a body put back later does not drop its gear again. It never uses a rat's squeak, squeal and stride or a snake's hiss and coil: its steps (one a stride, by its leg), rattle, clack when struck, clang on a shield and fall in a heap are `tools/synth_bones.py`'s. `FB_SPAWN=skeleton:<seed>[,<seed>...]` stands skeletons in front of the level's start, for captures. The first stone halls (depth 2) are eased for a fresh delver (#354, our own): their dead wear no armour, only rags, so their Armor Class is the stat block's 13, and hold a dagger or a club (1d4) or nothing but their 1d3 claws (`fb_items::undead::FIRST_HALLS`), and no more than two stand in a hall (`fb_level::creatures::FIRST_HALLS_GUARDS`); deeper halls keep the full table. Measured over 399 fresh delvers (8.3 hit points on average, the SRD's first-level hit points) and the guards of real levels, a depth-2 guard downs one on its 4.2nd landed blow on average (3.0 before) and the delver hits it 51% of the time with a plain weapon (40% before); `against_a_fresh_delver::report_the_stone_halls` prints the table. Each blow is logged by the weapon ("Skeleton slashes" with a sword, "strikes" with a mace or club, "stabs" with a dagger or spear, "hacks" with an axe, "claws" bare), and every swing's rattle and drawn-back arm come at least 0.5 s before the blow lands (`skeletons::MIN_WIND_UP`).
- Skeletons in the levels (#291). Only levels of stone halls have skeletons, placed after every rat and snake so the rough rock levels draw nothing new from the creature stream and keep their creatures. Each colonnaded hall (the three largest) has one to three guards standing in the gaps between its columns, facing the aisle, one pack to a hall; one or two side rooms (rooms with no columns, none a snake's or the starting room) hold a skeleton, or now and then a pair, facing the nearest door. Never on the two cells straight in from a door or by one, nor on a column, a stair or where a delver arrives. A level always has one when it has a room for it. A skeleton restored dead from the world record lies as a bare corpse marked `Looted`, its worn gear not drawn; one killed sheds its gear at once (as the item floor changes the record reads) so leaving right after still records it, once; a living one comes back in the gear its seed rolls, with its recorded hit points and place. A creature's blow lands only if the delver is in sight at the moment it lands, never round a wall. To hold the frame rate: a level's skeletons are rolled and fitted side by side on threads before any is spawned (about 2.3 s down to 0.4 s for five), the thin bones of hands and feet are drawn with a coarser lathe (43.6k bone vertices down to 34.9k), and a skeleton more than 2 m past the fog's end (`fog_end`) is not drawn.
- `--creature` shows one creature on the turntable's plate under the studio light, still, moving in place, rearing or striking, with its SRD hit points and bite (a skeleton stands, walks and swings; `--skeleton` opens the view on one).
- Creatures act for themselves (`game/src/creatures/behaviour.rs`). They wander their home room, idling between walks (a snake mostly lies coiled and seldom moves far from its spot). They notice the player within their sense range (the stat block's darkvision or blindsight, at least 20 ft, since the player carries a light) when the straight line between them crosses no rock (`fb_level::path::line_of_sight`, Amanatides and Woo's grid traversal). In sight they head straight in; out of sight they follow the shortest way round the rock to where they last saw the player (`fb_level::path::find_path`, A* on the grid with the octile heuristic, cut short by `steer_toward`). Each step slides round rock, folk, props, the player and each other (`player::slide_around`), so they never pass through any of them. Rats run at twice their speed, as the SRD's Dash (p. 93) gives; snakes come on at their speed and stay within three cells of their spot. In reach they turn to the player and attack on a cooldown (a rat every 1.5 s, a snake every 2.5 s, where the SRD's round is 6 s): a rat lunges and bites, a snake draws its coils in and rears, then strikes with its jaw open. The bite sends an `AttackOnDelver` with the stat block's bite (and a poisonous snake's poison, before its save) rolled, which combat applies (below). Out of sight for 3 s once at the place they last saw the player, they go back to wandering their room.
- Combat (`game/src/combat/`) is a placeholder until the character simulation is designed (#122): one pool of hit points a side, with every number cited from the SRD 5.1 or labelled our own. Left click swings the main hand's item, each swing thrown as one of the strikes its kind suits, never the same one twice running while there is another (our own weights): maces, axes and clubs fall mostly overhead or down from a high corner, swords cut from every side, a staff swings flat across, spears and daggers stab, and bare hands punch (the eye leans into each blow). The swing heads for the creature the blow will land on, at the point of its body nearest the crosshair as the swing begins, carried to the same place on the screen in the view model's narrower field of view, or for the crosshair, 3 m ahead, with nothing in reach. As a strike is drawn back the wrist turns the item into the strike's plane, the plane through the fist's line to where it heads and the way the strike travels across the screen, with its edge (or an axe's bit) leading; it is then thrown in an arc through that plane, the fist swinging across with it, and followed through a little past. A stab turns the item's point (the end of it farthest from the fist) onto the line to where it heads as it draws back, then hooks in and drives along it, so a spear carried across the view and a dagger standing up in the fist both strike point first. Each swing also turns its strike's direction, wind-up and width a little of its own, so repeated blows rarely look alike. All of this is our own, tuned against captures; the browser prototypes had no swings. `--swings` is a practice mode for trying them. Partway through, the blow lands on the nearest living creature whose body is within the weapon's reach and within 50° of straight ahead. A body is where its bones are posed that moment: each bone's main surface gives it a capsule (`fb_creatures::Bone::hit`, fitted along the long side of its bounding box, our own fit), tested as a row of overlapping spheres, so a rat's head, legs and tail and every segment of a snake, laid out along the floor or reared, can be struck, not just a circle at the creature's root; the reach is an arm to the wrist (0.332 of stature, Drillis and Contini's proportions as tabled in Winter) plus the item's length, so a sword reaches about 1.5 m, near the SRD's 5 ft (p. 95). A swing that finds a creature rolls to hit against its Armor Class (see "Attack rolls" below) and on a hit does the weapon's dice from `fb_items` plus its damage bonus and the Strength modifier (p. 96), at least 1 (our own; the SRD allows 0); a bare fist does 1 + Strength (p. 95), anything that isn't a melee weapon 1d4 as an improvised weapon (p. 65). Rolls come from a run's own dice, seeded from the clock, so the world stays a function of its seed while fights differ. A struck creature flashes, is knocked back (less the bigger it is), and squeaks or hisses (synthesised by `tools/synth_combat.py`). The flash turns every part 65% of the way to the page's cream and adds glow, holds at full for 0.12 s and fades over 0.2 s more; glow alone multiplies a part's own colour and left a black rat dark (our own). A creature has its stat block's hit points; at 0 it stands flashing through that 0.12 s hold, so a blow that kills at once is still seen to land, then collapses, a rat rolling onto its back and a snake going limp along the floor, and its body stays for the run. Bodies block nothing, since all lie flat enough to step over. The delver has a 1st-level character's hit points, the class's hit die at its highest plus the Constitution modifier (fighter and ranger 10, cleric 8, mage 6 as the SRD's wizard; pp. 24, 35, 15, 52), shown as "HP 8/12" in Plex Mono over a thin bar low on the left; each bite flashes the view's edge red. A poisonous snake's venom is halved on a d20 + Con save against DC 10 (p. 386). A failed save also leaves the delver Poisoned for a game hour (`fb_character::poison`), a departure from the SRD's snake, which does its venom once: the hour is our own, from the SRD's lasting poisons (1 minute for the quasit's same venom, p. 273, up to 24 hours; 1 hour the commonest, p. 204). While poisoned the life orb's liquid turns green and eases back to red as it wears off, and a Poisoned badge shows the minutes left. The SRD condition's disadvantage on attack rolls (p. 358) applies to every swing while it lasts, and the badge says "Attacking at disadvantage"; there are no ability checks yet. A constrictor's bite holds the delver at a quarter of their pace until they strike it, a placeholder for the SRD's Constrict and its restraining grapple (p. 370). At 0 hit points the delver has a last gasp of 0.5 s (`vitals::LAST_GASP`, our own) to drink a healing draught, from a quickslot or the inventory; healed above 0 they stand on, and nothing strikes them meanwhile, but they cannot swing or take a stair. Otherwise the run ends: they can no longer swing, shoot or raise a shield, the view fades to "You fall in the abyss" and after 3.5 s the game returns to the title, clearing everything the delve spawned, the inventory screen's doll stage, hover cards and what lay underfoot among it; death saving throws are left out.
- Recovering (`fb_character::recovery`). A wounded delver whose hunger and thirst are both in their best band (Fed and Quenched) and who is not poisoned regains a hit point every 10 game minutes (50 real seconds at the usual clock; `recovery.minutes_per_hit_point` in `tuning.toml`), never above their maximum, stepped by the game clock with the needs (`simulate::step_the_delver`). The time put toward the next point starts over whenever they stop recovering, so a meal does not bank time spent hungry. A green Recovering badge, on the HUD and in the sheet's conditions, says "+1 HP every 10 minutes while fed and watered". Our own: the SRD heals only by resting (SRD 5.1 p. 86, "Resting"), and there are no rests yet.
- Attack rolls (`fb_character::combat`). Every attack in the game rolls the same way, whoever makes it and whatever it is aimed at: a d20 plus the attacker's to-hit against the target's Armor Class, hitting when the total reaches it, a natural 20 always hitting and a natural 1 always missing ([SRD 5.1 p. 96](https://media.wizards.com/2023/downloads/dnd/SRD_CC_v5.1.pdf#page=96), "Attack Rolls"; `attack_roll_with`). The to-hit (`ToHit`) of the delver and of folk is the Strength modifier, the proficiency bonus of +2 (a 1st- to 4th-level character's, SRD 5.1 "Beyond 1st Level"; everyone counts as proficient with whatever they swing, our own simplification until classes carry weapon proficiencies; `PROFICIENCY_BONUS`) and the weapon's ToHit; a creature's is the attack bonus its stat block prints (rat +0, p. 387; giant rat +4, p. 378; poisonous snake +5, p. 386; constrictor snake +4, p. 369; skeleton +4, p. 346), plus a skeleton's weapon's ToHit. Armor Class (`combat::ArmourClass`) is on everything that fights: a rat's, a snake's and a bare skeleton's is its stat block's (rat 10, giant rat 12, poisonous snake 13, constrictor snake 12, skeleton 13; `combat::arm_creatures`), and a folk's, a geared skeleton's and the delver's is 10 + the Dexterity modifier + each worn item's armour, the delver's being the one the sheet shows. Anything with none counts as 10 (our own fallback). A creature's bite or a folk's blow at the delver rolls in `creatures::behaviour::BlowsAtDelver`: a hit is an `AttackOnDelver`, which hurts, jolts the view and flashes it red; a miss is a `MissedDelver`, which only whooshes past from the attacker's side (the swing's whoosh), and each roll is logged with its terms. Once the delver is down (0 hit points, or the run ending), nothing starts a bite or a swing at them and nothing is rolled or sent (`BlowsAtDelver::delver_is_down`; our own, since the SRD's attacks on a creature at 0 hit points, p. 97, follow death saving throws, which are left out). Advantage and disadvantage roll a second d20 and keep the higher or the lower ([SRD 5.1 p. 76](https://media.wizards.com/2023/downloads/dnd/SRD_CC_v5.1.pdf#page=76), "Advantage and Disadvantage"; `Odds`): a poisoned delver's swings roll at disadvantage (Poisoned, [p. 358](https://media.wizards.com/2023/downloads/dnd/SRD_CC_v5.1.pdf#page=358); `Poisoned::attack_odds`).

## Ways between levels

Our own proposal; the seed doc has levels but no way between them.

- The abyss is set when a delve begins. A run has one seed, and the level at each depth is always the same level, from `fb_level::depth_seed(run_seed, depth)`: the run's own seed at depth 1 (so `--seed 1207` is still level 1207) and a hash of the two below it. Going back up to a level finds it as it was laid out; persisting what happened on it is a separate task.
- Every level has a way down (`fb_level::Stair`, kind `Down`): a cell of floor against a wall of a room, far by path from the start (one of the cells whose approach lies at least 85% as far by path as the furthest such cell's, `FAR_SHARE` in `fb_level::stairs`, picked by the stair stream), with nothing else on it or on the cell in front, which is where you stand to use it. It is placed last, from its own salted stream (`STAIR_SALT`), so no earlier stream moved: level 1 of every seed is byte-identical to before (a test pins the hashes). Shutting its cell cuts no part of the level off.
- Below the first level the entrance is a way up. The door the delver came in by is not a door there: its cell is the stair up (kind `Up`), the start cell in front of it is where they arrive, and nothing is drawn behind it. The first level keeps its door, which shuts behind the delver; coming back up to it finds the door shut already.
- F near a stair and facing it (within 1.8 cells, within 60 degrees of straight at it) takes it: the view fades to black, the next level is made, and the view fades in on the delver standing in front of the matching stair, turned away from it, with "Depth N" fading in and out near the top. Taking the way down arrives at the next level's way up; taking the way up arrives beside the previous level's way down. The delver stands still while it lasts.
- The delve never leaves `AppState::Delving` to do it. `game/src/travel.rs` swaps `CurrentLevel`, and every system that lays a level out (stone, fixtures and stairs, folk, creatures, props, floor items, drips) clears its pieces and lays out the new level when it changes. The player, with their hit points, needs, stamina and mana, the character, the inventory and its equipment, and the map's record of each level seen stay as they are.
- A way down is drawn as a stone-kerbed stair going down into the floor (no floor in its cell) and a way up as steps climbing through a hole in the ceiling into a capped shaft (`game/src/level/stairs.rs`). The map marks seen stairs with a box and three treads, and its title names the depth. `print_level` marks them `>` and `<`.

# Forever Below: design

A copy of the seed design doc from the "Procedural Ultima" project, as of the handoff.

## Premise
A first-person, stylized-3D dungeon crawler in the spirit of Ultima Underworld. You come through a door into a Stygian abyss and it closes behind you. There is only one way out, and the abyss is infinitely deep. The way out is a rumor the world keeps feeding you, told differently by every culture.

## Core loop
Delve until you find a level worth inhabiting. Gain favor with its factions and make it your base for a time. Faction quests lead to rare items. You can keep a quest's reward instead of completing it; the quest expires and your standing drops. Then the abyss pulls you deeper.

## Systems
- Every character, you and NPCs alike, has skills, stats and an inventory, and is simulated: hunger, thirst, sleep, warmth, light, dirt, sickness, disease, limb health, pain, breath, encumbrance.
- Items persist in the world and carry history. Legendaries from earlier runs come back as found objects.
- Item stats are Diablo-style: rarity, plus prefixes and suffixes that can raise or lower any parameter.
- Lockpicking, sneaking, and breaking doors and containers by material and stats.
- Bartering depends on stats, skills, faction, personal relationship, scarcity, alignment and appearance.
- Quests are generated from NPC needs, alignments and goals.
- Folk: goblins, gnomes, humans, lizardfolk, dwarves, pigfolk. Enemies: slime cubes, skeletons, beholders, demons, ghouls, rats, snakes, bats, scorpions, spiders, giant hornets, worms, dragons, vampires, werewolves, tentacle monsters, liches, devils, ghosts.
- D&D coinage. No mining: resources come from what is in the abyss.
- Classes: Fighter, Ranger, Mage, Cleric. Freshly fallen and Scavenger are starting looks.

## Visual direction
- Spline Armory's renderer is the style guide (see `rendering.md`).
- Ultima Underworld's portrait-and-doll lineup is the character reference, at higher fidelity (`reference/concept/`).
- The inventory follows the parchment concept, restyled as a dark ledger so it does not glare over the abyss: engraved item icons in footprint grids, an underfoot drop zone, prepared outfits on invisible mannequins, and the doll inside a runic seal with slot boxes around it.

## Folk in the level (since the handoff)

Our own proposal; neither the seed doc nor the plate places anyone in a level.

- Each level holds three to ten folk (`fb_level::FolkSpot`), in groups of two to four, one faction to a room. Each stands in the faction's claim (below). A group stands round the room's brazier if it has one, otherwise round a point inside it, facing in as if talking. Nobody stands in the starting room, in a corridor, in a doorway or beside one, or on the loot.
- Factions are generated from the world (delve) seed (`crates/fb_faction`, #536). `fb_faction::World::new(world_seed)` rolls a pool of twelve to twenty `FactionSpec`s, and a level draws two to four of them from its own seed (`World::draw`); a faction on several levels is the same spec on each (name, colours, crest). Each spec has a name from the faction name grammar (below), unique in the pool; the folk it leans toward and a pair of outfits (keys of `data/outfits.json`); an alignment; an intention (way out, trade, worship, plunder); one to three likes and one to three hates (creature kinds, other factions of the pool, folk, classes; none in both); two tinctures of opposite kinds and a crest, a `armory::heraldry::Field` rolled by the shield's rule of tincture; an armour lean (padded, leather, mail, scale, plate); and whether it is insular. Three in four members are the faction's folk and the rest any folk, but an insular faction, which hates another folk, is wholly its own. The level holds its drawn specs (`Level::factions`) and each `FolkSpot::faction` indexes the pool; `generate_in(world_seed, depth, params)` is the level at a depth of a delve, a pure function of the two. `generate(seed, depth)` takes its seed as both the world's and the level's. The seven names in `data/factions.json` (the Lantern-keepers, the Mudfoot clan, the Brothers of the Last Door, the Scale Court, the Hogsmeet traders, the Gnome Guild of Gears, the Ash Choir) are examples the grammar was written from, and nothing reads them. Preview a pool with `cargo run -p fb_faction --example world -- <world seed> [level seeds]`. Legendary item histories name factions from the same grammar but not from a particular world's pool, since an item carries no world.
- Folk come from a third stream of the level seed, after the layout and the loot, so they never move a wall or an item.
- Factions hold ground (`fb_level::claims`, #541). Each faction on a level grows a `Claim`, a connected set of rooms grown out from a seed room along the room graph (`RoomGraph`), from its own stream of the level seed after the furnishing and before the folk, so claims move no wall, item or light. A claim's size follows the faction's strength: the level's six to ten folk are shared among the claiming factions and a claim takes a room for about every three members, plus one more by a roll. Claims never overlap, the starting room is never claimed, and a room no one claims is wild: creatures, webs and the like keep out of claims as they kept out of folk rooms (`creatures::has_folk`). Creatures keep half the rooms, so a small level claims for fewer factions than it holds, and a faction that finds no ground is not on the level (`Level::factions` holds only claimants). A claim grows only through rooms of at least nine cells, the size folk gather in, so closets and small chambers stay wild, and it never takes more than half the rooms. On stone halls the claims take the colonnades first. Ground is fixed per seed, and only the delver changes it. `fb_level::settle_claims` gives a level's claims after the delver's work: a faction with several members left keeps its claim whole, one with a single member keeps the one room that member holds, and one with none holds nothing, since only the delver wipes a faction out. The game writes these claims into `record::LevelRecord::claims` when a level is left and applies them when it is rebuilt, so leaving and returning finds every claim as it was left; a record lasts one delve. `print_level` marks each claim's rooms with its faction's letter (`W`, `X`, `Y`, `Z`) and prints a legend. The in-game map does not show claims yet.
- Denizen factions (#627, `fb_faction`, `fb_level`): from `fb_denizens::DENIZEN_DEPTH` (3) down a level can also hold a denizen faction, one made of minotaurs, cyclopes, fauns or demons rather than folk. A world's pool is its folk factions followed by four to six denizen factions (one of each kind, then up to two more), rolled from salted streams of their own after the folk, so a world's folk factions are exactly what they were; ids follow the folk's and stay under 256. `FactionSpec::denizen` is `Some(kind)` for them; their `folk` is a placeholder (human) that nothing reads, and `member_body` picks a member's `DenizenBody` among the kind's bodies (demons: winged, horned, succubus). A denizen faction rolls a name from the name grammar keyed by its kind (`data/faction_names.json` has `minotaur`, `cyclops`, `faun` and `demon` beside the folk: "the Horned Maze", "the One-Eyed Forge", "the Revel of the Ivied Vineyard", "the Ninth Pit"), unique in the pool; an alignment weighted by kind (demons lean evil, fauns chaotic good or neutral, minotaurs chaotic evil, cyclopes lawful neutral); an intention weighted by kind; likes and hates from creatures, classes, folk and every other faction of the pool; and colours and a crest as any faction. Denizens wear little, so their outfits and armour lean (light for fauns, heavier for minotaurs and cyclopes) are what they carry, and the creature bodies may ignore them. A denizen is never insular. A folk faction's likes and hates are not rolled against denizen factions (they stay as the folk rolled them, so shallow levels do not change), though a `Preference::Faction` may name one. `World::draw_at(level_seed, depth)` is the level's draw: above the depth exactly the folk draw (`World::draw`), at and below it the same folk and then usually one denizen faction (85 in 100) and sometimes a second (a fifth of those), last in the list; `generate` uses it, and levels above the depth are byte-identical to before (`levels_above_the_denizen_depth_are_unchanged`). Denizen factions claim ground as any faction does, but the claim order puts them first, so a level the creatures leave little room on still keeps its denizens (about 79 in 100 deeper levels hold one in a 96 level sweep). Their members stand in the claim as `FolkSpot`s with `denizen: Some(body)`: they are big, so they gather in wider rings and keep 1.1 cells from each other, 0.8 from loot and lights and 0.45 from walls (folk keep 0.7, 0.55 and 0.3). Guards and traders are planned for their camps as for folk, so barter can be gated by tongue later. The game does not spawn them yet: `folk::has_figure` leaves their spots out of the figures it builds, and the saved records still count their places. `print_level` marks them with a lower case letter and names their kind.
- Claimed rooms have roles (`fb_level::roles`, #546), so a claim looks lived in. Each room of a claim is a `RoomRole`: the hall is the claim room nearest the level's start whose opening leads out of the claim (its main door); the room nearest the hall is the hearth, the deepest is the sleeping room, a faction with traders (`FolkSpot::trade` other than guard, drawn before the roles from the trade stream, #550) keeps a market in the room between, and any rooms left over are stores. A claim short of rooms doubles up: the hall takes the roles with no room left, hearth first, so a one-room claim is hall, hearth and sleeping room at once. Roles come from their own salted stream after the claims and the camps' trades and before the folk, so they move no wall or item. A room has one fire. A hall that is not a hearth gets a brazier where the room has none, and a brazier always stands alone: no prop is set over or against it. A hearth, which is nearly always the hall too, gets a lit campfire in place of any brazier (`roles::light_roles`, the one thing here that adds lights, the campfire as `LightKind::Campfire`; only where no campfire fits does a hearth keep a brazier, so none is dark; the layout, items and lights hash was retaken for it, #546 and #569). The campfire is `Prop::Campfire`, the Armory campfire family's set form, lit, rolled per hearth from the level seed (`props::campfire_state`): a tripod with a pot, a hook with a pot, or just the fire. It blocks the way like a brazier, stands clear of doors, the way in, folk and other props, and its light is `Campfire::light`. Props furnish each role first, from their own stream, and the rest of the level is propped as before except that claimed rooms get no random props: the hearth gets its campfire and a keg or crate against a wall, the sleeping room a human-length bedroll (new `Scenery::Bedroll`, about 17 units long, lying along a wall across two cells) for three of four members up to six, the market three to five crates, chests, barrels and amphorae along the walls as stalls, and a store two to four. Market stalls and the hearth's keg are empty (`Prop::Container` with `loot` set to `fb_level::NO_LOOT`; `Prop::holds_loot`), since traders sell from their own shelves and a stall is set dressing; stores are stocked, faction property that will cost standing to take once stealing lands. The level's first loot container is still a chest. A bedroll is small new scenery and not a new Armory family; the hearth's campfire is the Armory campfire family, never a one-off shape. The packed campfire kit is left for camps (#315). Folk stand by role and by trade (#550): see the next paragraph for guards and traders; the rest gather round the hearth's campfire, then the hall, then the sleeping room. Props keep clear of doorways and folk, and every open cell stays reachable. `print_level <seed> <depth> roles` shows each claim room's role letter (`H` hall, `F` hearth, `S` sleeping, `M` market, `T` store) and `FB_DEBUG_CAMERA=role` with `FB_DEBUG_ROLE` or `FB_DEBUG_ROOM` stands the capture camera in one.
- Factions fly their colours (`fb_level::banners`, #551). Each claim's hall door (`roles::main_door`: the hall's cell at the claim's outward opening and the open cell beyond it) has a banner on the wall at each side inside (a door at a room's corner has one), the cloth moved 2.5 units off the jamb. Outside, where a room or widening lies beyond the door, a flanking banner hangs on each side of the wall's outer face; in a straight corridor, where side walls would show cloth edge-on, one short wide banner (`BannerSite::Lintel`, 5 to 7 wide, 5 to 8 long) hangs over the lintel on the door's corridor face, facing the approach. The hall and the market hang one or two more on bare walls clear of doors, lights and props, at least two cells apart, the hall's on the wall facing its door first. Hall, market and flanking banners are drawn big, to read across a hall: `Banner::roll_hall` (5 to 8 wide, 14 to 20 long; the family's length limit is widened to 22), with the rod set from the cloth so its middle hangs about 20 units up, and the cloth shortened under a low ceiling rather than hung higher. Banners are a list of their own, `Level::banners` (`BannerSpot`), not `Prop`s: they hang above everything standing, so they block no cell and move no prop, creature or stair, and come from their own salted stream after the props. `levels_match_those_before_finishes` was retaken; the layout, items and lights hash did not move. The game (`game/src/props.rs`, `lay_out_banners`) builds each as a rod-hung banner with the faction's `Field` set exactly and the hem trimmed in the crest's tincture, lit as every item is.
- In the game (`game/src/folk.rs`) each spot is a dressed `fb_body` figure. Who they are is a pure function of the spot: a body from its folk and seed, a name from `data/syl.json`, and one of the faction's outfits rolled through `fb_items::outfit` (`data/outfits.json`, the plate's `preparedSet`, which also gives a bare-headed gnome a pointed hat). Anyone the outfit leaves empty-handed carries a torch. They are dressed by the same `game/src/figure.rs` as the doll (`fit_hands`, then `fit_worn`), with the head, hair and headgear split off to turn about the neck. A lit torch or lantern in hand lights the room.
- Standing, folk breathe (about 15 breaths a minute, each a little faster or slower) and now and then shift their weight. Without a skeleton the whole figure moves a little about its feet. Within 4 m they turn their head toward the player, up to 70 degrees either side, about the neck; under a hood, whose cape lies on the shoulders, the body turns a little instead. Each stands in a circle the player cannot walk into (`play::Obstacles`, `player::slide_around`).
- Looking at someone within reach (2 m, as for picking things up) shows a card under the crosshair in the item card's style: their name, folk and faction ("Dwarf of the Ash Choir"), and what they wear and hold, each item's name in its rarity's colour. The item card wins when both are in view.
- Some folk have a trade (`fb_faction::Trade`, #544): a profession (armorer, weaponsmith, bowyer, tentmaker, general goods, alchemist, enchanter, or guard), a tier (novice < apprentice < expert < master) and, for smiths, a style (an armorer's padded, leather, mail, scale or plate; a weaponsmith's blades, axes and clubs, maces, or spears and staves). Tiers open by depth (`fb_faction::TradeRules`, defaults in code so a lab can tune them): novices and apprentices from level 1, experts from level 3, and masters only where the world seed places them (`World::masters`, from level 6 down, never two of one trade on a level). Each trade gets masters in turn from a shuffled bag, one or two on about 60% of the levels from 6 to 30, each with a stable slot and no faction fixed up front: the level resolves the faction as the one at `slot` modulo the factions that actually place folk on it, in the level's faction order (the next if that camp is all masters already; `fb_level::trades::master_faction` reads the answer off the generated level), so a folk can name a real master on a real level and the rumour is always true (`Masters::deeper`). A level reads its masters and generates them there (`fb_level::trades`): a master takes a trader's place in the faction's camp, or someone with no trade, and nobody is ever moved between camps. Everyone else is assigned per camp from a stream of their own, before the folk are placed (`fb_level::trades::plan`, one entry per member the claim places: guards first, then traders, then no trade), so no wall, item or light moves and the plan decides the market and the guards' posts (`FolkSpot::trade`; masters are then put in place after the folk stand, replacing a trader): each camp (a faction's folk on the level) has a guard (a second in a camp of four or more, one time in three) and about half its folk have a trade or a guard post, a trade-intention faction's camp one more; the rest have none. An armorer takes the faction's armour lean three times in four. Guards and traders stand where their work is (#550, `fb_level::folk::posts`): a camp's first guard stands inside its hall's main door, on the floor cell one step along the wall from the cell behind the doorway, on the side away from the door leaf's hinge (a swinging leaf hinges at the doorway's left edge looking in and rests against that wall, #553; a portcullis has no hinge and no sweep). Where the leaf hangs and how wide it is come from the door's kind and its doorway's style in one place, `fb_level::door` (`LeafSweep::of`, shared with `game/src/level/geometry/door.rs`), so posts keep out of the swing the game draws, and faces the doorway; never on the doorway or the cell behind it (a delver is 7 units across and a folk up to 10, in a 20 unit doorway) nor on the hinge side's first cell, and clear of items and lights, so the next candidate is taken (the hinge side only from two cells along). A second guard stands likewise at another outward opening of the claim three cells or more from the first, else further along the main door's wall; a guard with no such cell gathers with the rest, and a claim left with no one at all puts its first member on any free floor cell (measured over 64 seeds on levels 1 and 2: 323 of 336 guarded claims have a guard at the main door and 13 of 351 guards no post). Guards are placed first and the rest of the camp gathers round them; a guard's post is the one place a folk may stand by a doorway, or by a torch or a floor item. Traders gather in the market, and in the claim's other rooms if the market is too cramped. A guard keeps the post through `folk_routine::keeps_post`: it idles there facing out, turns to watch the delver who comes near like any folk (the band logic of `folk_regard` still decides whether they greet, watch or turn away), walks back to the post after a hold, a shove or a fight, and never chooses a place to stroll to. The talk panel's guard names a trader of its faction and where to find them from the hall door (`talk::lines::place_of`: "in the market, past the hall", "by the hearth", "where we sleep"). Because the market now follows traders and not the faction's intention, more camps keep one, and a market in a small hall may have no wall left for stalls. The folk card shows the trade under the name line ("Apprentice weaponsmith (blades)", "Master alchemist", "Guard"), and plain folk show nothing extra. `FB_SPAWN=folk:<faction>:<trade>` stands a folk with a trade (`weaponsmith`, `master-alchemist`, `guard`). `cargo run -p fb_level --example trades -- <world seed>` prints the master placements and the trade of every folk on levels 1 to 8.
- Folk can be struck (`game/src/folk_combat.rs`). A folk's abilities are their folk's scores in `data/races.json` and their class is their outfit's; hit points are a 1st-level character's as the delver's are (the class's hit die at its highest plus the Constitution modifier, SRD 5.1 "Hit Points at 1st Level"), and a scavenger or someone freshly fallen, who has no class, has a commoner's 1d8 at its average, 4 (SRD 5.1 "Commoner"), plus the modifier. Armor Class is the delver's sum over everything they wear and hold. A swing finds a folk at the circle they stand in and rolls to hit first, as every attack does (see "Attack rolls" under Creatures). A blow that lands flashes them, knocks them back as a Medium creature and thuds; at 0 they fall onto their back about their feet, the standing figure turned as a whole (our own simplification, no ragdoll), and "Name is slain" shows. The body stops its routine, turns its head no more, blocks nobody and shows no card.
- Folk fight back once given cause (`game/src/hostility.rs`, our own rules). Every folk starts friendly (`Hostility`), and nothing a friendly folk does hurts the delver, however near they come. Cause is a `Provoked` message any system may send: a swing aimed at a folk sends one whether or not it lands (`combat::swing::SwungAt`), and catching the delver stealing or a talk gone wrong can send one later. A provoked folk turns hostile together with the kin who see it: the folk of the same faction in the same room (within 8 cells when out in a corridor) with a clear line to them (`hostility::who_turns`); other factions stay friendly, and "The Ash Choir turn on you" shows. Hostile stays hostile while the delver stays in sight, and the world record keeps each folk's hit points, place and hostility (`record::FolkRecord`). A hostile folk who goes 25 s without sight of the delver (`folk.lose_interest_after` in `tuning.toml`, our own) loses interest: they turn friendly again and walk back to their spot to take up their routine (`Routine::head_home`), and striking them again provokes them again. Creatures already give up a chase sooner (3 s once at the place they last saw the delver, 9 s at most, `creatures::behaviour::LOSE_AFTER`), and go back to wandering their room. A hostile folk leaves their routine and comes at the delver as a creature does (straight in sight, else along `find_path`, sliding round everything), at twice their walking pace (the SRD's Dash, p. 93), facing them, and in reach swings their main-hand item with the delver's own `Weapon` rules (`folk_combat::Combatant`: an arm to the wrist of their folk's height plus the item's length, a fist an arm's length, a torch an improvised 1d4), a creature's swing time for the weapon (0.9 s for a dagger to 1.5 s for a mace) with 0.8 s between swings. The blow lands as the arm comes down (the main arm drawn up over the shoulder and brought down, `folk_pose::swing_turn`) if the delver is still in reach and in sight, and rolls to hit against the delver's Armor Class; a hit does the weapon's dice plus the Strength modifier, sent as an `AttackOnDelver` (its species is `None` for a folk), which takes the delver's hit points as any creature's blow does, and a miss whooshes past.
- Standing with factions (#542, `crates/fb_faction/src/standing.rs`): `Standing` is a number from -100 to +100, read as a `Band`: Hostile (-100 to -50), Wary (-49 to -10), Neutral (-9 to +9), Friendly (+10 to +49), Allied (+50 to +100). The edges, and the starts below, are fields of `StandingRules` (its `Default` holds the decided values) so a lab can tune them. `Standings` maps each faction of the world's pool to a standing: `start_from_alignment(delver_alignment, &World)` begins each at +10 for the same alignment, falling evenly with the distance across the alignment grid (one notch on each axis) to -30 at the opposite corner, so +10, 0, -10, -20, -30 at distance 0 to 4. `shift(faction, amount)` moves it, clamped; `turn_hostile` drops it to the top of Hostile (-50) if it was higher. It also notes which factions the delver has met. The game holds `Standings` in `WorldRecord::standings`, so every camp of a faction on every level reads the same standing, and a new delve builds a new one (`WorldRecord::new_delve`). A folk turning hostile (`hostility.rs`) drops their faction to Hostile, so the two agree; talk, barter and claim doors read the bands (see their bullets below). The folk card's second line carries the band ("Dwarf of the Ash Choir · Wary"), looking at a folk marks their faction met, and the character sheet's factions section lists the factions met with their band. On sight (#552, `game/src/folk_regard.rs`) the band decides what folk do: Hostile folk who see the delver attack, provoked or not, and a folk of a Hostile faction never calms down, so the `Hostility` flag and the band agree (a faction whose standing has risen again lets its folk calm after 25 s as before); Wary folk stop their routine and watch the delver while they stand in a room the faction holds and are seen, carrying on when they leave sight or the claim; Neutral folk ignore the delver; Friendly folk within 4 cells and in sight greet them once ("Bera nods to you.", a short hold and turn) and not again for 90 s; Allied folk greet as Friendly and also fight the creature that last struck the delver, if it is within 10 cells of them and they see the delver, closing in at a dash and swinging their main hand (our own smallest version: the blow rolls to hit against the creature's Armor Class as the delver's does, and a kill an ally makes earns the delver no standing and no heal-on-kill). Sight is a clear line within 12 cells. Standing moves by what the delver does (#547, all our own numbers, fields of `StandingRules` for a lab to tune, #558). A kill is weighed by every faction of the world, not only those on the level (`game/src/kills.rs` reads `Slain`; `Standings::on_kill`): a faction that hates what was killed (a creature kind, or a folk's folk, faction or class) rises, one that likes it falls, by `kill_amount` 2.2 for the first kill of that thing and `kill_decay` 0.9 times the one before for each after, counted in whole points of the running total per faction and thing. Kills of one kind therefore add up to at most about 22: 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 10, 20, 100 kills are worth 2, 4, 5, 7, 9, 10, 14, 19, 21, so six spider kills take a spider-hating faction from Neutral (0) to Friendly (10), and a hundred rat kills are worth 21 rather than 200. A faction that both likes and hates a victim ignores it. Killing a member of a faction costs that faction `member_kill` -40, not diminished; the blow that killed them also fired `Provoked`, so the faction has taken the -20 blow too (-60 in all, and Hostile). A faction counts a victim once for a kill even if it hates both the victim's faction and folk (the first match counts). Every faction whose standing a kill or dealing moves is marked met, so the sheet lists it. Striking a member costs `blow` -20 and then drops the faction to Hostile if it was higher (`on_blow`, called when a faction turns). A trade is +2, a gift +5 and a theft -15 (`on_trade`, `on_gift`, `on_theft`, for the barter and steal work to call). At the start of a delve a faction that likes the delver's class or folk starts 8 higher for each, and one that hates it 8 lower (`Standings::start_as`, folded into the start; the delver's class and folk do not change). `FB_KILLS=spider:6` (dev) applies kills at the start of a delve.
- Talk (#350, `game/src/talk/`): the Talk key (Primary, F) on a folk in reach reads their faction's standing band. A Hostile faction, or a folk who has turned on the delver, spits ("Hilda Stonefoot spits at your feet.") and no panel opens; a Wary folk says one line in a toast and goes on; Neutral and better hold the folk's routine (they stop and face the delver, `Routine::hold`) and open a dialogue panel (`Screen::Talk`, a menu-style context) on the right of the view, in the ledger's look, scaled with the window. It shows their name, folk and trade with tier, faction and band, a greeting (a separate rule set for Friendly and Allied, warmer than Neutral) and the topics, chosen by number key or click (hit-tested with `ui_cursor`). Topics: Who are you? (folk, faction and trade, coloured by the faction's intention), Your trade (for folk with one: a line by profession, the smith's style and the tier; a guard instead speaks of the faction's ground and names a trader of its faction on the level, if there is one), Commission (smiths, bowyers and tentmakers) or Enchant and Scrap (enchanters), This level (the factions that hold ground on it), and The way out (a rumour; some folk, more often those with a trade, instead name a real master deeper down, their trade, faction and how many levels below, from `World::masters` and `trades::master_faction`). Commission, Enchant and Scrap answer "Not yet." until their screens exist: `talk::service_ready` says when one does, and choosing it then sends a `ServiceChosen` message for that screen to open on. A need topic waits for quests. Lines are tracery grammars in `data/talk.json` expanded with `procgen_grammar`, seeded by the folk's own seed and the kind of line, so the same folk always say the same thing; a speaker's name, folk, faction, trade and the level's facts are laid over the grammar as one-rule symbols. The Talk key again or Esc leaves, and the routine is released however the panel closes; a folk who falls or turns on the delver ends the talk. A Wary folk keeps walking, which reads as turning away. `FB_TALK=1` opens the panel on the nearest folk 40 frames in (`FB_TALK_TOPIC=<n>` then chooses the nth topic) for captures.
- Barter (#351, `game/src/barter.rs`, rules in `data/barter_rules.json`, all our own numbers): the Barter key (Secondary, G) on a folk in reach, or the dialogue's Barter topic. A folk of a Hostile or Wary faction, or one who has turned on the delver, refuses with a toast ("The Mudfoot clan won't trade with you."). Plain folk, guards and enchanters have no shelf: "Hilda Stonefoot has nothing to sell." A trader of Neutral or better standing opens the inventory screen with their shelf as a grid of its own (a container the screen holds only while bartering, shown through `OpenContainer`) beside the delver's pack, and a balance under it. Dragging a ware from the shelf into the pack, or a thing from the pack onto the shelf, builds the deal: what left the shelf is bought, what was put on it is sold; the balance reads who owes whom in gp, sp and cp, and each item's card says what it costs or what the trader pays. Things lying about, the doll's occupied slots and the floor are out of play until it is settled, and a double-click moves a ware to the pack or the delver's own thing to the shelf. Accept settles the deal all or nothing: the delver pays from their carried purses, first ones first, or is paid into one (a new purse if they carry none); a deal the delver cannot cover, the trader cannot cover, or the pack has no room for the change of, moves nothing. Cancel, Esc or closing the screen puts everything back as it was. Coins are always compared by `Coins::total_copper`, since `Coins` compares field by field. Prices: a ware costs its value times the tier's markup (novice 1.0, apprentice 1.1, expert 1.25, master 1.5) times the band's (Neutral 1.3, Friendly 1.0, Allied 0.85); the trader pays 0.6 of an item's value for goods of their line of work and half of that for anything else, times the band's (Neutral 0.8, Friendly 1.0, Allied 1.15), so buying and selling the same thing never turns a profit. Each trader has a purse by tier (novice 30 gp, apprentice 60, expert 150, master 400) kept on their shelf (`Shelf::purse`, in the level record), which pays for what they buy and fills with what they are paid, so they can run out; a restock tops it up to the tier's, never cuts it. What the delver sells stays on the shelf (`Shelf::sold`) through every restock, and the shelf, sold-in wares and purse included, is kept in the level record so a revisit shows it. A settled trade calls `Standings::on_trade_with` (`StandingRules::trade`, +2) and marks the faction met, but only the first with each folk on a game day (`Barter::trade_standing`), so trading small things back and forth cannot farm standing. The shelf grid scrolls inside its own area (`ShelfScroll`, at most 500 interface px tall) with the balance and its buttons pinned above it; Cancel puts back only the items the deal moved, and leaving the delve ends a barter in progress. `FB_BARTER=1` (dev) presses Barter on the nearest folk, `deal` also starts a deal and `accept` settles it, for captures.
- Claim doors (#553, `game/src/doors.rs`, `fb_level::Level::claim_doors`): each door cell on the edge of a claim, one of its rooms opening onto ground the claim does not hold, is hung with a swinging leaf or a rising grate (by its kind, below) that stops the delver, creatures and other factions' folk while shut, and not the door's own folk (the leaf swings open for one who walks up to it); leaving a level records its doors before the next level's are hung (`LeavingLevel` runs before the swap that resets them). F opens and closes it. The claim's main door is the one of its hall nearest the level's start. Whether it is locked is read live from the delver's band with the faction: Hostile and Wary lock every claim door; Neutral leaves the main door unlocked and locks the rest; Friendly and Allied lock none, and an Allied faction gives the delver "Key of <faction>" once (`WorldRecord::keys_given`, a toast, a `Key` good rolled from the Armory's Keys sundry and seeded by the faction; never found loose). An unlocked door is still shut until F opens it. A locked door shows "Locked" over the crosshair with "G Unlock with key" when the delver carries the faction's key and "R Pick the lock" when they carry lockpicks. G unlocks with the key; R held for 1.5 s picks: d20 plus the Dexterity modifier, the lockpicking of worn gear and 1 for the picks against 12 (Neutral), 15 (Wary) or 18 (Hostile). Every try costs the 1.5 s hold, and a miss costs nothing more unless it is heard: sound carries through a shut door, so a folk of the faction within 8 cells hears it whatever lies between (distance only, no line), the faction's standing drops by 6 ("The Ash Choir heard the picks") and the bands do the rest; unheard it is only "The picks slip", and the delver may try again. A shut door is a wall to everyone but the folk of its own faction, creatures included (a creature chasing the delver stops at it), and it is opaque to sight for all but them, so no one sees or notices the delver through a shut leaf (`doors::solid_for`, read by walking, pathing and sight alike). Key or picks unlock the door for good. All of these numbers are our own. Each door's open and unlocked state is kept per level in `LevelRecord::doors`. Banners and guards at the hall door are other work; the door is the only new thing on its cell.
- Claim door kinds (#568, `fb_level::doorkind`, `game/src/level/geometry/door.rs`): each claim door is wood, steel or a portcullis (`ClaimDoor::kind`), and has a switch on its claim's side, a lever or a chain (`ClaimDoor::switch`). Both are plain data rolled in `Level::claim_doors` from the level seed on a stream of their own, salted by the door's cell, so the same seed gives the same doors and nothing else moves. The weights are our own: rough rock leans to wood and timber grates (60 wood, 10 steel, 30 grate in a hundred before the faction), stone halls to steel and iron grates (20, 40, 40); a way-out or plunder faction multiplies steel by 2.5, the grate by 1.5 and wood by 0.4, a trade or worship one the reverse (wood 2.5, steel 0.4, grate 0.67); a heavy armour lean (mail, scale, plate) multiplies steel by 1.5 and wood by 0.67, a light one (padded, leather) the reverse; every kind stays possible. The claim's main door is rolled like any other, so it is not necessarily the strongest. Over 288 levels the shares (percent wood, steel, grate) were 56, 15, 30 in rough rock and 31, 43, 27 in the halls, and 72, 11, 17 for trade and worship factions against 22, 38, 41 for way-out and plunder ones. The switch suits the kind: a portcullis gets a chain 85 times in a hundred, a steel door 25, a wooden one 20, a lever otherwise. Each leaf is built to its doorway's real opening (`Opening::of(DoorStyle)`: a timber frame is square, 13 by 22 with a sill; the round and rock-cut arches rise to 22.5 over straight sides to 16; the pointed arch to 26 over sides to 14.7), cut to the head with a small clearance: wood is boards cut to the head under iron straps, steel a riveted plate 1.1 thick with flat bands, ribs, heavy strap hinges, a lock box and a pull bar, and a swinging leaf hangs in the reveal on a pin set 0.8 off the hinge jamb, its hinge edge a round post so it never meets the jamb, with pintles let into the jamb; a portcullis is upright bars with pointed feet and cross rails cut to the head, sliding in iron guides, rising 23 to 27 units into the lintel (timber beams shod with iron in rough rock, iron in the halls). A steel door's pick roll is 4 harder (`DoorKind::pick_penalty`), a portcullis cannot be picked ("The grate can't be picked", and the prompt offers no R). A portcullis stays down for anyone but its faction's folk until it is raised: from outside by G with the faction's key at the gate post (or F where the standing table leaves the door unlocked), from inside only by its switch; a shut grate stops the body (`doors::solid_for`) but not the eye (`doors::opaque_for`, read by every line of sight the folk and creatures make to notice or turn on the delver), so a guard hall shows its guard through the grate. Leaves move at their own pace (0.6 s wood, 0.84 s steel, 1.6 s grate). Switches are small: a lever is a 1.4 by 3 plate with a short vertical slot the handle comes out of, thrown up and down (about 38 degrees either way) as the door opens and shuts; a chain hangs from a 1.3 by 1.9 housing with a ring at its foot and is drawn down 1.6 units and springs back as the door moves. Each is laid on the side wall of the doorway's cell beside the door on the claim's side (5.5 off the door's plane, the lever at 11 above the floor and the chain's housing at 14.5, so the ring hangs at hand height), on the drawn wall (the rock's relief moves it and tilts it; dressed stone stands 0.5 proud) and always on the side away from the hinge, so the leaf's sweep never reaches it. Sounds: the game has no door sounds beyond the entrance door's slam, so none are added per kind yet. `--doors` shows a door and its switch in a doorway frame with sliders for the kind's parameters (`game/src/door_view.rs`; `--doors --kind portcullis --doorway pointed_arch --level stone_halls --switch chain`). The record keeps each door's open and unlocked state as before; the kind and switch follow from the seed.
- A slain folk leaves everything they wore and held where they fell (`belongings::drop_belongings`, the one system every slain character drops through): as they start to fall, each item is put into the inventory as a loose item and set down round the body (`inventory::underfoot::set_down_around`, the ring a skeleton's gear is set down in, now shared), as the real item with its rarity, affixes and wear, ready to pick up and wear. The body is drawn again bare and marked `Looted` at once, so the floor changes are in before the record could read the floor and nothing is set down twice; the record keeps the body (`FolkRecord::dead`), and a level laid out again brings it back bare and lying where it fell, with no routine and nothing to drop.

## Names from a grammar

Our own, from the faction design (#314). `fb_faction` names each generated faction with it.

- `procgen_grammar` expands tracery-style grammars with no Bevy: a grammar maps a symbol to rules, `#symbol#` expands recursively, `#symbol.capitalize.s#` applies modifiers (`capitalize`, `upper`, `lower`, `s` for plurals, `a` for articles), and `##` is a literal `#`. Choices come from a `procgen_rng::Mulberry32` stream, so `expand(&grammar, "#origin#", seed)` is a pure function of the seed. Nesting past 24 levels or 10,000 expansions is an error, and a missing symbol, an empty symbol or an unknown modifier is an error, never shown text.
- `data/faction_names.json` holds the faction name rules. Each folk (human, dwarf, gnome, goblin, lizardfolk, pigfolk) defines the same word lists (`name`, `adj`, `thing`, `place`, `brothers`, `watchers`, `court`, `choir`, `group`, `raiders`, `traders`, `guild`, `company`, `goods`, `folkname`); the denizen kinds (minotaur, cyclops, faun, demon) are keyed the same way beside them; each intention (`way_out`, `trade`, `worship`, `plunder`) defines `origin` rules over them. A name is the folk's lists laid under the intention's `origin`, so a goblin plunder band is "the Mudfoot clan" and a pigfolk trade house "the Hogsmeet traders". The seven names in `data/factions.json` are all reachable.
- `faction_name(folk_key, intention_key, seed)` returns the name. The keys are mixed into the seed so two factions with one seed do not pick alike.
- Preview with `cargo run -p procgen_grammar --example names [first_seed] [count]`, which prints every folk and intention pair. A test fails if any rule names a symbol the folk lacks.

## Props in the level

Our own proposal; neither the seed doc nor the plate furnishes a level.

- Each level stands containers and scenery along the walls of its rooms (`fb_level::PropSpot`): Armory chests, barrels, crates and amphorae (`Family::Chests` styles, each with its own seed and a loot seed for what it holds), and rubble, bones and pillars (`armory::build::scenery`). Strongboxes and coffers stand among them now and then. No container lies about as loose loot: the floor's loot table (`fb_level::generate::LOOT`) has no chests.
- Containers are drawn 1.6 times their true Armory size (`fb_level::CONTAINER_SCALE`, the game's `container_scale` tuning), so a chest is about a metre wide rather than the prototype's 60 cm; the user's call, since at true size they read as small from standing height. Their floor shapes, the room a chest's lid needs behind it and their look bounds grow with them. Placement keeps to the constant, not the tuning, so a seed lays out the same level whatever the tuning file says; a square container is also set off its walls by as far as its small turn (`CONTAINER_TURN`) swings its corners. A tuned scale well above 1.6 can push a container into the rock.
- Props come from a fourth stream of the level seed, after the layout, the loot and the folk, so they never move a wall, an item, a light or anyone standing about.
- A room holds about one prop per nine cells of floor, at most five, and one room in seven stands bare. Each stands with its back to a wall, or in a corner against two, far enough off the grid's wall face for the rock's relief and, for a chest, for its lid to swing open. Pillars stand only in corners. The first container in every level is a chest, and every level has at least one container.
- None stands in or beside a doorway, in a corridor, on the straight way in from the entrance, or near an item, a light or a folk. Treating every cell with a prop as rock, every other open cell is still reachable from the start, and every cell with a prop borders one that is, so a prop against its wall never closes the way.
- In the game (`game/src/props.rs`) a container is its Armory item, shut, drawn in its own palette, condition and etching like any item; scenery is drawn as the level's fixtures are. Scenery is a set of builders in `armory::build::scenery` rather than an Armory family, since it is never an item: no state, noun, turntable page or loot entry. Rubble is a heap settled in layers of roughened, faceted stone chunks with now and then a slab of fallen ceiling; bones are a skull with its eye sockets and nose pressed in (`geom::carve_hollows`), long bones turned on a lathe and curved ribs; a pillar is a faceted column with a slight swell on a stepped plinth, either whole with its capital bedded in the ceiling's relief or broken off short with a ragged top and chips of it lying on the plinth.
- Each prop rests at the lowest point of the drawn floor under it and blocks the way (`play::BlocksTheWay`, gathered into `play::Obstacles` with the folk): a circle for round things, a rectangle turned with it for chests, crates and pillars. Things set down from the inventory stop short of props as they do of rock.
- Looking at a container within reach and pressing the pick-up key opens it rather than picking it up: the inventory opens with the container's grid ahead of what lies underfoot, and a chest stands open in the level from then on, its lid raised (the Armory's `chest.open`). Every container is in the `Inventory` from the start, loose, filled from its loot seed, so what is left in it stays for the rest of the run (`docs/items.md`, "Containers").
- `print_level` marks containers `c` chest, `b` barrel, `x` crate, `a` amphora, `s` strongbox and `f` coffer, and scenery `%` rubble, `~` bones and `o` pillar.

## Creatures

Rats and snakes, something simple to fight. Their shapes, sizes, colourings, numbers and behaviour are our own proposal; their combat numbers are placeholders from the [D&D 5e SRD 5.1](https://media.wizards.com/2023/downloads/dnd/SRD_CC_v5.1.pdf) (CC-BY-4.0) until the character simulation is designed.

- `fb_creatures` (plain Rust) rolls a typed state from a seed: `rat(seed)` gives a rat or a giant rat (one in five), `snake(seed)` a poisonous snake or a constrictor (one in three), and `Creature::of(species, seed)` one of a chosen species. A rat has a length, build, tail and ear size, whisker length, a colouring (agouti, black, blue, fawn, albino) and a pattern (plain, Berkshire, hooded, variegated), named after the American Fancy Rat and Mouse Association's [standards](https://www.afrma.org/rstndrd.htm). A snake has a length, girth, head width, a colouring (earth, ash, moss, coral, sand, night), markings (diamonds, saddles, bands, a stripe or none) and the hand it coils to.
- Each state builds a `Rig`: named bones, each carrying its own `armory::ItemMesh` built from Armory geometry in the bone's space. A rat is a body, a head, four legs of three bones (upper leg, shin, paw) and a four-bone tail; a snake is a head with a jaw and a forked tongue, and 22 spine segments. Bones overlap where they meet (a snake's segments are rounded past their joints), so the body stays whole as it bends without skinning. Long parts run along their bone's +Y, so the Armory's wrapped patterns run round them.
- Rats have fur: the body's colour with tufts standing out along the back and flanks, combed back. Ears are cupped, thin and bare; paws are bare with toes and claws; the tail is bare and scaled; whiskers fan from the muzzle; the incisors are orange, as rats' enamel is. Snakes carry the Armory's `Scales` pattern; their markings are patches laid over the skin with three unused anchor vertices, so the pattern's wrap (measured from a part's vertices) matches the skin's and the scales run on across a marking. The belly has broad scutes. A viper has a broad head, brow scales, heat pits and fangs; a constrictor a narrower head and rows of small teeth; both have slit-pupilled eyes and a pale mouth lining that shows when the jaw drops. Every eye has a glint that glows a little, so eyes catch torchlight.
- Sizes depart from the SRD's (p. 92), by our own choice, so rats and snakes read as a threat across a room: each is drawn a size up from its stat block (`drawn_size`), and only the drawing changes; hit points and bites stay the SRD's. A rat (SRD Tiny) is 45 to 60 cm nose to rump, a giant rat (SRD Small) 1 to 1.5 m with its back at a delver's shin or knee, a poisonous snake (SRD Tiny, a short coil on the floor) 1.5 to 2.5 m and thick as a forearm, a constrictor (SRD Large) 4 to 6 m and thick as a thigh. A rat's body fits its drawn size's space and, tail and all, overfills the size below; a snake coiled fits its drawn space. Every creature's collision circle stays under half a cell, so the biggest still passes a one-cell doorway or corridor.
- `fb_creatures::pose` turns a motion into bone placements, so the rules are tested without Bevy. A rat trots, diagonal legs together, each paw planted flat on the floor through its stance and lifted through its swing, the leg bent to reach it by a two-bone solve; its body bobs, its tail swings in a wave and drags on the floor rather than passing through it, its head sniffs, turns and lunges to bite. A snake's spine is laid along a curve given by its heading along its length: a serpenoid curve (heading a sine of the distance along the body, after Shigeo Hirose's *Biologically Inspired Robots*, 1993) travelling back down the body to slither, an Archimedean spiral to coil, the front raised in an S to rear, which a strike throws straight. Rearing, the neck climbs in a column no steeper than about 72° (so the body keeps its length) and the head is held level over it, at least at a human's knee (0.285 of stature, Drillis and Contini's proportions as tabled in Winter's *Biomechanics and Motor Control of Human Movement*). Coiling and uncoiling slide each point of the body from where it lay to where it is going, rather than blending headings, which swept the body round through walls. Laid out, the neck leads from the creature's root and the body follows the trail the head left (`Trail`, a `SnakePose::bend` along the body), so a 5 m constrictor turning a corner keeps to the floor it crossed; coiled, the coil's middle is on the root. A snake's collision circle is its coil, capped below half a cell. The belly rests on the floor; the neck lifts to carry the deeper head, a straight segment on a bending line is lifted clear of the floor, and the jaw drops only as far as the floor lets it.
- Each species carries its SRD stat block's hit points, hit dice, bite damage, speed, senses and page (`Species::stat_block`): rat 1 (1d4 − 1) hit points and a 1-point bite (p. 387); giant rat 7 (2d6), bite 1d4 + 2 (p. 378), found only from the second level down, since a pack of them is too much for a fresh delver (`fb_level::GIANTS_FROM_DEPTH`, our own); poisonous snake 2 (1d4), bite 1 plus 2d4 poison halved on a DC 10 Constitution save (p. 386); constrictor 13 (2d10 + 2), bite 1d6 + 2 (p. 369; its Constrict is left for later). Speeds are the block's feet per 6-second round (p. 90).
- Skeletons (`fb_creatures::skeleton`, #288) are a human's bones, rolled from a seed (`SkeletonState`): stature (0.93 to 1.07 of a human), build, bone colour from bleached through yellowed to dark, cracks, pitting, and missing pieces (the jaw, ribs, fingers, teeth). The rig is 18 bones (pelvis, spine, ribcage, neck, skull, jaw, and an upper arm, forearm and hand, a thigh, shin and foot a side) built from the Armory's bone primitives (`armory::build::bone`: long bones with knobbed ends and condyles, vertebrae, ribs on a half ellipse, a skull carved with sockets and a nose with teeth, a separate jaw, a pelvis, hands and feet of separate bones) on the joints of the `fb_body` human the skeleton is measured on (`SkeletonDims::body`). Every bone's rest rotation is the identity and its origin is on its joint, so a pose is a turn about a joint in body axes; the hand and foot are built in `fb_body`'s frames (`SkeletonDims::hand_frame` is `Body::hand_placement`, a left hand's `x` unmirrored, and the mesh mirrored), so worn and held gear can be fitted to the bones as to a body (#289). Poses (`pose::skeleton`): standing, a walk whose planted feet rest on the floor exactly (each leg solved to its ankle; the pelvis rides at the height the lower reach allows, rounded where the weight changes legs; arms swing against the legs, the ribcage against the hips; a step is 0.7 of the leg), and a swing of the right arm (guard, wind-up over the shoulder, blow, follow-through) with both feet planted and the weight shifting. Its stat block is the SRD's skeleton (p. 346): Medium undead, Armor Class 13 (armor scraps), 13 (2d8 + 4) hit points, speed 30 ft., 10, 14, 15, 6, 8, 5, vulnerable to bludgeoning, immune to poison, exhaustion and being poisoned, darkvision 60 ft., a shortsword (+4, 1d6 + 2 piercing) and a shortbow (+4, 1d6 + 2, 80/320 ft.); the stat blocks gained Armor Class, ability scores, damage and condition immunities, a ranged attack, challenge and XP. `--skeleton` opens the creature view on a skeleton (Left and Right roll seeds, M stands, walks and swings it). Bone colours, the skull's and pelvis's shapes and every amplitude are our own.
- Each level holds one to three packs of two to five rats, each pack one species (one in four giant rats) round a point in a room of its own, and up to two snakes (one in three a constrictor), each alone in a room no folk stand in (`fb_level::CreatureSpot`, with the pack it belongs to). None is in the starting room, a corridor, a doorway or beside one, near an item, a light or a folk, or against a prop or another creature. They come from a fifth stream of the level seed, after the props, so they never move a wall, an item, a light, a folk or a prop. `print_level` marks them `r` rat, `R` giant rat, `v` poisonous snake and `S` constrictor.
- In the game (`game/src/creatures.rs`) each creature is a root on the drawn floor with one entity per bone carrying that bone's Armory parts, drawn by the `ItemSpawner` in the house style (toon bands, ink, wear; skin and fur with a little grime). Each frame the root's movement (pace and the distance walked, which advances the step cycle or the wave so feet and body do not slide) and what the creature means to do (`Intent`: coil, rear, strike, jaw, tongue, a head turn, eased at their own rates) become a pose that is copied onto the bones. Snake segments and the head rest on the floor's relief under each. Standing about, rats sniff and sway their tails; snakes lie coiled, raising their heads now and then and flicking their tongues every three seconds or so.
- Skeleton gear (`fb_items::undead::skeleton_outfit`, `fb_creatures::gear`, #289). A skeleton rolls its gear from its seed through `roll_item` like loot, so every piece has a rarity, affixes and a history, and then wears it hard (wear 0.55 to 0.95, iron rusted, cloth and leather rotted, edges nicked). Two in ten are kitted (helm, body armour, gloves or gauntlets, boots, a sword, axe or mace and nearly always a shield); four in ten are part dressed, each piece by chance, with a tattered tunic where there is no armour; four in ten are bare-boned, usually with a weapon (a spear, bow or club too) and sometimes nothing at all. Worn gear is fitted by `fb_body::fit_worn` to the human the bones are laid on, as to any folk, so it keeps the shape of the man who wore it and hangs off the ribs and limbs; held gear by `fb_body::fit_hands`. The rig is rigid, so a fitted mesh is cut by triangle and each piece rides the bone it lies nearest (headgear the skull; a torso garment the ribcage, its sleeves cut off along the arms below the ball of the shoulder; gloves the hands; a boot's foot the foot and its shaft the shin; held gear the hand), stored in its bone's own space, much as plate armour is jointed. Gloves and boots replace the bare bony hand and foot. An arm that holds something is posed by the fitting (the forearm raised to a level grip), where the rig's arms hang at rest, so its `ArmStance` is the turn of upper arm, forearm and hand that brings the bones to the fitted arm and the gear is stored for the bones in that stance (`pose::skeleton_armed`; a holding arm does not swing as it walks, the right arm's blow still drives its upper arm and forearm and the left arm braces its shield). The bony hand of an armed arm is closed into the grip `Body::grip` assumes (`armory::build::bone::hand_gripping` lays the fingers along `fb_body`'s digit joints at the curl a gripping hand takes), so a held weapon sits in the fingers. Tests across 200 seeds (the sweeps are `#[ignore]`d with a default-run `_smoke` twin): the same seed gives the same gear; no more than 3% of a piece's vertices come inside a long bone's shaft, the spine or the skull (a sleeve's collapsed root lies on the arm's axis), none is further than 3.2 units from a bone, and a held weapon passes within 0.3 of the fist's grip.
- Skeletons in the game (`game/src/creatures/skeletons.rs`, #290). A skeleton is drawn in its fitted gear (`SkeletonGear::roll`, each piece a child of the bone it rides) and posed with its stance. Everything fixed about it is worked out once at spawn into a `SkeletonKit` on its `Critter` (the bones' measures and height, the weapon's reach, dice and swing time, Armor Class, the shield's chance to block), so no frame rebuilds a body. Its weapon sets what it does: a blow reaches an arm to the wrist and the weapon's length beyond it, as the delver's does (`combat::Weapon::in_hand`), so a spear outreaches a sword and a sword a dagger; it rolls the weapon's dice and damage bonus; a swing takes 0.9 s with a dagger to 1.5 s with a mace and lands as the arm comes down (0.62 of it), if the delver is still within a seventh again of its reach; with nothing in hand it claws for 1d3 at an arm's length, and a bow is swung as an improvised 1d4 (nothing shoots yet). Armor Class is the delver's sum (10, the stat block's Dexterity 14 modifier, each item's armour) when it wears anything, else the stat block's 13. The delver's swings roll against it, and its own blows add its weapon's ToHit to the stat block's +4. A shield turns a blow from within 60 degrees of its front with a chance of 0.2 plus 0.08 for each point of the shield's armour, at most 0.65 (our own); bludgeoning (a mace, club, staff or fist: `Weapon::bludgeoning`) does it double damage and nothing is poison. Struck down it falls to pieces where it stood: every joint gives at once and each bone drops and tumbles to lie flat in a heap round its feet, the feet and shins landing first and the skull last (a bone falls for as long as it was high), the jaw knocked loose and the skull rolled off to one side (`pose::skeleton_heap`; the body's root stays standing and the heap is all in the bones, rolled from the skeleton's seed so a body brought back from the record lies in the same heap). As it starts to fall, every item it wore and held is put into the inventory as loose items and set on the floor round it (`FloorChange::SetDownAt`), as real items with the rarity, affixes and wear they had, once: the body is marked `Looted` as it dies and the drop fires at once, so a body put back later does not drop its gear again. It never uses a rat's squeak, squeal and stride or a snake's hiss and coil: its steps (one a stride, by its leg), rattle, clack when struck, clang on a shield and fall in a heap are `tools/synth_bones.py`'s. `FB_SPAWN=skeleton:<seed>[,<seed>...]` stands skeletons in front of the level's start, for captures. The first stone halls (depth 2) are eased for a fresh delver (#354, our own): their dead wear no armour, only rags, so their Armor Class is the stat block's 13, and hold a dagger or a club (1d4) or nothing but their 1d3 claws (`fb_items::undead::FIRST_HALLS`), and no more than two stand in a hall (`fb_level::creatures::FIRST_HALLS_GUARDS`); deeper halls keep the full table. Measured over 399 fresh delvers (8.3 hit points on average, the SRD's first-level hit points) and the guards of real levels, a depth-2 guard downs one on its 4.2nd landed blow on average (3.0 before) and the delver hits it 51% of the time with a plain weapon (40% before); `against_a_fresh_delver::report_the_stone_halls` prints the table. Each blow is logged by the weapon ("Skeleton slashes" with a sword, "strikes" with a mace or club, "stabs" with a dagger or spear, "hacks" with an axe, "claws" bare), and every swing's rattle and drawn-back arm come at least 0.5 s before the blow lands (`skeletons::MIN_WIND_UP`).
- Skeletons in the levels (#291). Only levels of stone halls have skeletons, placed after every rat and snake so the rough rock levels draw nothing new from the creature stream and keep their creatures. Each colonnaded hall (the three largest) has one to three guards standing in the gaps between its columns, facing the aisle, one pack to a hall; one or two side rooms (rooms with no columns, none a snake's or the starting room) hold a skeleton, or now and then a pair, facing the nearest door. Never on the two cells straight in from a door or by one, nor on a column, a stair or where a delver arrives. A level always has one when it has a room for it. A skeleton restored dead from the world record lies as a bare corpse marked `Looted`, its worn gear not drawn; one killed sheds its gear at once (as the item floor changes the record reads) so leaving right after still records it, once; a living one comes back in the gear its seed rolls, with its recorded hit points and place. A creature's blow lands only if the delver is in sight at the moment it lands, never round a wall. To hold the frame rate: a level's skeletons are rolled and fitted side by side on threads before any is spawned (about 2.3 s down to 0.4 s for five), the thin bones of hands and feet are drawn with a coarser lathe (43.6k bone vertices down to 34.9k), and a skeleton more than 2 m past the fog's end (`fog_end`) is not drawn.
- `--creature` shows one creature on the turntable's plate under the studio light, still, moving in place, rearing or striking, with its SRD hit points and bite (a skeleton stands, walks and swings; `--skeleton` opens the view on one).
- Creatures act for themselves (`game/src/creatures/behaviour.rs`). They wander their home room, idling between walks (a snake mostly lies coiled and seldom moves far from its spot). They notice the player within their sense range (the stat block's darkvision or blindsight, at least 20 ft, since the player carries a light) when the straight line between them crosses no rock (`fb_level::path::line_of_sight`, Amanatides and Woo's grid traversal). In sight they head straight in; out of sight they follow the shortest way round the rock to where they last saw the player (`fb_level::path::find_path`, A* on the grid with the octile heuristic, cut short by `steer_toward`). Each step slides round rock, folk, props, the player and each other (`player::slide_around`), so they never pass through any of them. Rats run at twice their speed, as the SRD's Dash (p. 93) gives; snakes come on at their speed and stay within three cells of their spot. In reach they turn to the player and attack on a cooldown (a rat every 1.5 s, a snake every 2.5 s, where the SRD's round is 6 s): a rat lunges and bites, a snake draws its coils in and rears, then strikes with its jaw open. The bite sends an `AttackOnDelver` with the stat block's bite (and a poisonous snake's poison, before its save) rolled, which combat applies (below). Out of sight for 3 s once at the place they last saw the player, they go back to wandering their room.
- Combat (`game/src/combat/`) is a placeholder until the character simulation is designed (#122): one pool of hit points a side, with every number cited from the SRD 5.1 or labelled our own. Left click swings the main hand's item, each swing thrown as one of the strikes its kind suits, never the same one twice running while there is another (our own weights): maces, axes and clubs fall mostly overhead or down from a high corner, swords cut from every side, a staff swings flat across, spears and daggers stab, and bare hands punch (the eye leans into each blow). The swing heads for the creature the blow will land on, at the point of its body nearest the crosshair as the swing begins, carried to the same place on the screen in the view model's narrower field of view, or for the crosshair, 3 m ahead, with nothing in reach. As a strike is drawn back the wrist turns the item into the strike's plane, the plane through the fist's line to where it heads and the way the strike travels across the screen, with its edge (or an axe's bit) leading; it is then thrown in an arc through that plane, the fist swinging across with it, and followed through a little past. A stab turns the item's point (the end of it farthest from the fist) onto the line to where it heads as it draws back, then hooks in and drives along it, so a spear carried across the view and a dagger standing up in the fist both strike point first. Each swing also turns its strike's direction, wind-up and width a little of its own, so repeated blows rarely look alike. All of this is our own, tuned against captures; the browser prototypes had no swings. `--swings` is a practice mode for trying them. Partway through, the blow lands on the nearest living creature whose body is within the weapon's reach and within 50° of straight ahead. A body is where its bones are posed that moment: each bone's main surface gives it a capsule (`fb_creatures::Bone::hit`, fitted along the long side of its bounding box, our own fit), tested as a row of overlapping spheres, so a rat's head, legs and tail and every segment of a snake, laid out along the floor or reared, can be struck, not just a circle at the creature's root; the reach is an arm to the wrist (0.332 of stature, Drillis and Contini's proportions as tabled in Winter) plus the item's length, so a sword reaches about 1.5 m, near the SRD's 5 ft (p. 95). A swing that finds a creature rolls to hit against its Armor Class (see "Attack rolls" below) and on a hit does the weapon's dice from `fb_items` plus its damage bonus and the Strength modifier (p. 96), at least 1 (our own; the SRD allows 0); a bare fist does 1 + Strength (p. 95), anything that isn't a melee weapon 1d4 as an improvised weapon (p. 65). Rolls come from a run's own dice, seeded from the clock, so the world stays a function of its seed while fights differ. A struck creature flashes, is knocked back (less the bigger it is), and squeaks or hisses (synthesised by `tools/synth_combat.py`). The flash turns every part 65% of the way to the page's cream and adds glow, holds at full for 0.12 s and fades over 0.2 s more; glow alone multiplies a part's own colour and left a black rat dark (our own). A creature has its stat block's hit points; at 0 it stands flashing through that 0.12 s hold, so a blow that kills at once is still seen to land, then collapses, a rat rolling onto its back, a centipede curling into a flat spiral with its legs drawn in (`pose::centipede`'s `curl`, driven by `Intent::fallen`) and a snake going limp along the floor, and its body stays for the run. Bodies block nothing, since all lie flat enough to step over. The delver has a 1st-level character's hit points, the class's hit die at its highest plus the Constitution modifier (fighter and ranger 10, cleric 8, mage 6 as the SRD's wizard; pp. 24, 35, 15, 52), shown as "HP 8/12" in Plex Mono over a thin bar low on the left; each bite flashes the view's edge red, thuds, and draws a grunt of pain from the delver (`combat::voice`: one of three takes, never the same twice running and louder for a harder blow, or a hard cry for a blow of 30% of their hit points or more or one that brings them to 0; at most one per frame and none within 0.2 s of the last; the low or high voice by sex, played 0.88 to 1.15 times as fast by folk; synthesised by `tools/synth_grunt.py`). A poisonous snake's venom is halved on a d20 + Con save against DC 10 (p. 386). A failed save also leaves the delver Poisoned for a game hour (`fb_character::poison`), a departure from the SRD's snake, which does its venom once: the hour is our own, from the SRD's lasting poisons (1 minute for the quasit's same venom, p. 273, up to 24 hours; 1 hour the commonest, p. 204). While poisoned the life orb's liquid turns green and eases back to red as it wears off, and a Poisoned badge shows the minutes left. The SRD condition's disadvantage on attack rolls (p. 358) applies to every swing while it lasts, and the badge says "Attacking at disadvantage"; there are no ability checks yet. A constrictor's bite holds the delver at a quarter of their pace until they strike it, a placeholder for the SRD's Constrict and its restraining grapple (p. 370). At 0 hit points the delver has a last gasp of 3 s (`vitals::LAST_GASP`, our own) to drink a healing draught, from a quickslot or the inventory, while dark blood blooms in from the view's edge in ragged splotches and flung drops, creeping further in as the gasp runs out and blinking twice a second from full (`combat::blood`, `blood.wesl`; `vitals::gasp_blink`, under WCAG 2.3.1's three flashes a second); healed above 0 they stand on, and nothing strikes them meanwhile, but they cannot swing or take a stair. Otherwise the run ends: they can no longer swing, shoot or raise a shield, the view fades to "You fall in the abyss" and after 3.5 s the game returns to the title, clearing everything the delve spawned, the inventory screen's doll stage, hover cards and what lay underfoot among it; death saving throws are left out.
- Falling unconscious (`fb_character::revival`, `game/src/combat/revival.rs`; all our own, `[revival]` in `rules.toml`). When the last gasp runs out and the world still has a revive left (2 per world), the delver falls unconscious instead of falling in the abyss: the view darkens to "You fall unconscious" with Come to (Enter) and Let go (Backspace), and nothing happens until they choose. Letting go ends the run as above. Coming to leaves a wound that never heals, a permanent effect on one body part, never one they already have (`lame_leg` pace ×0.85, `ruined_eye` sight ×0.8, `cracked_ribs` sleepiness ×1.3, in `effects.json`). If friendly, living folk stand in a room of the level, the faction with the most of them carries the delver to its hall (the room most of them stand in, at the open cell nearest its centre a cell and a half clear of everyone), heals them in full and takes half the coin they carry, rounded up, out of their purses with change; with no friend they come to where they fell 4 game hours later, at a quarter of their hit points. Either way every creature hunting them goes home, and a card says what happened, the wound and the revives left. Exhaustion level 6 still ends the run outright. The world is made afresh with each run, so the count starts over with it for now; once worlds persist across deaths it belongs in the world's save.
- Recovering (`fb_character::recovery`). A wounded delver whose hunger and thirst are both in their best band (Fed and Quenched) and who is not poisoned regains a hit point every 10 game minutes (50 real seconds at the usual clock; `recovery.minutes_per_hit_point` in `tuning.toml`), never above their maximum, stepped by the game clock with the needs (`simulate::step_the_delver`). The time put toward the next point starts over whenever they stop recovering, so a meal does not bank time spent hungry. A green Recovering badge, on the HUD and in the sheet's conditions, says "+1 HP every 10 minutes while fed and watered". Our own: the SRD heals only by resting (SRD 5.1 p. 86, "Resting"), and there are no rests yet.
- Attack rolls (`fb_character::combat`). Every attack in the game rolls the same way, whoever makes it and whatever it is aimed at: a d20 plus the attacker's to-hit against the target's Armor Class, hitting when the total reaches it, a natural 20 always hitting and a natural 1 always missing ([SRD 5.1 p. 96](https://media.wizards.com/2023/downloads/dnd/SRD_CC_v5.1.pdf#page=96), "Attack Rolls"; `attack_roll_with`). The to-hit (`ToHit`) of the delver and of folk is the Strength modifier, the proficiency bonus of +2 (a 1st- to 4th-level character's, SRD 5.1 "Beyond 1st Level"; everyone counts as proficient with whatever they swing, our own simplification until classes carry weapon proficiencies; `PROFICIENCY_BONUS`) and the weapon's ToHit; a creature's is the attack bonus its stat block prints (rat +0, p. 387; giant rat +4, p. 378; poisonous snake +5, p. 386; constrictor snake +4, p. 369; skeleton +4, p. 346), plus a skeleton's weapon's ToHit. Armor Class (`combat::ArmourClass`) is on everything that fights: a rat's, a snake's and a bare skeleton's is its stat block's (rat 10, giant rat 12, poisonous snake 13, constrictor snake 12, skeleton 13; `combat::arm_creatures`), and a folk's, a geared skeleton's and the delver's is 10 + the Dexterity modifier + each worn item's armour, the delver's being the one the sheet shows. Anything with none counts as 10 (our own fallback). A creature's bite or a folk's blow at the delver rolls in `creatures::behaviour::BlowsAtDelver`: a hit is an `AttackOnDelver`, which hurts, jolts the view and flashes it red; a miss is a `MissedDelver`, which only whooshes past from the attacker's side (the swing's whoosh), and each roll is logged with its terms. Once the delver is down (0 hit points, or the run ending), nothing starts a bite or a swing at them and nothing is rolled or sent (`BlowsAtDelver::delver_is_down`; our own, since the SRD's attacks on a creature at 0 hit points, p. 97, follow death saving throws, which are left out). Advantage and disadvantage roll a second d20 and keep the higher or the lower ([SRD 5.1 p. 76](https://media.wizards.com/2023/downloads/dnd/SRD_CC_v5.1.pdf#page=76), "Advantage and Disadvantage"; `Odds`): a poisoned delver's swings roll at disadvantage (Poisoned, [p. 358](https://media.wizards.com/2023/downloads/dnd/SRD_CC_v5.1.pdf#page=358); `Poisoned::attack_odds`).

## Ways between levels

Our own proposal; the seed doc has levels but no way between them.

- The abyss is set when a delve begins. A run has one seed, and the level at each depth is always the same level, from `fb_level::depth_seed(run_seed, depth)`: the run's own seed at depth 1 (so `--seed 1207` is still level 1207) and a hash of the two below it. Going back up to a level finds it as it was laid out; persisting what happened on it is a separate task.
- Every level has a way down (`fb_level::Stair`, kind `Down`): a cell of floor against a wall of a room, far by path from the start (one of the cells whose approach lies at least 85% as far by path as the furthest such cell's, `FAR_SHARE` in `fb_level::stairs`, picked by the stair stream), with nothing else on it or on the cell in front, which is where you stand to use it. It is placed last, from its own salted stream (`STAIR_SALT`), so no earlier stream moved: level 1 of every seed is byte-identical to before (a test pins the hashes). Shutting its cell cuts no part of the level off.
- Below the first level the entrance is a way up. The door the delver came in by is not a door there: its cell is the stair up (kind `Up`), the start cell in front of it is where they arrive, and nothing is drawn behind it. The first level keeps its door, which shuts behind the delver; coming back up to it finds the door shut already.
- F near a stair and facing it (within 1.8 cells, within 60 degrees of straight at it) takes it: the view fades to black, the next level is made, and the view fades in on the delver standing in front of the matching stair, turned away from it, with "Depth N" fading in and out near the top. Taking the way down arrives at the next level's way up; taking the way up arrives beside the previous level's way down. The delver stands still while it lasts.
- The delve never leaves `AppState::Delving` to do it. `game/src/travel.rs` swaps `CurrentLevel`, and every system that lays a level out (stone, fixtures and stairs, folk, creatures, props, floor items, drips) clears its pieces and lays out the new level when it changes. The player, with their hit points, needs, stamina and mana, the character, the inventory and its equipment, and the map's record of each level seen stay as they are.
- A way down is drawn as a stone-kerbed stair going down into the floor (no floor in its cell) and a way up as steps climbing through a hole in the ceiling into a capped shaft (`game/src/level/stairs.rs`). The map marks seen stairs with a box and three treads, and its title names the depth. `print_level` marks them `>` and `<`.
- Crests (#577, #580): the factions tab and the map draw a faction crest from the armory (armory::crest::crest_mesh, the banner pattern and device shapes), drawn once off screen like an item icon (inventory::icon::Icons::crest). The map marks each claim with any seen cell at the mean of its seen cells (map::crests): a bare shield until the faction is met, under the delver and clear of stairs. A rune charge is cut from armory::crest::device_seed of the field so banner and crest agree. Capture switches: FB_EXPLORE_ALL, FB_MAP_ZOOM, FB_DEBUG_CAMERA=banner with FB_DEBUG_BANNER.
- Runes (#580): banners, shields and crests cut a rune charge from `armory::crest::device_seed(field)`, a pure function of the field by explicit encoding (variant positions, not names), so a faction's rune reads the same on all three. This re-rolled the runes of existing banners and shields once.

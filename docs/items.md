# Items

These are the rules the prototype settled on. Keep the intent and improve the details. Read alongside `reference/forever-below-plate.html` (`rollItem`, `rollSA`, `rollAffixes`, `legendize`, `dressState`, `saMeta`) and the tables in `data/`.

## Two layers

1. **Shape**: an `armory::Item`, rolled by the Armory's own "Forge new". `fb_items::armory_base` maps each base name (from `data/bases.json`, `data/goods.json` and the plate's own Armory list) to an Armory family, the styles of it the base accepts (for example, `Boots` accepts a `boot.style` of `boot` or `sabaton`), and sometimes a tweak applied after rolling (a Mantle is a short cloak, a Purse always holds coins). `fb_items::armory_base::roll_shape(base, seed)` rolls the shape: an accepted style straight from the family's `roll_as`, then the tweak. Unlike the plate it never retries seeds; see the first point under "Bases from the Armory".
2. **Game layer**: rarity, affixes, stats, footprint, history.

`fb_items::roll_item(base, seed)` does both: the shape, its profile (slot, tags, stats, footprint), then for gear the rarity and affixes, the name, a legend for a legendary, and the dress; goods stay common and plain, and a purse counts its coins. It is pure in the base and seed. The shape draws from the Armory's stream and the game layer from its own (`fb_items::game_rng`), so the game layer can change without changing any shape.

Every base is now made by the Armory; the plate's laced shirt, leather jerkin and pointed hat are no longer drawn outside it. See "Bases from the Armory" below.

### Bases from the Armory

The plate kept its base-to-family map, `SAB`, as JavaScript: each base named a family, an `acc` filter on the rolled state and an optional `tw` tweak. `tools/extract_data.js` wrote the filters out as `s=>arr.includes(s[k][f])`, losing which field and styles they named, and the tweaks set fields (`food.color`, `food.color2`) that the typed Armory states have since renamed. So `data/sab.json` is gone and the map is typed Rust, `fb_items::armory_base::ARMORY_BASES`: each filter is a list of the family's own style enum and each tweak a function on the typed state, so a renamed style or field fails to compile. Where the port departs from the plate:

- The plate rolled and retried (`SA.roll(fam, seed, acc)`, seeds `(seed + i*7919) % 999983 + 1`, up to 120 tries). A base here picks one of its styles uniformly and rolls it with the family's `roll_as`, so it lands first time, and club and axe archetypes, which the Armory does not store, can still be chosen. The plate's retries weighted styles by the family's roll weights; picking uniformly is this port's own choice.
- `Club` takes any club, `Hand axe` a hatchet, hand axe or bearded axe (the one-handers; broad, long, battle and horseman's axes are left to later bases). `Torch`, `Lantern` and `Belt` take any of their family; the plate's belt is a container, so its belt pouch is always on. `Trousers`, `Breeches` and `Leggings` take their legwear style, `Hood` a hood or cowl, `Mail coif` a mail coif.
- The goods are food or sundries: `Glowcap` is a mushroom given a glowing cap if it rolled dull, `Waterskin` and `Lamp oil` (a flask) are always filled, and lamp oil's amber colour is this port's own. `Rope`, `Lockpicks`, `Candle` and `Bandages` are their sundry kinds, `Scroll`, `Note` and `Book` their writing kinds, and a `Spare torch` is a torch that has never been lit. The plate kept the spare torch in the pack only; here it is still a plain good (common, no affixes, its own card line) but can also be held in the off hand like the gear `Torch`, so a spare found in a pack, a chest or lying in the level is a torch the delver can pick up and hold (`profile::held_light_slot`). The other fix, rolling only the gear `Torch` for the level's torch spots, would have left spares in containers and outfits that could not be held.
- `Bedroll` (7 lb) and `Tinderbox` (1 lb) are the sundry kinds of those names, weights from SRD 5.1 p. 68 (a bedroll is 2x2 cells, a tinderbox 1x1). They are carried and set down like any good and roll in the forge, but are held back from loose loot, crates and outfits (`HELD_BACK`) until tentmakers stock them (#548); a bedroll is set down and slept in (see "Setting camp"); nothing yet strikes a tinderbox.
- **Elixirs** are the alchemist's goods beside the Potion and Healing draught: four named flasks, each a full potion shape in its own colour, `named_by_base`, common with no affixes. Drinking one (F, as for any drink; it is used up) gives the delver an effect from `data/effects.json` (`fb_items::ELIXIRS`, `game/src/eat.rs`), joining any effects already on them. They take nothing off hunger or thirst, and are held back from loose loot (`HELD_BACK`): alchemists sell them (#548). Their names keep clear of the affixes: of the Owl is darkvision and of the Bear Constitution, so the wakeful elixir is of Vigil and the sating one of Fasting. Our own numbers.

  | Elixir | Colour | Effect | Lasts |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | of the Hare | rose | Swift: walking pace x1.25 (`pace`) | 1 game hour |
  | of Vigil | lilac | Wakeful: sleepiness builds at x0.2 (`need_rate`) | 8 game hours |
  | of Fasting | amber brown | Full-bellied: hunger builds at x0.3 (`need_rate`) | 8 game hours |
  | of Embers | orange | Warmed (the existing effect), and drives out Cold and Chilled blood at once | fades in about 2 game hours |

- `Blind cavefish` sets the fish's skin (`outer`) and fins (`accent`), the plate's `color` and `color2`. `Healing draught` sets the potion's `liquid` to the healing brew and tints its glass toward it as the Armory tints every potion.
- The plate's native bases become near styles, named for the base rather than the Armory's noun: a `Shirt` is a short, unbelted tunic with straight long sleeves; a `Leather jerkin` is a brigandine cut in a leather colour from `data/leather.json`, riveted one time in three for the plate's "Studded". A `Pointed hat` is the Armory's pointed hat style (see Hats below).
- Level loot names only a family: `fb_level`'s item spots carry an Armory family and a seed. `fb_items::roll_loot(family, seed)` picks one of that family's bases uniformly from the seed (`base_for`) and rolls it from the same seed. `fb_level` keeps its family weights and rolls no items itself, and the layout of every level seed is unchanged. (It depends on `fb_body` only for the `Folk` of its folk spots.)

### Clubs and axes

Clubs and axes are now Armory families (`armory::families::club`, `armory::families::axe`), after daggers in `Family::ALL`. They have no prototype fixtures, since the prototype's plate faked them outside the Armory. Where the Rust port departs from the plate:

- A club is one piece of wood: the haft swells into the striking end and closes in a dome (`Swell` in `sections::hafted`), and knots, iron bands and studs follow that surface. The plate's materials become archetypes: Oak and Ash are the wood's palette colour, and Bone-studded is bone teeth set in the swelling. Cudgels, plain, gnarled, knobbed (and shillelaghs), studded, spiked and iron-bound clubs roll too.
- An axe head is a bit crosswise through an eye (`sections::axe_head`, `build::axe_head`). It is wedge-sectioned, with a hard bevel line and a blade cross-section across its height, and it takes nicks and a broken edge from the item's condition. The eye is a socket section, with optional rings and langets. A plain, hammer, spike or second-bit poll backs it. Hatchets, hand, bearded, broad, long, battle, horseman's and throwing axes roll. The plate's Iron and Steel are both iron.
- The bit faces +X in item space, along the haft frame's normal. The plate's "turn 90° so the bit faces forward" (`docs/body.md`) still applies when the axe is held.

### Legwear

Trousers, breeches and leggings are the Armory's `legs` family (`armory::families::legs`), which the Armory prototype never had: its sliders, ranges and nouns are the port's own. They are drawn on `LowerBodyForm` (both legs and the hips, built from the garment's `TorsoForm` and the boot's `LegForm`), so `fb_body` can fit them by passing a folk body's dimensions. Where the port departs from the plate:

- The plate sweeps a tube down each leg at a fixed offset and lays a band over the hips. Here each leg becomes one half of the hips above the crotch, seamed down the front and back, so the pair reads as one garment.
- Breeches gather into a buckled knee band; trousers can have turned cuffs, knee patches, or puttees wound from the ankle to below the knee with the cloth bloused above them. The waistband closes with a drawstring, laces or toggles.
- A patterned pair of leggings is named Hose.

### Hoods and coifs

Hoods, cowls, coifs and mail coifs are the Armory's `hood` family (`armory::families::hood`), also new to the Armory, drawn on `HeadForm` (a parametric head from the plate's `headFn`) set on the garment's `TorsoForm`. Where the port departs from the plate:

- The plate draws a hood as one offset of the head with a torus for the cowl, and a mail coif as a lathed dome with a skirt. Here each is one sheet with thickness: over the head, round the face opening, falling from the back of the head and the chin to the shoulders, and out as a cape hung with the garment drape.
- Hoods draw their crown to a point and may trail a liripipe down the back; capes may be dagged. Cowls fall in a rolled loop over the chest. Coifs stop at the jaw and nape and tie under the chin; a quilted coif is an Arming Cap. Mail coifs take the shader's mail pattern, a leather binding round the face and sometimes a strapped ventail.
- A hood's hem trims the cape's edge (`hemline`) and the face opening (`neck`).

### Hats

Pointed hats, wide-brimmed hats and felt caps are the Armory's `hat` family (`armory::families::hat`), new to the Armory, drawn on the same `HeadForm` as hoods. They are a family of their own rather than hood styles: a hood is one draped sheet from the crown to the shoulders, and none of its controls (cape, face opening, liripipe, ties, ventail, mail) mean anything to a stiff crown and brim, while a hat's (crown height, brim width and set, band, feather) mean nothing to a hood. The plate groups them as "Hoods and hats" only on its shelf. Where the port departs from the plate:

- The plate draws its `Pointed hat` as a torus brim under a cone swept along a bent spline, with a band. Here the crown is raised from the head's brow line (`HeadForm::brow_point`), so it follows the head's oval and tips back a little as hats are worn, and is kept off the head where its profile would cut in. A bent crown turns each row square to its spine, so a slumping tip keeps its girth. The brim is a sheet bent along a circular arc: flat, drooping, or turned up against the crown.
- Pointed hats slump at the tip over a modest brim. Wide-brimmed hats have a rounded crown and a broad brim that may droop (a Slouch Hat), often a band and sometimes a feather (a Plumed Hat). Caps sit low over the crown with a turned-up brim or none; a cap whose top falls forward is a Phrygian Cap. `crumple` dents the crown and waves the brim, as hand-blocked felt does.
- The `Pointed hat` base takes the pointed style and the Armory's noun. The level's loot weights give hats 2, so their only base, the pointed hat, now lies about; wide-brimmed hats and caps wait for bases of their own.

### Belts

Belts are the Armory's `belt` family (`armory::families::belt`). The strap is the garment's `belt` section (width, leather, buckle), so a belted tunic and a belt base share it; the fittings are the port's own: plain, studded, sword belts slung toward the left hip with one or two frogs, girdles with a long tail, bar mounts and a strap end, and pouch belts with loops and D-rings. Where the port departs from the plate:

- The plate draws a band with a torus buckle and an ellipsoid pouch. Here the strap has its section, the buckle its frame and tongue, the free end runs through a keeper, and the plate's `holds` belt pouch is drawn as the bags family's own belt pouch (the state's `pouch`), hung behind the right hip with the belt through its loops.

### Food and drink

The Armory's food family (`armory::families::food`) now also makes the glowcap, the waterskin and the lamp-oil flask from `data/goods.json`, as the `mushroom`, `waterskin` and `flask` styles. Where the Rust port departs from the prototype:

- The prototype's `cut` toggle is a portion: whole, cut (a wedge out of a wheel, pie or round loaf, the end off a loaf, a slice out of an apple), a single piece (a slice of bread, a wedge of cheese, a slice of pie) or bitten (bread, fruit, a roast). Cut faces show a band of crust, rind, skin or pastry round the crumb, paste, flesh or filling. `Blind cavefish` and `Healing draught` tweaks set colours, which now have named roles (`outer`, `inner`, `accent`, `liquid`) instead of `color` and `color2`.
- **Potions** are one Armory model family in four sizes, `armory::families::food::PotionSize`: Vial, Small, Medium and Large, built at scale 0.7, 0.9, 1.1 and 1.4 of one seed's bottle (neck, bubbles, glow, wax). The size also picks the bottle shape, so a size always reads the same: the Vial and the Small are the tall, narrow skinny bottle (`Provision::skinny`), the Medium and the Large the round-bellied flask. This replaces the first cut, where the skinny bottle came up for any size whenever the seed's shape roll passed 0.75; a saved potion keeps its name, size, seal, count and glow, and only its silhouette can differ. Only the liquid changes between kinds: `Food::roll_potion(seed, size, brew)` is the same bottle at any brew, the glass and liquid taking its colour (`Provision::sized`, `Provision::brewed`). The size is the base: Vial, Small potion, Potion (the old base, now medium), Large potion hold the brew their seed rolls; Healing vial, Small healing draught, Healing draught (the old base, medium) and Large healing draught always hold healing. Old saves therefore keep their bases. The noun names the size: Minor, Lesser, (none), Greater before the brew's name. All our own numbers: stack limits 5, 4, 3 and 2 (`data/goods.json`, the more it holds the fewer share a cell); every potion takes one cell (its `data/goods.json` footprint, 1 by 1, which a potion keeps instead of the one its mesh would make), the large flask too; a healing potion restores 1d4 + 1, 2d4, 2d4 + 2 and 4d4 + 4 (`fb_items::Heal`, the medium one SRD 5.1's Potion of Healing, the large its Greater); drinking goes by the liquid, so any healing brew heals at its size (`game/src/eat.rs`). Potions of one base join a stack when they hold the same brew, whatever bottle their seeds made (the stack shows the first's); a small and a large never join. A potion is priced by its brew at its size, whichever base rolled it (`value::brew_factor`, our own: healing, mana, elixir and holy water at the plain price of the size, an antidote half, insight half as much again), so a plain Large potion that rolled healing costs what a Large healing draught does. The plain bases' card line is "A brew, known by its colour" (the liquid shows what it is; drinking is what acts on it). A potion's base is its size, and `Item::from_parts` snaps a saved bottle to it, so an old record at any scale keeps its name and reads as medium. The size is drawn from a stream of its own (`seed ^ 0x90710A5E`), so the seal, bubbles and glow of an existing potion seed are unchanged; only its scale is new. Among food, loose loot (`base_for`) weights each potion base a quarter of any other food base, so potions are about one find in six (other food keeps its relative weights, but adding eight bases shifts which base a seed picks); chests, amphorae and the alchemist roll the new sizes. The elixirs are unsized and do not stack.
- A potion's brew is typed, and the noun still follows the liquid's colour, so a retinted potion is named for the brew it looks like.
- Glass is its own material (`MaterialKind::Glass`), drawn see-through so the drink inside shows; liquids use the gem material in their own tint. A potion's glass is still tinted toward its brew. Cork is tinted wood, wax seals tinted bone, a bottle's paper label tinted cloth.
- Food's condition word is `freshness()` (Fresh, Day-old, Stale, Mouldy, Rotten), the prototype's `condWord` for food.
- Eating and drinking (our own numbers, `fb_items::provision`): with the inventory open, F eats or drinks the item under the mouse. It is used up, one item at a time, and `fb_character::Needs::consume` takes its value off the meter, never below 0. What each takes off: Bread 0.4 hunger, Cheese 0.3, Smoked fish 0.3, Apple 0.15, Meat pie 0.5, Sausages 0.35, Roast haunch 0.6, Blind cavefish 0.25, Glowcap 0.1; Tankard 0.5 thirst, Wine 0.3. The Potion and the Healing draught are drunk but take nothing off either meter (healing is its own rule). The lamp oil is not consumed. A waterskin is drunk from, not used up: each F takes a swallow of 0.25 off its fill and quenches that much thirst (about four swallows from a full skin), and the skin stays, empty at the end. The card says what F does and how much it eases. The delver's needs are `DelverNeeds` on the player, which starts Fed with each delve.
- Quickslots (`game/src/quickslots`): ten slots along the bottom of the view, bound by dragging an item from the inventory onto the strip of slots the inventory screen shows (the HUD bar hides under screens). The keys 1 to 0 use the item in a slot: food and drink are eaten and drunk as with F (a healing draught heals), a torch or lantern is drawn into the hand and then lit or put out, and a weapon or shield is drawn into the hand. Only carried food, lights and hand items can be bound. Pressing a slot's number over an item in the inventory binds it too; over one lying underfoot it is first picked up into the first carried place with room, as a pickup would put it, and with no room nothing is picked up or bound (our own). A slot keeps its item until it is used up or leaves the inventory; only ammunition keeps a count (below), so when one loaf is eaten the slot moves to another loaf if one is carried, and empties after the last. Binding an item to a second slot takes it from the first.

### Lights

Torches and lanterns are Armory families (`armory::families::torch`, `armory::families::lantern`), drawn from the bases the plate drew in `weaponGroup()`. Each light-giving family has a `light(&state)` that returns an `armory::build::light::LightAnchor`: the light's position in item space, a suggested colour, a radius in item units converted from the base's light stat in feet (`light` in `data/bases.json`), and how much it flickers. The game hangs the carried light there. Where the Rust port departs from the plate:

- A torch has three heads (a wrapped rag, a bound bundle of resinous splints, an iron cresset packed with tow) and burns through four states: fresh, burning, doused and spent. The plate's `Pitch` and `Tallow` materials are the head's fuel. How charred the head is, and how tall the flame stands, are sliders. A spent torch is named `Torch Stub`.
- Flames and embers are glowing meshes rather than an unlit material and a sprite. The halo is left to the game.
- A torch's light reaches 20 ft at an ordinary flame, the SRD's torch (20 ft bright), scaled a little by the flame's height and the head: a cresset reaches further and a splint bundle less far. Tallow burns a little dimmer. The shares are kept narrow, so the strongest torch, a cresset with a tall flame, reaches about 23 ft.
- A lantern (`armory::families::lantern`) has three bodies (horn panes in a frame, a pierced tin drum, a wire cage round a glass chimney), each under a hood and over a base, hung by a wire bail on 0 to 6 chain links from the carrying ring the hand holds. Inside stands a candle in a socket or an oil font with a burner and a filler cap. The plate's `Tin`, `Brass` and `Iron` are the lantern's metal; tin is iron tinted pale and horn is bone tinted amber, until there are materials for them.
- A lit lantern's panes, holes or chimney glow. Its light reaches about the SRD's hooded lantern (30 ft bright): 30 ft for horn with oil at an ordinary flame, farther from an open cage, less through a pierced drum, and less with a candle than oil. Every lantern reaches farther than any torch, even the weakest, a pierced lantern with a small candle flame (about 24 ft). The prototype's shares put that one near 14 ft, below a plain torch, and were retuned.

- A glowcap (a food mushroom glowing at 0.25 or more) gives light too: `Food::light` puts it at the middle of its caps, in the caps' colour, steady. The goods table only says it "glows faintly", so its reach (4 ft at full glow) is a proposal, not a number from the plate.

### Sundries

`armory::families::sundry` makes the small goods: candles, a coil of rope, lockpicks, a ring of keys, a tinderbox, a bedroll and bandages. Each rests on the ground, centred over the origin. Where it departs from the plate's `goodGroup()`:

- Keys, the tinderbox and the bedroll are new; the plate's text names them (lockpicking, "fever took them in their bedroll") but never drew them. A bedroll is the same rolled, strapped blanket a backpack carries (`armory::build::roll`).
- A candle can stand in a chamberstick, carry runs of wax, burn down and be lit, and `Sundry::light` gives its 5 ft light from the flame. The rope is one twisted rope laid in a coil and tied, not four stacked rings, and the lockpicks are hooks, rakes, diamonds and a tension wrench in an open leather roll.

- **Bandages are held back** (our own) until bandaging exists, like the affixes nothing reads: `armory_base::HELD_BACK` lists them, `find_weight` gives them 0 so no loose spot picks one, crates and the class goods (fighter and cleric carry them in `data/outfits.json`) skip them when rolling, and they stay in `ARMORY_BASES` and `data/goods.json` so the forge and `roll_item("Bandages", seed)` still make them. The crate's and the outfits' other picks shift with the table, so some existing seeds hold something else.

### Writing

`armory::families::writing` makes written goods: scrolls, notes and books. It is new; no prototype draws any of them, so every slider is this port's own. One state holds the three kinds (`WritingKind`): a `piece` section every kind shares (kind, size, paper, ink, lines of ink) and one section of options per kind. The scroll (`scroll`), the note (`note`) and the book (`book`) are built. Each rests on the ground, centred over the origin, its long side along X, and writing is never etched.

- A scroll's sheet is one thick sheet along a single path: a spiral wound on a roll, a rippled flat span with a curling free end, and, on two rollers, a second spiral wound back in. Rolled shut it is only the spirals; partly unrolled (`unroll` of 0.12 or more) the span shows rows of ink as thin strips lying on the paper. When the span is long the scroll is turned so its long side lies along X.
- Paper is parchment, vellum (paler) or papyrus (its fibres show). Rollers are none, one or two turned dowels with wood, bone or brass knobs. A scroll rolled shut can be tied with ribbon or cord and sealed with wax (a colour, and a star of 3 to 9 points raised on it); an open scroll never is.
- Condition drives the wear rather than a slider of its own: `damage` ragged and bitten edges and a torn end, `wear` times `rot` foxing and a browner sheet, and wear with damage scorched edges.
- A note (`build::note`) is one thick sheet from a scrap (0.6 units) to a full page (about 2.2 by 3). Its `form` is flat (cockled, bowed at the sides, corners lifted), folded once, folded in thirds (the creases are rounded geometry and the panels spring a little open), a folded letter closed with a wax seal (the scroll's seal, shared), or crumpled into a displaced, faceted ball. `tear` pulls the sides and ends in by noise for torn edges; `scrawl` lays a few uneven strips of ink on the top face, with an optional ink `blot` and `blood` stain. Condition adds tears, foxing, grime and scorch. Nouns: Note, Scrap of paper, Torn page, Folded note, Sealed letter, Crumpled note.
- A book (`armory::build::book`, own options in `BookOpts`, rolled from a stream of its own) runs from a pocket book (1.4 units long) to a great grimoire (4 units), with its thickness independent. Boards of leather, cloth or bare wood stand a little past the block of leaves and meet a rounded spine with 0 to 6 raised bands. The block (`armory::build::pages`) is a stack of separate leaves, so the fore-edge, head and tail show fine ridges and the leaves sit slightly uneven; the edges can be plain, gilt or stained red. Fittings are optional: corner plates, one or two clasps or a buckled strap, a central boss or a set stone, a chain and staple (a chained tome), and a ribbon bookmark. It lies shut or open, an open book with its leaves dipping into the gutter, the top ones lifted and fanned, and rows of ink on the open spread. `wear` scuffs the covers, `damage` rubs leather through at the corners, `fray` with wear frays cloth, and `wear` with `rot` browns the paper; rust and patina on the fittings come from the condition at draw time as for every metal. `piece.size` does not scale a book, which has its own length slider.
- Writing is loot and a good. `data/goods.json` has rows for `Scroll` (1x2, about 1 gp), `Note` (1x1, about 2 sp, four to a cell) and `Book` (2x2, about 25 gp), and `armory_base` maps each to `Accept::Writing`, which rolls its `WritingKind` through `Writing::roll_as`. The goods table has no price column, so the prices live in the card line only; the grid footprint comes from the built shape as for other unfolded families. Writing carries no affixes (goods stay common and plain), and `Family::Writing` has weight 4 in the level generator's loot table, so the first-level snapshot hashes were retaken. Within that weight a find picks its base by `armory_base::find_weight`: Scroll 10, Note 10, Book 1 (our own), since a book teaches its reader for good, so about one writing find in twenty-one is a book. The turntable and `--forge --family writing` show it.
- A book teaches (the user's call; `fb_items::lesson`). Its seed fixes one of the five ability scores, on its own stream so the shape is unchanged, and its card says "Teaches +1 WIS". F in the inventory, or its quickslot, reads it: the score rises by 1 for good (`Character::learned`, added under what is worn in `Character::scores`), and the book's seed goes on `Character::books_read`, so a book teaches a delver once and says it has nothing more to teach after. The book is kept, so it can be carried, left in the world and read by a later delver. Both are saved with the last delver (`last_character.json`, as soon as they change), so Continue keeps them; older files load with none. Scrolls and notes do nothing yet.

### Banners

`armory::families::banner` makes banners: painted cloth hung from a crossbar. It is new; no prototype draws one, so every slider and range is this port's own. It is scenery rather than loot: `fb_items` has no base for it (`Item::Banner` has no etching and its tests skip the family), so a faction's hall places banners through the Armory directly. Item space has the crossbar's middle at the origin, the cloth hanging down -Y from its top edge, its face toward +Z, and a pole standing behind it. One state:

- `cloth`: `width` (3 to 9 units), `length` (4 to 14; a wall banner rolls 6 to 12), `end`, `end_depth` (how far the notch or point cuts up, as a fraction of the length), `sag`, `folds` and `hang`. The ends are straight, forked (a V notch, square tails), swallowtail (a deeper notch, tails drawn to points) and pointed.
- `field`: the shield's `heraldry::Field` as it is (division, two tinctures, a charge, its tincture and size), so a faction's colours and crest go straight in: the Scale Court's green and gold per pale with a tower is `Field { division: PerPale, tincture: Vert, tincture2: Or, charge: Tower, .. }`. The roll keeps the rule of tincture through the shield's `roll_field`.
- `hem` (`on`, `width`, `trim` tincture), `fringe` (`on`, `length`, `strands` hung along the foot) and `hanging`: a `rod` (finials at each end: ball, spike or disc) or a `pole` (a standard: a crossbar on a pole with `reach` above and `foot` below, topped by the finial), `stuff` (wood, iron or brass), `overhang` past the cloth's sides, and `tabs`, the cord loops that tie the cloth to the bar.
- `cond`: wear as for any cloth. `fray` and `wear` are the shader's, as on garments; nothing is cut into the mesh.

Where it departs from the shield and the garments:

- The cloth is one sheet laid by `build::plate` on a drape surface, not a flat card. It hangs from its tabs, the top edge dips between them by `sag`, folds grow towards the foot by `folds` (their wavelength comes from the seed), and the foot swings out from the wall by `hang`. The sheet is made double sided with `MeshBuilder::make_double_sided`, so the back shows the painting mirrored, as dyed cloth does.
- The field's uv runs 0..=1 over the whole cloth, so divisions fall as they do on a shield; the charge sits above the foot's cut. It is a decal triangulated on the cloth's own drape and grid and lifted just off it, front and back, so it follows every fold and shows on both faces, tinted in its own tincture. The cloth and charge use the Cloth material (not Paint), so `fray` wears them.
- The hem is a thicker band between the outline and the same outline drawn in, with the sheet filling the inside. It falls away if the inset would fold over itself.
- Nouns: Banner (square, on a rod), Forked Banner, Swallowtail Banner, Pointed Banner, Standard (square, on a pole), Gonfalon (any tailed or pointed end on a pole), and Pennant for any point cut up 55% of the length or more. `--forge --family banner` rolls every end and both hangings.

### Crystals

`armory::families::crystal` makes every crystal in the game: a cavern's towering columns and the rough gems a delver pockets come from the one generator. It is new; no prototype draws one. It is also a good: the rough gems (below). `Item::Crystal` has no etching. One state, `crystal`, plus the usual `cond`:

- `habit`: prismatic, columnar, bladed, acicular, tabular, cubic, octahedral or dodecahedral (rhombic), after real crystal habit. The five prisms differ in `sides` (3 to 8), `length` (length against width), `flat` (flattened to a blade), `taper` and `point` (the height of the termination against the prism's width); the isometric forms take `length` as a stretch and `truncation` shaves their corners into the combinations fluorite and garnet show. `double_ended` points both ends instead of rooting one; `twinned` grows a second crystal through the first (a V twin for a prism, a penetration twin for an isometric form); `scepter` grows a wide head over a slender shaft.
- `growth`: a single crystal, a cluster (`count` crystals about a lump of rock, `spread` fanning them out from upright; stubby habits scatter over the rock instead of leaning) or a geode (a split nodule opening toward +Y, lined with `count` small crystals pointing in).
- `stone`: a `Gemstone`, which fixes the colour (turquoise and moonstone are opaque and never roll). `cloud` washes it milky, `cracks` lay pale fractures on faces, `inclusions` sink dark flecks into them, `glow` lights the surface in the stone's colour.
- `size` is the crystal's height (a plate's width, a geode's width) in units of 10 cm.

Where it departs from the other families:

- Every crystal is a convex solid cut from planes: each face starts as a huge square on its plane and is clipped by every other plane, so facets are exactly flat, carry their own true normal and meet at real edges. Face distances are jittered a few percent so no two faces are the same size, and termination faces alternate large and small as quartz's do.
- Item space: a single stands on y = 0 pointing +Y (the octahedron and dodecahedron are set on a face), a cluster rises from its rock and a geode opens upward, so the game can place any of them with +Y as the surface normal.
- **Rough gems** (#660): seven bases, `Rough garnet`, `Rough amethyst`, `Rough citrine`, `Rough sapphire`, `Rough emerald`, `Rough ruby` and `Rough diamond` (`fb_items::armory_base::ROUGH_GEMS`), each the crystal family forced to one stone (`rough_stone`, `rough_base`): a single crystal 7 to 15 cm across, never glowing, always a little clouded and sometimes cracked, its habit and proportions the seed's. A rough emerald's stone is the same `Gemstone::Emerald` the Armory sets into rings and pommels, so its colour is the cut stone's. Turquoise and moonstone are opaque and polished, so they never come rough. They are goods, 1x1 cells and 0.1 lb (`data/goods.json`), priced in `data/values.json` at about a twentieth of a cut stone (our own: citrine 3 gp, garnet and amethyst 5 gp, sapphire and emerald 50 gp, ruby and diamond 250 gp), and so sell to any trader like any good (`Item::value_copper`). Loose they are `Family::Crystal` floor loot (`base_for` picks one of the seven evenly); a cavern lays them itself (`fb_level::crystals::place_rough_gems`): at the foot of half its floor crystals, one to three spilled at the foot of each geode, and a couple on the bare floor of each chamber, always of the chamber's or geode's own stone (the item spot's seed is searched until `base_for` names that stone's base), and never in a room a faction holds. Chests, strongboxes and coffers keep them now and then, but only from depth 5 (`GEMS_FROM_DEPTH`), and the dear stones (sapphire, emerald, ruby, diamond) only from depth 9 (`DEAR_GEMS_FROM_DEPTH`), so a shallow container is as it was; barrels, crates and amphorae keep none.
- Nouns: "Amethyst Crystal", "Garnet Column", "Bladed Emerald", "Diamond Needle", "Citrine Plate", "Cubic Ruby", "Sapphire Octahedron", "Diamond Dodecahedron", "Emerald Cluster", "Garnet Geode". `--forge --family crystal` offers the habit, growth and stone as choices.

### Tents and campfire kits

`armory::families::tent` and `armory::families::campfire` make the camp goods that [camps](https://github.com/turn-radius/forever_below_game/issues/315) are built from. Neither is in the prototypes, so every slider is this port's own. Each has two forms in one state, picked by a `form` choice in the Forge: the form it is carried in, and the form it takes when put down.

- **Tent.** Its `style` is a ridge tent (an A-frame: two sloped canvas panels with a short skirt, a closed back gable, a front gable split into two door flaps, a ridge pole on two uprights standing just outside the gables) or a bell tent (a cone of canvas round one centre pole, with a doorway cut in its wall and two flaps over it). Pitched, it stands on its groundsheet with guy lines and pegs: `guys` (up to eight, to the ridge ends, the eave middles and the eave corners; round the cone for a bell), `pegs` (per eave, or per quarter turn of a bell's ring, each with a loop of tape from the skirt), `door_open` (0 shut, 1 tied back wide) and `patches` (sewn on at seeded spots). `height` runs 10 to 14 units, the pole's top, and `width` and `length` set the floor (a bell's width is its diameter; its length only matters packed). Packed, the same canvas is rolled round its poles (`build::roll`), strapped twice, with a bundle of pegs tied on and a coil of guy line; its girth grows with the canvas area (`tent::packed_size`), so a bigger tent is a fatter roll. The `canvas` choice is linen, hemp, oilcloth or hide (hide is leather to the shader, the others cloth), with a colour and, on linen, an optional stripe. Fray, rot and grime come from the shared condition sliders.
- **Campfire kit.** Set down it is a ring of `stones` round a bed of ash, `logs` stacked crosswise two to a layer, `kindling` leaned over them, and a `rig` (nothing, an iron post with a swinging arm, or a tripod lashed at the top) that hangs an iron `pot` on a chain. The pot only draws with a rig. `lit` adds a flame and `Campfire::light` gives its 40 ft light (our own: a torch is 20); `ash` chars the wood and greys the bed. Packed it is a bundle of sticks tied with cord, the rig laid along its top and the pot set at one end.
- **Bases.** `data/goods.json` has `Tent` (about 2 by 5 cells packed, 20 lb) and `Campfire kit` (about 2 by 3 cells, 12 lb), and `armory_base` maps each to its family with a tweak that rolls the carried form. The tent weight is SRD 5.1 p. 68-70, "Tent, two-person". The kit's 12 lb is our own: about 2 lb of kindling and a rig, plus an iron pot, which the SRD (p. 68-70) weighs at 10 lb. A pitched tent and a set fire are not packable (`profile::is_carried_form`) and stand on their base (`stands_on_base`); putting a carried one down is the camp system's job (`Tent::in_form` gives the pitched form of a packed state). Both are held back from loose loot until tentmakers stock them (#548): `armory_base::HELD_BACK` lists them, so `roll_loot` never yields one and no existing seed shifts, while `roll_item("Tent", seed)` still makes them. A lit fire's 40 ft light is not read by anything yet.

### Setting camp

A bedroll and a tent are set down to be slept in (#616). Setting one down changes its form and nothing else: a bedroll is unrolled (`armory::families::sundry::Article::laid`, built by `laid_bedroll`: a mat, a thin mat and a padded blanket as displaced cloth grids over one heightfield, rims rolling down to the floor, a hem rolled along the blanket's head, a plump pillow, the strap bent over the foot; the blanket wears the bedroll's weave as a field pattern over its own uv) and a tent is pitched (`TentForm::Pitched`). `fb_items::camp` does the change both ways (`set_up`, `pack_up`) by taking the item apart and putting it back together (`Item::from_parts`), so its seed, rarity, affixes, history and wear are kept and its profile follows the form: a pitched tent is no longer packable, a packed one fits a pack again. A name that was only the shape's noun follows the form ("Rolled Ridge Tent" becomes "Ridge Tent"); a given one stays.

- **Setting down.** With the inventory open, F on a bedroll ("Lay out") or tent ("Pitch") in the pack sets it on the floor in front of the delver (`game/src/camp.rs`): the near edge 30 cm ahead for a bedroll and 60 cm for a tent (`camp::gap_for`, so the tent reads whole), square to them, a bedroll lengthways with its foot toward them, a tent with its door toward them. It is refused, with a line in the toast, across a doorway or gate, across a stair or the cell you stand in to use one, over rock or a cell the way is shut across, over a prop or someone standing, or over another set camp. A tent must also leave the way open (`camp::check_passage`): it is refused ("It would block the way.") if it leaves less than the delver's width past it, or cuts the floor round it into more pieces than before, sampled a unit apart. A set camp is a floor item like any other: the world record keeps it as the item it now is, in its set-down form, so it is where it was left when the delver comes back to the level. A tent blocks the way as a prop does, by its canvas rectangle (`fb_items::camp::canvas_half`), not its guy lines and pegs; a bedroll is walked over. Camp goods are drawn at their own size on the floor, not the larger floor scale, so what was checked against the room is what fills it.
- **Sleeping.** F on a set camp in reach (`game/src/camp.rs`; the hover card lists "F Sleep") lies the delver down in it. They are `Asleep`: the view darkens to "Sleeping in the tent: 3 hours", the world gets no focus from them (`level_has_focus`), the game clock runs at `[camp] sleep_clock_ratio` game seconds a second (900, a night in about 30 seconds; the folk and the needs keep up, since every step is exact for any length), a torch or lantern they carry is put out, and the needs step as `Activity::Sleeping`: sleepiness falls at the needs' own rate (`sleep_hours_to_rested`, 8 hours spent to rested) in a bedroll (`bedroll_rest` 1.0) and `tent_rest` 1.5 times that in a tent, so a tent rests in about 5.3. Hunger and thirst rise at half pace, mana and stamina refill, and unbroken hours ease exhaustion, all as the sleeping rules already had them. They cannot lie down while already Famished or Dry (`exhaustion.famished_at`), or with a creature or a hostile folk within `wake_distance` (80 units, 8 m, through walls too). In a camp a rested delver (sleepiness under `tired_from`, 0.25 of the meter) may lie down anyway: the delve is saved as ever, and they nap `nap_hours` (1 game hour) and wake "You wake from a short rest." (the rested wake does not apply to a nap; pain, a hostile near and Famished still wake them; the nap's end hour is in the save as `nap_until`, so a resumed nap ends on time). Sleeping rough, out of a camp, would still need `tired_from`. They wake rested, when hunger or thirst reaches the Famished band, when something hostile comes within `wake_distance`, when they lose any hit points since they were last looked at (the cold included; healing in their sleep hides no later blow), when their bed is gone, or at any key or mouse button, or a screen opening, after the first half second; the toast says which, and how long they slept. All of those numbers are our own, in `data/rules.toml` under `[camp]` and in the lab's rules page.
- **Packing up.** G on a set camp in reach, or on one lying underfoot with the inventory open ("G Pack up" on its card), takes it back into the first bag with room (`camp::pack`): the same item in its carried form (`fb_items::camp::pack_up`), its wear, affixes and history as they were, lifted off the floor and out of the level's record. A pack with no room refuses with "There is no room in your pack for it." and the camp stays as it is. A tent's packed roll is two cells by five, so a small pack may not take it. A camp that is slept in is not packed up: the sleeper's own wake comes first.

## Rarity and affixes

- Roll `x` against the find's **odds** (`fb_items::Odds`: a depth and a cap). On the first level: `x < .83` common, `< .95` magic, otherwise rare, and never legendary (83% / 12% / 5% / 0%). These are our own, set lower than the plate's `.58 / .85 / .97` so the first level gives out less. Containers cap at rare.
- **Nothing on the first level is legendary.** `Odds::loot(1)` caps at rare, and the cap holds however much deeper a find rolls (`Odds::deeper`), so neither luck nor a jackalope's boost brings a legendary there.
- **Deeper finds roll better.** Each threshold moves from the first level's (`.83 / .95 / 1`) toward a deep limit (`.07 / .40 / .88`: 7% common, 33% magic, 48% rare, 12% legendary) by `t = 1 - (2/3)^((depth - 1) / 4)`, so a third of the way four levels down and never quite all the way. Rare or better comes up about four times as often by depth 5:

  | depth | common | magic | rare | legendary |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | 1 | 83% | 12% | 5% | 0% |
  | 2 | 75.7% | 14% | 9.1% | 1.2% |
  | 3 | 69.1% | 15.9% | 12.9% | 2.2% |
  | 5 | 57.7% | 19% | 19.3% | 4% |
  | 10 | 37.5% | 24.6% | 30.7% | 7.2% |
  | 20 | 18.1% | 29.9% | 41.7% | 10.3% |

  The curve and the deep limit are our own proposal, not from the plate or any source: a saturating curve so that deep levels keep improving without ever making common gear vanish. The depth is an effective one: a find that should roll better than its level, such as a locked room's (not built yet), passes a deeper one (`Odds::deeper(2)`).
- **Luck rolls loot deeper.** Each whole point of luck the delver wears (`Stat::Luck`, summed by `fb_character::Gear::luck`) rolls the loot of a level they come to one level deeper (`Odds::with_luck`), keeping the level's cap. It counts what they wear as they arrive: `game::character::loot_odds` gives the odds the floor (`carry::lay_out_items`) and the containers (`props::lay_out_props`) roll against, and the level below, made ahead of time, is rolled again on arrival if the luck changed since (`staged::Staged::take_items`, `take_props`). A level visited before keeps what its record holds. Luck does not change what folk and the risen dead carry (`Odds::kit`), nor a jackalope's drop, which is rolled as the creature spawns. Each point being a level is our own proposal.
- **Every loot roll takes the level's depth.** Floor loot (`roll_loot`) and container contents (`roll_contents`) roll `Odds::loot(depth)`, with the delver's luck. The gear folk wear (`Outfit::roll_with`) and skeletons carry (`skeleton_outfit`) rolls `Odds::kit(depth)`: the same odds, but capped at magic on the first level and at rare on the second, so nobody met on the first level carries rare or legendary gear, none on the second carries a legendary, and enemies grow better equipped as one descends. The caps are our own; the plate rolled every outfit at the same odds. The player's starting kit is common only.
- An item keeps the odds it was rolled against (`Item::odds`), so its base, seed and odds always roll it again the same. `roll_item(base, seed)` is the first level's odds; `roll_item_with(base, seed, odds)` takes any.
- Common: no good affix. Magic: one good prefix or suffix (50/50). Rare: a good prefix and a good suffix. Legendary: a good prefix and two good suffixes. A rare or legendary suffix slot with no suffix that fits takes a second good prefix instead (`rarity::good_suffix_fits`), never an affix it already has or one of its group; a prefix slot with nothing stays empty (a ring rolls no good prefix, so a rare ring still has one good affix), and a slot whose kind has a pool draws as it always did. Magic still falls back either way. The enchanter's bench follows the same rule: a second good prefix only where no good suffix fits. A name lists every good prefix ("Gleaming Long-burning Lantern").
- **Any rarity can roll a flaw**, less often the rarer it is: one **flaw** prefix at 30% for common (the plate's), 20% for magic, 10% for rare and 5% for legendary (`FLAW_CHANCE`, our own; the plate gave flaws to common only). The flaw is rolled after the good affixes, so a magic or better item keeps the good ones it would have rolled and its name keeps the good prefix (the card lists the flaw), and it never shares a group with them (no Sluggish on Fleet boots).
- An affix applies only where its `on` tag matches the item's tags (`cloth`, `leather`, `metal`, `wood`, `wear`, `armor`, `quiet`, `weapon`, `light`, `trinket`, `container`, `ranged`, `blunt`, `blade`, or `any`). Tables: `data/afx.json`. Each `fx` is a list of typed effects (see "Affix effects" below).
- **Affixes nothing reads yet are held back** (our own). An affix rolls only if one of its effects changes a stat the game reads (`Stat::in_play`, `Affix::in_play`). Stealth (read only by the HUD eye, not by any creature), break chance and charisma are not in play, so Silent, Squeaking, of Quiet Steps, Cracked, of Presence and of the Silver Tongue do not roll (disease risk came back into play with sewer plague, so Mildewed, Lice-ridden and of Lesser Warding roll, and lockpicking came back with the dice roll on a door's lock, #805, so of Thieves rolls, on gloves, rings, amulets, charms and belts, the gear a thief works in, and not on weapons or lanterns); they stay in `data/afx.json` and can still be applied by hand. When a stat's system arrives it comes off the list in `Stat::in_play` and its affixes roll again. `pick_affix` still draws against the whole pool, so an item that rolled an affix in play rolls the same one; a draw that lands on a held-back affix takes the same place among those in play, or leaves the slot empty when none fit (a quiet item's only flaw is Squeaking, so a common boot rolls no flaw for now). A magic item whose drawn kind has nothing that fits rolls the other kind instead (a ring has no good prefix, so it takes a suffix), so every magic item does something.
- **Groups, tiers and slots** (`data/afx.json`'s optional `grp`, `r` and `at`). An affix with a group never rolls on an item that already has one of that group, so a weaker and a stronger tier of one stat do not stack (`Affix::stacks_with`). `r` is the least rarity it rolls on, so the stronger tiers come only on rarer finds; the pattern follows Diablo II, whose stronger suffixes need a higher affix level (Strength +1-2, Might +3-5, Ox +6-9 and so on, [The Arreat Summit, magic suffixes](http://classic.battle.net/diablo2exp/items/magic/suf.shtml)), with rarity standing in for item level. `at` lists the slots it rolls in, besides its `on` tag (a ring counts as `ring1` for either hand). Every number below is our own. **The comfort suffixes keep to fitting slots**: of Plenty and of Wakefulness (belt, neck, charm, ring), of the Ox (belt, back, body), of Lesser Warding (head, hands, neck, charm) and of the Mole (head, neck, charm) no longer roll on weapons, shields and the rest of the armour. The affix pool of those slots is smaller, so some existing seeds now roll a different affix.
- **Affixes added after the plate** (all our own numbers): speed (`speed`, a multiple of the delver's walking and sprinting pace, on boots, legs and charms): Fleet ×1.1, of Haste ×1.2 (rare), and the flaw Sluggish ×0.9. Ability scores (on rings, amulets, charms and belts): of Might and of the Giant (+1 and +2 STR, the +2 from rare), of Deftness and of the Fox (DEX), of Vigour and of the Bear (CON), of Wit and of the Sage (INT), of Insight and of the Seer (WIS), of Presence and of the Silver Tongue (CHA, held back until the delver has a Charisma score), and the flaws Feeble, Clumsy and Frail (−1 STR, DEX, CON; prefixes, since only prefixes roll as flaws, which is why the user's "of Frailty" is called Frail). Attack: Vicious +2 damage and Accurate +2 to hit (rare), of Carnage +3 damage and of Precision +3 to hit (legendary). Armour: Reinforced +2 (rare), of the Bulwark +3 (legendary). Block, on shields, gloves and charms: of Deflection 10% and of the Rampart 20% (rare); they are `on: any` with `at` offhand, hands and charm and a `not` of `light`, so a torch in the off hand never rolls them (`Affix::except`). To hit: Sighted +1 to hit with missiles, on bows and crossbows (the `ranged` tag), of the Hawk +2 with missiles on rings, gloves and helms (rare), and of the Steady Hand +1 to hit on rings, gloves and amulets. Attack rate, a multiple of the speed of a swing, a draw and a reload (`attack_rate`): Quick ×1.1 and of Swiftness ×1.2 (rare) on weapons, in one group, and of the Duellist ×1.05 on gloves, in its own so it stacks with them. Stamina (both multiples, and the two groups `stamina_refill` and `stamina_cost` each keep a weaker and a stronger tier apart): refill, Tireless ×1.2 on body armour and boots and of Second Wind ×1.3 (rare) on amulets, charms and body armour; cost, Light-handed ×0.75 on weapons and shields (the off hand but not a torch or a lantern, by `not: light`) and of Endurance ×0.8 on legs, boots and belts. Mana refill (`mana_refill`, a multiple of how fast the pool refills in real seconds, one group): Attuned ×1.2 on helms, amulets and charms, of the Wellspring ×1.25 (rare) on amulets, rings and charms, and the flaw Drained ×0.8. Darkvision, on helms, amulets and charms: of the Owl 30 ft (rare) and of the Bat 60 ft (legendary). Luck, on rings, amulets and charms: of Chance +1, of Fortune +2 (rare) and of Luck +3 (legendary), named and ordered after Diablo II's magic-find suffixes Chance, Fortune and Luck ([The Arreat Summit, magic suffixes](http://classic.battle.net/diablo2exp/items/magic/suf.shtml)). Wards, on rings, amulets and charms (the user's call, a +1 tier and a +2 tier from rare): speed, of the Hare ×1.05 and of the Wind ×1.1, in the speed group so a charm never carries one with Fleet or of Haste; recovery, of Mending +1 and of the Troll +2, each point mending a quarter faster (`recovery.faster_per_point`, so +2 takes 6.7 game minutes a hit point instead of 10); and poison resistance, of Purity +1 and of the Mongoose +2, added to the Constitution save against venom.
- **Light affixes** (`on: "light"`, torches and lanterns in any slot they are carried in, all our own numbers). Reach, a prefix tier ladder in the `light_reach` group so an item rolls one: Gleaming +10 ft, Radiant +20 ft (rare), Blazing +30 ft (legendary). Burn time, the `burn_time` group: Long-burning ×2, Enduring ×3 (rare), Everlasting ×4 (legendary), and the suffix of Patience ×1.5, which cannot stack with them. Warmth, suffixes in the `light_warmth` group: of the Ember +2 and of the Furnace +4 (rare), read from the worn warmth because the carried light counts as equipped (`Character::loadout`), and only while the light burns: an unlit lantern warms no one (`Character::warmth_of`). Reach reaches the level through `Item::light_factor` and burn time through the fuel (`scale_fuel`). Left out: a dazzle affix and a notice affix, since neither the dazzle system nor stealth reads a stat from a light yet. Of the Hearth stays on clothing (`wear`).
- **Capacity affixes** (on worn bags, all our own numbers). A pack, satchel, sack or belt pouch carries the `bag` tag, and three affixes in the `capacity` group act on it through the `grid_rows` stat: of Holding (suffix, +1 row, from magic), of the Bottomless (suffix, +2 rows, from rare) and the flaw Cramped (prefix, one row less). A grid never has fewer than one row, so a Cramped belt pouch is 4x1. `Profile::fit_capacity` sizes the inner grid once the affixes are applied, in both `roll_item_with` and `Item::from_parts`, so a satchel of Holding is 6x4 against a plain 6x3 and the record rebuilds it the same. The card reads "Empty · 6x4 inside (plain 6x3)" (`Profile::plain_grid`), and wearing one over another counts the cells as before. Quivers, bolt cases and purses carry no `bag` tag, so they roll none (quivers hold ammunition stacks, a later task). Containers cap at rare, so there is no legendary tier. The grid is fixed when the affix rolls, and `enchant::enchantable` refuses every container, so no bench can add or strip a capacity affix and shrink a grid under its contents; should a later change let one, it must refuse a strip or reforge that would leave the contents not fitting. Value follows the usual affix factors (good 1.15 to 1.8 by tier, flaw 0.7). Bag affix pools are larger, so some seeds roll a different bag affix.
- **Roll shift from the mana affixes.** Attuned, of the Wellspring and Drained join the head, neck, ring and charm affix pools, so an existing seed for one of those slots may roll different affixes than before, as the stamina affixes did.
- What they do in the game: an ability bonus adds to the score (`Character::scores`, held within 1 to 30, SRD 5.1 p. 76), so its modifier flows into to-hit, damage, Armor Class, capacity, saves, the mana pool and the hit point maximum, which moves with the Constitution modifier (`combat::vitals::fit_hit_points`). Speed multiplies the pace (`player::GearPace`). Block chance counts while the shield in the off hand is raised (hold right click; without a shield right click swings what the off hand holds, or punches with it empty, with no Strength bonus to its damage as for two-weapon fighting, SRD 5.1 p. 95) and the blow comes from within 60° of where the delver faces: it turns aside a blow that would have hit, logged "blocked", clanging on the shield and costing `stamina.block_cost` (`combat::guard`, `combat::vitals::BlockChance`). The guard cannot swing, walks at half pace with no sprinting, and drops once the delver is winded. Every shield has a chance of its own by size, our own numbers: a buckler 30%, a tower or pavise 60%, any other 45%; the block affixes add to it, and so do the block suffixes on worn gloves and charms, but only while a shield is held: with none they do nothing, and the total never passes 90% (`fb_character::MAX_BLOCK_CHANCE`, our own; `Character::loadout` sums the worn items' block only when a shield is held, so a buckler's 30% with gloves of Deflection blocks 40%). Gear besides the weapon adds its `to_hit` to the d20 of every swing and shot (`Weapon::held_in`, `combat::shoot::shot_to_hit`; the weapon's own is already on its roll), and `ranged_to_hit` (`Stat::RangedToHit`) adds to a shot's alone, the bow's own included; so a ring of the Hawk adds nothing to a sword swing. Attack rate (`Stat::AttackRate`) counts only from the weapon in the main hand and the gloves (`Character::loadout`), multiplied across them; a swing runs on at that rate (`combat::swing::Swing::rate`, so the blow lands at 0.24 s over the rate and the whole swing takes 0.5 s over it: a Quick sword swings in 0.45 s), a bow draws in 1 s over it (`combat::shoot::advance_draw`) and a crossbow reloads in its 1.5 or 3 s over it (`reload_seconds`). Stamina refill (`Stat::StaminaRefill`) multiplies the refill rates standing, walking and sleeping, and stamina cost (`Stat::StaminaCost`, lower is better) multiplies the swing cost, the block cost and the sprint drain alike, one stat for all three (our own, so of Endurance, worded after the sprint, saves on swings and blocks too). Each item's multiple multiplies the others' (`Gear::stamina_refill`, `Gear::stamina_cost`): of Second Wind refills a full bar in 8 / 1.3 s, a Light-handed sword's swing costs 0.06 of the bar instead of 0.08, and Light-handed with of Endurance costs 0.6 of usual. The game reads them from `character::GearStamina` on the delver (`player::weigh_the_delver`) in `simulate::step_stamina` (`Stamina::step_geared`), `combat::swing::start_swing` and `combat::guard::take_blocks`. The lab's stamina bar shows the rules' own sprint seconds, with no gear. Luck rolls the loot of the levels they come to deeper (above). The sheet shows Speed, Block chance, Luck, Abilities, To hit, Ranged to hit, Attack rate, Stamina refill and Stamina cost with the other gear numbers, with each item's part on hover, and the card lists each.
- Foes, wards and shuns (our own numbers). Creatures fall into kinds, `fb_items::Foe`: Vermin (rats, bats, centipedes), Undead (skeletons), Spider, Serpent (snakes), Beast (jackalopes) and Troll; `fb_creatures::Species::foe` says which, and the stat arrays `Stats::ward` and `Stats::shun` are indexed by it, so a per-kind stat is one more array (the banes below are a third). A ward (`Modifier::Ward`) takes a fraction off the damage that kind's blows do the delver: the suffixes of the Ossuary (undead), of the Rat-catcher (vermin), of the Web (spiders) and of the Charmer (serpents) each take 25%, on amulets and charms, one group per kind. Fractions from different gear add, up to 75% (`fb_items::foe::MAX_WARD`), and the damage is rounded but a blow that lands for 1 or more still does 1 (`combat::vitals::take_bites`); folk blows are never warded. A shun (`Modifier::Shun`) is a chance that a creature of the kind never notices the delver: of Vermin's Peace 50% (vermin), of the Grave's Silence 35% (undead, rare) and of the Still Web 35% (spiders, rare). Shuns from different gear combine as 1 - (1 - a)(1 - b), up to 90%. Each creature rolls once, from its own seed (`creatures::behaviour::shun_roll`, kept on its `Brain`), and does not notice the delver while that roll is under the delver's chance for its kind (`combat::vitals::Shunned`), so the same creatures pass them every time and nothing flickers; a creature the delver strikes (`creatures::behaviour::Aroused`) notices them like any other from then on. The card lists each ward and shun, and the sheet's Creature wards row sums them with each item's part on hover.
- Banes (our own numbers). A weapon's bane (`Modifier::Bane`, `Stats::bane`, an array of optional dice indexed by `Foe`) is extra damage dice against one kind of creature. The tags say what a weapon is: `blunt` (mace, club, staff; the club's row in `data/bases.json` carries it), `blade` (sword, dagger, hand axe) and `ranged` (bow, crossbow), set in `profile::from_shape` and `data/bases.json`; a tag changes no existing draw, since a pool filters by `on` and `not`. Of the Bonebreaker (+1d6 against the undead, legendary) and Bone-crushing (+1d4, rare) roll on blunt weapons only, in the group `bane_undead`; Ratbane (+1d4 against vermin, magic) on any weapon; of the Spider-slayer (+1d6 against spiders, rare) on any weapon; Hunter's (+1d4 against beasts and serpents, rare) on any weapon but a blunt one (`on: weapon`, `not: blunt`), so blades, spears and bows. A bane is the weapon's own, never summed from other gear: `combat::Weapon::bane` reads the swung weapon's stats and `combat::shoot::Shot::bane` the bow's (the arrow's own are not read). When a blow or a shot lands, `combat::bane_damage` rolls the dice from the same stream as the blow's damage, when the target's `Species::foe` has a bane, and adds them to the blow's damage in `Struck`, so a skeleton's bludgeoning doubling (`combat::feedback::damage_to`) doubles them too. Folk have no kind and take none. The card lists each bane ("+1d6 against the undead") and compares them by their mean.
- `tools/extract_data.js` writes `afx.json` from the plate, which has none of these fields or affixes; run it again only to merge.
- When affixes are chosen by hand (from the armory), rarity follows from them: two good affixes make it rare, one makes it magic, none makes it common.
- Name: `<prefix> <Armory noun> <suffix>`. The noun comes from `forgeNoun` (Longsword, Rondel Dagger, Kettle Hat).
- A **legendary** gets a title (`<owner>'s <noun>` or `The <adjective> <noun>`). The original name becomes its subtitle, and it gets three lines of history: where it was made and by which faction, who carried it and what they did, and how it was lost. The factions it names are the delver's world's own: an item found on a level (floor loot, container contents) is told with `Item::in_world(level.faction_names())`, which picks them from the level's factions by the item's seed and leaves the title as rolled; with no world, as in the Forge, they come from the faction name grammar. A record keeps the legend as told, so a restored item names the same factions.

### Affix effects

Each affix's `fx` in `data/afx.json` is a list of `fb_items::modifier::Modifier`s acting on `fb_items::stats::Stats` or on the Armory `Condition`: `add` or `scale` a stat, `damage_or_armour` (the plate's Rusted: damage bonus on a weapon, armour on anything else), or `condition`, which raises a condition slider to at least a value. `Stats` is the minimal set the affixes need; deriving it per family is still to do. Amounts and distances never fall below zero (the plate's `Math.max(0, ...)` for warmth and armour, applied to every one); bonuses such as stealth and damage can.

Where the effects come from:

- The plate's `fx` source: Warm, Featherlight, Hardened, Keen, Gleaming, Moth-eaten, Rusted, Smoky, of the Hearth, of the Ox. Featherlight keeps the weight exact where the plate rounded it to 0.1 lb; rounding is left to the card.
- `dressState`'s condition for flaws, so a flaw's wear travels with the affix: Rusted raises wear to 0.75 and rust to 1.5; Moth-eaten, Mildewed and Lice-ridden raise wear to 0.6, fray to 1.4 and rot to 1.2; Cracked raises wear to 0.6 and rot to 1.5.
- The card text, for affixes the plate gave no `fx`: Silent, Squeaking and of Quiet Steps (stealth ±2), Balanced (+1 to hit), Long-burning (burn time ×2), of the Mole (darkvision 10 ft), of Plenty and of Wakefulness (hunger and sleepiness ×0.85), of Thieves (lockpicking +2), of the Leech (heal 1 on a kill).
- These numbers are this port's own proposals, since the text gives none: Mildewed and Lice-ridden multiply disease risk by 1.5, of Lesser Warding by 0.5, and Cracked adds a 10% chance to break on a hard blow. Lice-ridden worn brings on itching, and a lit Smoky light in hand stinging eyes (`game::exposure`).

`fb_items::naming` writes the names and legends. The noun is the Armory's, or the base's own name for the bases marked `named_by_base` in `fb_items::armory_base` (the plate did this for `Blind cavefish` and `Healing draught`). The plate's material word for its own bases ("Linen Shirt", "Oak Club") is gone, since those bases are Armory shapes now and the Armory noun already says what they are. A legendary's title takes its epithet from the plate's lists (Tooth, Oath, Wick, Keepsake...), chosen by whether it is a weapon, a light, a trinket or anything else, and the owner's name is a first name from `data/syl.json`. The title uses a straight apostrophe where the plate used a curly one, because the HUD's font has no curly one.

### Items as they are now

An item can change after its roll (an enchanter swaps its affixes, a smith makes it), so nothing about it is assumed to be what the seed rolls. `fb_items::Item::from_parts(ItemParts)` builds an item from its base, seed, Armory shape, rarity, affixes, name, legend, history lines, coins and odds, rolling nothing: the profile is worked out again from the shape, and the stats from the affixes. `ItemParts::from(item)` takes one apart, so a change is take apart, edit, put together. `Item::history` holds lines beyond a legendary's legend ("Made by ..."), shown on the hover card after the legend's. An affix is found by its name (`tables::affix_named`; names in `data/afx.json` are unique). `game::record::ItemRecord` keeps all of this (affixes by name) and restores through `from_parts`, so a changed item survives leaving a level and coming back. Records last for one delve, so they carry no migration.

## Rarity shows on the object

`dressState` sets the Armory condition from rarity: common wear 0.15–0.50, magic 0.06–0.26, rare 0.02–0.14. A legendary is either pristine (0.02) or old and worn (0.4–0.7). Rare and legendary items get etching; legendary etching is gold inlay. Flaws raise the matching decay: Rusted sets rust to 1.5, and Moth-eaten, Mildewed and Lice-ridden raise fray and rot.

`fb_items::dress::dress` does this from the plate's own stream (`mulberry32(seed * 3 + 1)`). Where it departs from the plate:

- A flaw's decay comes from the affix's own `condition` effects (see "Affix effects"), applied after the rarity's wear, rather than from a list of names inside `dressState`.
- Food, torches, lanterns and sundries have no etching in the Armory, so a rare one is only cleaner.
- The plate also turned a legendary's gem glow up to 0.8. The Armory keeps gems in a different section in each family (a mace's head gem, a ring's or amulet's stone, a coffer's crowning stone), so that is left for later.

## Stats and footprint (`saMeta`)

Each family maps to a slot, tags, weight, armour, warmth and damage, plus a grid footprint from the item's real size: `cells(units) = clamp(ceil(units / 2.4), 1, 9)`, about 24 cm per cell. A staff, spear or bow longer than 14.4 units (six cells) cannot be packed. Bags define their inner grid: backpack 8×5, satchel 6×3, sack 4×4, belt pouch 4×2.

`fb_items::profile::profile(base, &shape)` does this. Where it departs from the plate:

- **Stacks.** An `Item` has a `quantity` (at least 1, saved in `ItemRecord.quantity`, absent in old saves and written only above 1) and `Item::stack_limit`: arrows and bolts stack to 10 (`profile::AMMUNITION_STACK`), food and drink (anything `fb_items::provision` knows) take the goods table's `stack` column (bread 3, a vial 5, a large flask 2), and everything else holds 1. Eating and drinking take one off a stack (`Inventory::use_one`, through `take_one`), so a stack keeps its id and its quickslot until the last is gone. Bread and the rest only share a stack of one seed, so in play they seldom do; potions stack by size and brew (see Potions). `Item::can_stack_with` needs the same base, seed, shape and affixes, so one Armory model and one seed stand for the stack and two kinds of arrow never share one. `Inventory::stow` (so picking up) tops up stacks of the kind already carried, then fills places of its own ten at a time: 14 arrows make 10 and 4. A shot takes one piece off a stack (`Inventory::take_one`) and one that comes back folds into it. The cell shows the count in a badge and the card says "x10" with the stack's weight; weight counts every piece. Chest ammunition and an outfit's twenty come as one kind from one seed. Dropping a stack on another of its kind tops it up and leaves the rest where it was (`Inventory::merge_onto`). Pickup may fold the item into others (it is forgotten) or be partial (`Inventory::is_partial`: some stowed, the rest still lying). While bartering nothing folds (`stow_apart`), so Cancel finds every id; what was bought folds at settle. A stack is priced and scrapped by the piece and is never enchanted (`enchantable` wants one piece). A body's kit arrows lie as stacks (`fb_items::stack_up`). **Old saves:** an outfit and a chest now draw one seed for all its arrows, so levels not yet visited in an existing save roll slightly different kits and chests; visited levels keep what the record holds.
- **Quivers and bolt cases hold stacks.** A holder's place is one row of 3 to 5 stack places (`Grid { cols: 3..=5, rows: 1 }`), so 30 to 50 shots: a quiver of 4 takes 40 arrows and the 41st goes to the pack. The count comes from the holder's size (`Quivers::stacks`, `BoltCases::stacks`, from the mouth and length of the tube or the width, depth and length of the box), so a bigger quiver holds more and the same seed always holds the same. It takes only its own ammunition (a quiver arrows, a bolt case bolts; `Grid::accepts`), and a full one still tops up a partial stack but takes no new one. The card says "Holds 4 stacks, 32/40 arrows" and the quiver's doll slot carries the total count badge. The room figure uses the ammunition's own stack limit, not a fixed ten. The worn model draws the fraction of its visible arrows that the count is of the capacity (`combat::shoot`), not one drawn arrow per piece. A load puts every item back at its saved place and turn (`record::restore_contents`), for every bag, chest and quiver alike. Only one that cannot go there (an old quiver's twenty arrows, one seed each, in a row of stack places now) folds into a stack of its kind or takes a free place, and one with no room is never forgotten: it is listed in `Inventory::spill`, logged once, and set down at the delver's feet (`belongings::set_down_spill`). Arrows bought at a barter settle stay in the pack; they do not top up the quiver. A skeleton archer's few arrows are one kind, so they stack in its quiver. The icon of ammunition is a sheaf (`ItemMesh::fanned`): one arrow draws as itself, 2 to 5 as three fanned, 6 or more as five, laid slantwise and thickened so they read in one cell (one arrow in a cell was a hairline).
- A base with a row in `data/bases.json` or `data/goods.json` (Club, Torch, Bread, Rope...) takes its slot, tags, weight, armour, warmth, damage, light, stack and card line from that row. Every other base derives them from its shape as `saMeta` does.
- The footprint is measured from the built mesh (`ItemMesh::bounds`): rows from the longest extent, columns from the next. The plate measured only a few fields per family (blade plus grip, shield size) and fixed the rest. Garments, hoods, legwear, gloves, belts and bags fold, so they keep the plate's folded footprints instead of their size on the mannequin. Worn gear (helms, hoods, hats, garments, legwear, gloves, boots, belts, amulets, rings, charms and bags) and chests stand upright in the grids, as worn or as they stand (`fb_items::profile::stands_upright`), so their footprint runs the way the built shape stands: more columns than rows for a shape wider than tall, more rows for one taller than wide (`upright_footprint`). The cell count is unchanged; a pair of gloves, folded 2 by 1 on the plate, stands 1 by 2.
- The pack limit uses the same longest extent, so a staff's head or a bow's string counts.
- The plate's shields had tower, buckler and everything else; the port's pavise counts as a tower (armour 3, 15 lb).
- Chests cannot be packed, as the plate's `long` flag had it.

## Value

`Item::value()` gives what an item is worth in coins (`fb_items::value`); `Item::value_copper()` is the same in copper. The card shows it on its info line, before the cells ("Magic · Sword · 4 lb · 1d8 · 62 gp 3 sp · 1×4 cells").

- **Coins** convert at 1 gp = 10 sp = 100 cp (SRD 5.1 p. 62, Coins). `Coins::from_copper` and `total_copper` move between the two, `plus` (and `+`) adds with change, `minus` takes a price off with change and returns `None` when short (2 gp less 15 sp leaves 5 sp; 15 sp less 2 gp is `None`), and `Sum` over purses is a delver's wallet. `Display` still lists all three; `Coins::short` leaves out the empty ones for the card.
- **Base price** is in copper in `data/values.json`, one row per base, new and common. Where a base matches an SRD 5.1 equipment price it takes it (pp. 62 to 70): longsword 15 gp (Sword), dagger 2 gp, mace 5 gp, quarterstaff 2 sp (Staff), spear 1 gp, shortbow and light crossbow 25 gp, shield 10 gp, padded 5 gp (Gambeson), leather 10 gp (Leather jerkin), studded leather 45 gp (Brigandine), scale mail 50 gp (Scale coat), chain mail 75 gp (Mail), breastplate 400 gp (Cuirass), handaxe 5 gp, club 1 sp, backpack 2 gp, sack 1 cp, chest 5 gp, torch 1 cp, candle 1 cp, hooded lantern 5 gp, oil 1 sp, hempen rope 1 gp, thieves' tools 25 gp (Lockpicks), waterskin 2 sp, book 25 gp, paper 2 sp (Note), a scroll's sheet 1 gp, arrows and bolts 1 gp per twenty (5 cp), quiver 1 gp (and the bolt case), loaf of bread 2 cp, cheese 1 sp, mug of ale 2 cp (Tankard), wine 2 sp, potion of healing 50 gp (Healing draught and Potion), clothes 5 sp (Tunic) and traveller's clothes 2 gp (Cloak), robe 1 gp. The rest are our own: Helm, Mantle, Gloves, Gauntlets, Shoes, Boots, Satchel, Ring, Amulet, Charm, Mail coif, Pointed hat, Belt, Hood, Shirt, Trousers, Breeches, Leggings, Glowcap, Smoked fish, Meat pie, Sausages, Roast haunch, Blind cavefish, Bandages. A good-quality cover price for a good, not a market: no folk haggles yet. A new base needs a row (`every_base_has_a_price` fails otherwise).
- Camp goods and elixirs: bedroll 1 gp, tinderbox 5 sp and a two-person tent 2 gp (SRD 5.1 p. 68), a campfire kit 2 gp 1 sp (the SRD iron pot and 1 sp of kindling, our own); the Elixir of the Hare 25 gp, of Vigil and of Fasting 15 gp, of Embers 10 gp (our own, under the 50 gp healing potion).
- **Every factor is in `data/value_rules.json`** (`fb_items::value::RULES`), so the trade lab (#559) can tune them.
- **Rarity** multiplies it (our own): common 1, magic 2, rare 5, legendary 12. The first cut ran 1, 4, 20 and 200, which put a master's rare hauberk at about 2,000 gp; a rare sword is now about 120 gp and a magic piece two to three times its common base.
- **Affixes** multiply it (our own): each good affix by 1.15, 1.3, 1.5 or 1.8 by the least rarity it rolls on, each flaw by 0.7. Held-back affixes count like the rest. The product of all of an item's affixes is held between 0.4 and 2.0, so a heap of good affixes cannot run the top end away: a legendary sword tops out at 12 x 2 x 15 gp = 360 gp.
- **Purses** grow with depth: their coins times `1 + 0.25 (depth - 1)` (`coin_depth_step`), so the first level's purses are as they always were.
- **Condition** multiplies it (our own): `(1 - 0.6 wear) (1 - 0.4 damage)`, and a quarter of that for a snapped blade. Wear 0.7 leaves 58%. The Armory's rot and fray multipliers (and rust, which only shows on iron when raised, #795) already act through wear, so they are not counted twice.
- A value is never below 1 cp, except a purse: it is worth its coins, and nothing for the bag. A container's value leaves out what it holds; the contents are worth their own.

## Commissions

A smith, bowyer or tentmaker makes an item to order at the Armory's controls (`game/src/commission.rs`, with the field sorting and price in `fb_items::commission`). The screen is the Forge view's turntable and slider panel over the families of the maker's trade; `commission::open(trade, maker, faction)` (or an `OpenCommission` message) opens it, which the dialogue's Commission topic calls. `FB_COMMISSION=weaponsmith:master:blades` opens it for captures (README, "Debug switches").

- **Rolls** are a list down the left of the screen: six rows, each with the roll's icon, a line (its main material and length) and its price, and clicking one picks it. The list follows the family showing.
- **Families** are Armory bases: an armorer's Gambeson, Brigandine, Mail, Scale coat, Cuirass, Helm, Hood, Mail coif, Gloves, Gauntlets, Shoes, Boots and Shield; a weaponsmith's Sword, Dagger, Mace, Club, Hand axe, Spear and Staff; a bowyer's Bow, Crossbow, Arrow, Bolt, Quiver and Bolt case; a tentmaker's Tent and Campfire kit. The delver picks one, then one of the maker's six rolls of it (seeds drawn from the maker's name and the base, so a maker offers the same rolls every time), then shapes it. Items are made new, with no wear.
- **The tier sets the open controls**, a filter over `armory::Item::fields` by what each field is (`fb_items::commission::classify`):

| Tier | Open |
|---|---|
| Novice | the six rolls only |
| Apprentice | rolls, materials and colours |
| Expert | those, and every shape slider across the middle half of its range (`Slider::narrowed`, centred, same step) |
| Master | every range at full width, shape choices and parts switched on or off, etching, gems and inlay |

- **Which field is what.** Material: a choice named `material`, `*_material`, `metal`, `plate`, `sole`, `buckle`, `binding`, `trim`, `fittings`, `fabric`, `canvas`, `stuff`, `vessel` or `cover` (a part's options are cut to metal, bronze, gold, silver, wood, bone, stone, leather, string and cloth). Colour: a dye (`FieldKind::Colour`, an `Rgb` the reflection lists at `color`, `color2`, `*_color`, `*_cloth`, `ink`, a provision's `outer`, `inner`, `accent` and `liquid`, and the palette's wood, leather, cord and fletching tints), shown as a row of swatches (`tweak_panel::dye_palette`: the livery's tincture dyes, the garment dyes, undyed wool, browns, greys and blacks), and a choice named `*tincture*` or `*color*`. Ornament: the `etch`, `gem`, `stone` and `setting` sections, any `gem*` field, `inlay`, `stone`, `stones`, `device`, and the heraldry of a shield's `field` apart from its tinctures. Shape: every other slider, choice and toggle. A part that is switched off (a section whose `on` toggle is false), a gem's details while its toggle is off, the palette tints of what the thing is not made of, a cloth garment's body `material` and an armoured garment's `cloth` are not shown and do not count towards the price (`fb_items::commission::visible_fields`). A material's options are what the part is made of (`material_options`: metals for a blade, head, buckle or mail, leather as well for scales; leather, cloth, cord and wood for a grip or wrap; wood, bone and iron for a shaft). The condition is never open: a commissioned item is new. An expert sees no shape choices (a guard's style, parts on or off): those are the master's.
- **In the smith's own style the controls are one tier better** (a master stays master), for the families of that style: padded (Gambeson, Hood, Gloves, Shoes), leather (Brigandine, Hood, Gloves, Boots), mail (Mail, Mail coif), scale (Scale coat), plate (Cuirass, Helm, Gauntlets, Boots, Shield), blades (Sword, Dagger), axes and clubs (Hand axe, Club), maces (Mace), spears and staves (Spear, Staff). A novice bladesmith's sword opens an apprentice's panel; their mace does not.
- **Price** (our own) is the base's copper price from `data/values.json`, times a material factor: 1 plus each visible material choice's difference from iron, weighted by the part (blade, head, body, shaft 3; grip, wrap, belt, guard, pommel 0.5; buckle, trim 0.1; else 1) and divided by the total weight or 3 if more (iron 1, bronze 1.3, gold 12, silver 5, wood 0.6, bone 0.5, stone 0.4, leather 0.8, string 0.3, cloth 0.5), times a size factor (the square root of the shaped bounds' volume over a typical roll's, between 0.5 and 2.5), times 1.2 for etching and 1.25 for each gem or inlay switched on, times what the tier charges at the tier the controls open at (novice 1, apprentice 1.25, expert 1.6, master 2.5). It updates as the item is shaped and shows in coins.
- **Ready** after 4 game hours plus half the square root of the price in copper, at most 72.
- **Confirming** takes the whole price at once from the carried purses (`enchanting::pay`, all or nothing; there is no deposit) and the maker hands the finished item over on the spot, with a toast: into the pack (`Inventory::pick_up`), or set down at the delver's feet (`FloorChange::SetDown`) when there is no room. There is no wait and nothing is recorded in `WorldRecord::orders`. Short of coin, the screen stays open, shows "Needs 3 sp, have 12 cp." beside the price, and takes nothing. Cancelling or Esc closes the screen.

### Collecting

- **"Your order"** is a topic the maker offers (after Commission) only while they hold an order of the delver's (`Service::Order`). Orders are no longer left waiting, so this serves saves from before commissions were finished on the spot: only the folk who took the order, at the depth they stand, hand it over, whatever its old `ready_at`, with a toast, into the pack or underfoot as above. Several orders come over together. An order is handed over once and leaves the record.
- **A maker who has fallen** takes the order with them: nobody is left to ask, so it stays in the record unreachable and is lost. (Taking it from the body was considered and left out: the work is not on the body, only what it wore.)
- **The finished item** is `fb_items::commission::finish`, pure in the order. Its shape is the edited Armory shape, taken as it is and never re-rolled. Its rarity and affixes roll exactly as that maker's trader stock does (`fb_items::stock`): odds at the level's depth plus the grade's bonus, capped by the grade, so a novice's work is common, an apprentice's at best magic, an expert's and a master's at best rare (a master's at better odds), never legendary, and never flawed. It leaves the bench new, with the grade's finish wear. Its history ends with "Made by Oda, master bladesmith of the Brothers of the Last Door, for Wren." (the same noun a shelf's line uses, then ", for" and the delver's name). Its seed is salted so its icon is cached apart from a found item's of the same base and seed.
- **It survives records exactly.** Once handed over it is an ordinary item: the record keeps its Armory state, rarity, affixes and history lines, and rebuilds it with `Item::from_parts`, nothing rolled again.
- `FB_COMMISSION_ORDER=ready|<hours>|collect` places an order with the nearest maker on the level for captures, without taking coin (an older save's waiting order: `ready` is finished already, hours is how long it was to wait; `collect` hands the item over into the pack at once); `FB_COMMISSION_BASE`, `FB_COMMISSION_ROLL` and `FB_COMMISSION_SET` shape it, and `FB_TALK=1 FB_TALK_TOPIC=4` chooses "Your order".

## Scrapping

`fb_items::scrap::scrap(&Item)` says what an item breaks into: a `Salvage { essence, materials }`, or `None` for what is not gear (food, purses, bags, lights). An enchanter spends the result (`EnchantingStock::spend` fails and takes nothing when short). All the numbers are our own, in `data/scrap.json`; the plate had no scrapping.

- **Essence** by rarity: common 2, magic 8, rare 30, legendary 120. Each good affix adds by the least rarity it rolls on: 1, 2, 4 or 8. Each flaw halves the total, so a flawed item gives less than the same one unflawed. Never below 1.
- **Materials** come only from rare and legendary weapons, one of each that applies: Gravesteel sliver (blade), Ossuary dust (blunt), Wraith sinew (ranged), then one for each good affix group the weapon carries, Blood salt (damage), Seer's glass (to hit), Quicksilver bead (attack rate) and Wardstone chip (any bane). A legendary also gives a Heartstone. An enchanter can ask for these by id for legendary-tier affixes and for legendaries.
- A pure function of the item, with no randomness.

| Item | Essence | Materials |
|---|---|---|
| Common cloak | 2 | none |
| Magic cloak, one common affix | 9 | none |
| Rare sword | 30 plus affixes | Gravesteel sliver, and one for each affix group |
| Legendary sword | 120 plus affixes | the same, and a Heartstone |

The game keeps the delver's `EnchantingStock` in the `Enchanting` resource for the delve: it resets when a run begins. `enchanting::scrap_item` takes an item out of the inventory and adds its salvage (a bag with something in it is refused); the enchanter's talk (#350) will call it. The character sheet shows a line "Essence 38 · Gravesteel sliver ×1" under "enchanting".

## Enchanting

`fb_items::enchant` changes what an item carries at an enchanter's bench. Every function is pure and rebuilds the item through `Item::from_parts`, so the stats follow the affixes and the record of it round-trips. The numbers are our own, in `data/enchant.json`, kept apart from the item prices in `value.rs`.

- **Adding.** `addable(item, tier)` lists good affixes from the pools the item could roll from (its tags and slot, `on`, `not` and `at`, held-back ones left out), none of a group it already has, with room left: at most one good prefix and two good suffixes, three good in all. `add(item, affix, &Enchanter)` puts one on.
- **Rarity follows the good affixes**: none is common, one magic, two rare, three legendary. Adding raises it; stripping lowers it. An affix rolls only on its rarity or better (`least`), so it can only be added where the count it makes is at least that: of the Bat (legendary tier) goes on an item that already has two good affixes, and makes it legendary. A legendary made this way gets a legend as if rolled.
- **Tiers.** A novice only strips flaws. An apprentice strips anything and adds magic-tier affixes (those that roll on a common or magic item), so makes magic items at most. An expert adds rare-tier ones and makes rare items, a master legendary-tier ones and legendary items. A master alone makes a rare item a legend (below); a legendary itself cannot be changed ("A legend cannot be changed.").
- **Stripping.** `removable(item, tier)` and `remove(item, affix, &Enchanter)`. A flaw is cheap to take off; stripping Rusted also takes the rust off the shape. The item keeps the condition it has earned: only the difference between a fresh piece dressed for the old rarity and affixes and one dressed for the new moves its wear, rust, patina, rot and fray (stripping Rusted takes off the rust and the 0.75 wear the flaw set, and any wear beyond that stays); nicks and breaks stay, and the etching follows the new rarity.
- **Reforging.** `reforge(item, seed, &Enchanter)` rolls every affix again at the item's rarity from the same pools as a fresh roll, flaws possible at the rarity's chance. It tries a few seeds so the result differs from what it had when it can. A common item has nothing to reforge, and a novice cannot.
- **History.** Each change adds a line: "Enchanted by Ysolde, expert enchanter of the Ash Rite: of the Hawk.", "Stripped by ...: Rusted.", "Reforged by ...."
- **Making legends.** `make_legendary(item, affix, &Enchanter, level)` is a master's work on a rare item: a lesser enchanter is told "Only a master enchanter can make a legend.", a common or magic item "Only a rare item can be made legendary.", and a legendary "A legend cannot be changed." (the bench's Strip, Reforge, Add and Legend lines all say so for one) `legendary_options` lists the affixes the item could take as its third good one (any it could roll, whatever tier: the legendary-tier ones are few and a cloak, for one, takes none); the item gets the one chosen, or none when it has no room, and is raised to legendary whatever its count (a rolled legendary of a narrow slot has fewer than three too), its etching going gold-inlaid with its rarity. It is given a title, "The <adjective> <epithet>" or the enchanter's own, "Ysolde's Shroud": the enchanter put the legend there, so they are the owner it names (the delver's name is not known to the item). Its legend (`Legend::made`) tells its making and not a found relic's story, with no carrier and no loss: the item's own first history line ("Made by ..."), or "Found in the abyss.", then "Made legendary by Ysolde, master enchanter of the Ash Rite on level 4.", then "It was a rare <name> before."; `Item::in_world` leaves a made legend as it is. `legend_cost` is 120 essence, 3000 cp and one Heartstone, plus the material of the chosen affix's group where it has one (Blood salt, Seer's glass, Quicksilver bead, Wardstone chip), then scaled by the master as adding is: 84 essence and 75 gp. In the bench it is a row of the Add list for each option, "Make legendary with ..." (or a single "Make legendary" for an item with none), priced by `legend_cost`; a bench that cannot make the item legendary lists none and has no legend area. `FB_ENCHANT=legendary` drives it for captures.
- **Costs** (`add_cost`, `remove_cost`, `reforge_cost`), essence, coin and, for legendary-tier affixes, one special material. By the affix's tier (the rarity it rolls from) and scaled by the enchanter: a better enchanter wastes less essence and charges more coin.

| Work | Essence | Coin | Material |
|---|---|---|---|
| Add a common-tier affix | 6 | 40 cp | none |
| Add a magic-tier affix | 10 | 100 cp | none |
| Add a rare-tier affix | 24 | 300 cp | none |
| Add a legendary-tier affix | 60 | 1000 cp | one by its group (below) |
| Strip a flaw | 4 | 20 cp | none |
| Strip a good affix | half of adding it | half | none |
| Reforge a magic item | 5 | 60 cp | none |
| Reforge a rare item | 18 | 200 cp | none |

By enchanter: essence ×1.25 novice, ×1.0 apprentice, ×0.85 expert, ×0.7 master; coin ×0.6, ×1.0, ×1.5, ×2.5. An expert stripping Rusted pays 3 essence and 30 cp; reforging a rare item is cheaper than choosing even one affix of it. Legendary-tier materials are Blood salt for damage, Seer's glass for to hit, Quicksilver bead for attack rate, Wardstone chip for any bane, and a Heartstone for the rest (the ones scrapping gives).

In the game, the Enchant topic of an enchanter's talk opens the bench (`game/src/enchanter.rs`); there is no separate Scrap topic. The left lists the gear the delver carries, richest first, under two tabs: Equipped (worn or held) and Inventory (packed); picking an item shows the tab it is listed under. The right shows the picked item drawn from its Armory mesh (the inventory's icon), marked Equipped when worn or held, with its card, a Strip button on each affix, Reforge and the affixes the enchanter could add, each with its cost, and the card stays up as the result with its new history line. Costs come out of the delver's `Enchanting` stock and their carried purses (`enchanting::do_work`; there is no wallet yet, so coin is paid out of purses, the first ones first); short, nothing is spent and the screen says what is lacking. Scrap is an action on the card, after Strip and Reforge and before Add, shown only for gear that breaks into something (not a purse, not a bag with things in it) and naming the essence and materials it would give; scrapping removes the item, adds the salvage to the stock and the right shows what it gave. `FB_ENCHANT=<equipped|inventory|select|strip|reforge|offer|legendary|sword|scrap>` with `FB_TALK=1 FB_TALK_TOPIC=<n>` (or `FB_ENCHANT_OPEN=<novice|apprentice|expert|master>`, which opens the bench with no talk) fills the pack and stock and drives the bench for captures. The inventory icon is keyed by base, seed and the item's history length, so it is drawn again after enchanting. Payment is all or nothing: the new purses are worked out first, and stock and purses change only if the whole cost is covered.

## Stock

`fb_items::stock::stock(&Shop, depth, seed)` rolls what a trader has to sell, a pure function of its arguments. `Trade::shop(maker, faction)` in `fb_faction` makes the `Shop` from a folk's trade (the types are mirrored in `fb_items` because `fb_faction` sits above it in the crate graph). `cargo run -p fb_faction --example stock -- armorer master mail 8` prints a shelf with values.

- **Lines.** Armorer: body armour, helms, coifs, gauntlets, boots, shields. Weaponsmith: swords, daggers, maces, clubs, axes, spears, staves. Bowyer: bows, crossbows, arrows, bolts, quivers, bolt cases. Tentmaker: tents, bedrolls, campfire kits, tinderboxes. General goods: lanterns, torches, lamp oil, candles, food, drink, waterskins, rope, bags, pouches. Alchemist: potions, healing draughts, elixirs. Enchanters and guards sell nothing. Held-back bases ([`HELD_BACK`]) are stocked here: traders are where they come from.
- **Rarity** is capped by the grade through `Odds`: novice common, apprentice magic, expert and master rare, never legendary. An expert rolls one level deeper than the level and a master three, so a master's rare pieces come at better odds (our own).
- **Size** grows with the grade: 3 to 4 pieces for a novice, 5 to 6 an apprentice, 7 to 8 an expert, 9 to 10 a master (our own).
- **Style.** In a smith's own style, three pieces in four are that style's bases (mail: Mail and Mail coif; plate: Cuirass, Helm, Gauntlets; scale: Scale coat, Brigandine; leather: Leather jerkin, Boots; padded: Gambeson; blades: Sword, Dagger; axes and clubs: Hand axe, Club; maces: Mace; spears and staves: Spear, Staff). The fourth comes from the whole line, so a mailwright's shelf is mostly mail with a shield or boots among it.
- **Maker's line.** Every piece's history ends with "Made by Bera, master mailwright of the Ash Choir." The noun is our own per profession and style: quilter, leatherworker, mailwright, scalesmith, platesmith, bladesmith, axewright, macesmith, spearwright, then armorer, weaponsmith, bowyer, tentmaker, alchemist. A general goods merchant sells what others made, so "Sold by Orla, novice general goods merchant of the Ash Choir."
- **New from the bench.** Stock is made, not found: each piece is reset to pristine (so no age from its rarity and no rust, fray or rot from a flaw), then given a little finish wear by grade, a novice's 0.10 to 0.20 (it can read as serviceable), an apprentice's 0.02 to 0.06, an expert's and a master's 0.0 to 0.02 (our own). A piece that rolled a flaw or an affix that changes its condition is rolled again from the next seed, a few tries, so the shelf keeps its size.
- **Restock.** In the game, a tradesperson's `Shelf` is rolled from their spot seed turned by the game day (`game/src/shelf.rs`), so it restocks each game day and not before. Leaving a level writes each live trader's shelf and its day into the level's record (`LevelRecord::shelves`, items as `ItemRecord`s, so rarity, affixes and history come back as they were); a revisit the same day shows the same shelf, a later day rolls a new one. Buying and selling (#351) will change the shelf and so the record.
- For captures, `FB_DROP=@armorer/master/mail/8` sets the first three pieces of that stock down in front of the delver.

## Slots

`data/slots.json` lists the slots: head, neck, shirt, shoulders (cloak), armour (body), main hand, hands, feet, back, belt, off hand, legs, two rings, a charm, the hip, beside the belt, the quiver, over the back's slot, and the satchel, below the quiver, none of which the plate had. A quiver or bolt case is slung only in the quiver slot, which needs no belt, so it never vies with a lantern at the hip or a pack on the back; its grid shows with the other carried containers, and arrows or bolts picked up go into it first. A bow or crossbow draws only from a worn quiver or bolt case. A satchel has a slot of its own, so one is worn beside a pack or sack: the back slot holds the pack or the sack (a second one cannot be worn there, and picking one up while the back is taken stows it), the satchel slot the satchel. Both grids show with the carried containers, the back's first. On the body (`fb_body::worn`) the pack rides on the back and the satchel hangs at the hip on the wearer's left, its back against the side, from a strap over the other shoulder and across the chest; it keeps clear of the pack, which stays within the shoulders' width. The slot is `satchel` on disk and last in the `Slot` enum, so saves keep their slot order and names; a save from before it holds the satchel in `back`, and loading moves a worn item that does not fit the back but fits the satchel slot there, while a pack or sack stays on `back` (`game/src/save.rs`, `current_slot`). Of the Ox rolls on a satchel as on a pack. `FB_DEBUG_KIT` also hangs a satchel. Rings fill whichever ring slot is free. Any weapon (sword, dagger, mace, club, axe, spear, staff, bow, crossbow) can be held in either hand (`fb_items::Item::slots`), the user's call: the plate held weapons only in the main hand. Equipped without saying where (a double-click, F, a drop on the doll) it takes the main hand if that is free, else the off hand if that is, else it swaps out the main hand's (`Inventory::target_slot`). Only the main hand swings; an off-hand weapon is held and drawn but does not attack until the character simulation is designed (#122). A two-handed weapon (a staff, spear, bow or crossbow) in the off hand is held in the left hand alone, as the doll holds it in the right, so a staff in the off hand beside a sword in the main hand is drawn in both fists; this is our own choice until two-handed holds are designed.

A lantern can go in either hand, or hang from the belt at the hip (the user's call). The hip takes only a lantern, and only while a belt is worn ("It hangs from a belt. Wear one first."); taking the belt off moves the lantern into the first carried bag with room, or else sets it down underfoot (`Inventory::unhang`). F or a double-click on a lantern puts it in a free hand, the off hand first, then at the hip, and swaps out the off hand's only when both hands are full and no belt is worn. T, Hang at hip (`interact::Verb::HangAtHip`, on Special because R turns a dragged item in the inventory), sends it straight to the hip from a bag, a hand or the floor, swapping out what hung there; it is offered only while a belt is worn and the lantern is not already there. A lit lantern at the hip gives its light whether or not a hand holds a lit thing (`carry::carried_lights`), and is the carried light whenever no hand holds one (`carry::carried_light`), so both hands can hold weapons. On the doll it hangs plumb from the belt on the off hand's side, a little toward the front (`fb_body::fit_hip`), and lights that side of the doll and the seal. In first person it hangs from the player's body in the level, not on the view model's stage: it is seen at the bottom of the view when looking down, swings a little with the steps (`view_model::hip_swing`), and its light rises from the hip, lower than a held light's, lighting the floor round the feet and, by a light of its own on the stage, what the hands hold from below. The swing and the stage light's place are our own tuning.

## Containers

A bag keeps its contents wherever it goes, and its weight counts through every bag you carry. A bag set down opens underfoot as its own grid. A bag cannot go inside itself or inside a bag it already holds.

The dead keep their gear. A slain folk, denizen or skeleton stays dressed in what it wore and held, and nothing is set on the floor (`game/src/remains.rs`). Its things are a "Body" container in the inventory: F ("Search") near the body opens its grid as a window beside the pack, which takes things too long to pack (a spear) that a pack would refuse. A worn bag in that window (a satchel, pack, belt pouch or quiver) opens as a window of its own with a click, so its contents come out without the bag, and what hangs on a pack or belt (its lashing, strap, frogs and pouch grids, "Pack and belt accessories" below) opens with it and is drawn on the body only while the pack or belt is on it. Anything dragged out goes where it is dropped and leaves the body's drawn kit at once. The level record keeps what is left on each body and where each thing lay and in what slot, so the body comes back still dressed in what was not taken; a body with nothing left comes back bare. The windows close when the delver walks away from the body.

The Armory says where a container's contents go: each container family has a `container(&state)` returning an `armory::build::container::ContainerSpace` (the mouth's centre, facing and size, the interior bounds, and the capacity in litres) in the same item space as its mesh. The game can drop items through the mouth or draw an open container's contents inside it. The capacity is the real volume of the modelled body; the inner grid sizes above stay a game rule.

Departures from the prototype's bags: the front pocket and lashing roll only on backpacks and spilled coins only on pouches and purses (the prototype rolled them everywhere but drew them only there); a sewn patch can go on any bag, not just sacks; cloth bags take any woven pattern and leather ones can be studded. As in the prototype, the pattern goes on the body (and a backpack's pocket) in the second colour, while the flap, patch and bedroll stay plain in that colour. Every panel has a real thickness, straps lie on the surfaces they cross and close with buckles, and a satchel's strap hangs from D-rings.

Departures from the prototype's chests: each style rolls only the options it shows. Iron hoops go on barrels, shipping straps and rope handles on crates (the prototype ignored `bands` and `handles` there), and `lock` is an amphora's lid. Coffers and strongboxes can stand open as well as chests. A coffer's crowning stone is a gemstone in the state, not the second colour, and stencils can go on barrels as well as crates. A lidded box is hollow, with walls, a rim and a darker inside, so an open lid shows a real interior with the hoard heaped in it. Its lid turns on hinges and carries its hasp and bands. An amphora is clay (the stone material) tinted with its glaze colour and painted with its glaze pattern; the glaze is a `Fabric`, like a bag's cloth, so both reach the shader as a `SurfacePattern`. Stencils are the paint material.

Chests, barrels, crates and amphorae standing in a level (`docs/design.md`, "Props in the level") open into grids of their own (no chest lies about as loose loot). The plate gives chests no inner grid (its `saMeta` returns none for `chests`), so the sizes are our own proposal (`fb_items::profile::chest_grid`): chest 8×4, crate 6×4, barrel 4×5, strongbox 5×3, coffer 4×2, amphora 3×4. What a standing container holds is rolled from its loot seed (`fb_items::roll_contents`), also our own proposal: chests keep purses, weapons, trinkets, clothes, draughts and candles; strongboxes and coffers purses and trinkets; barrels food and drink; crates torches, rope, candles, oil, bandages and the odd tool or garment; amphorae wine, potions and oil. Chests also keep the odd quiver or bolt case, and chests and crates keep arrows and bolts by the sheaf, six to twelve to a pick, since a bow shoots only from a worn quiver and one arrow is no use. Each thing is rolled through `roll_item` like any loot, so gear among it takes rarity and affixes, and anything with no room left in the grid is left out. A container itself is a common good too heavy to carry (`fb_items::container_item`). Double-clicking something in a container you are not carrying takes it into your carried grids; that is this build's own rule.

Archers keep what they shoot. A folk archer's outfit rolls a hip quiver or bolt case and twenty pieces, and a skeleton with a bow carries a quiver with four to twelve weathered arrows left (`fb_items::undead::ARROWS_LEFT`). When either is slain the arrows fall inside the quiver rather than scattered round the body (`inventory::underfoot::set_down_around`). The rest of a folk outfit's goods (its purse, food and draughts) are still not carried or dropped.

### Pack and belt accessories

A pack and a belt can roll with attachment points: a pack's lashing and weapon strap (#719) and a belt's two frogs, potion pouch and sack (#722). They are rolled variants in the Armory (`armory::families::bags::Bag::lashing` and `weapon_strap`, `armory::sections::belt::BeltFittings`), seeded with the pack or belt, listed as `Accessory::Lashing`, `WeaponStrap`, `LeftFrog`, `RightFrog`, `PotionPouch` and `BeltSack` (`Item::accessories()`, `has_accessory()`), never found loose, bought apart or added by the delver, and they last as long as the pack or belt. A fitting never comes with anything on it: nothing is rolled, spawned, dropped, sold or found tied or hung. The lashing takes the place of the old drawn-on bedroll in the bag's roll stream (a saved bag's `bedroll` field still loads as `lashing`); a belt's potion pouch and sack are the last two draws in its stream, so no other seed's belt moved. Over 2,000 seeds (our own numbers): 60% of backpacks have a lashing and 35% a strap; 20% of belts have frogs (one 11%, two 9%), 23% a potion pouch (a pouch belt 50%, a girdle 10%, the rest 20%) and 20% a sack.

Each fitting but the sack is an item slot of its own that belongs to its pack or belt and shows with the main grid in the container's window (#791), under its label: "Lashing", "Strap", "Left frog", "Right frog", "Potion pouch". A slot is framed and drawn as the doll's slots are (`inventory::doll::slot_contents` and `slot_node`: the item's icon fitted inside it, a thin long one on the diagonal), big for the lashing, strap and frogs and small for the pouch's three, and the slots sit in a column beside the main grid when that is no taller than the grid, else in a row under it (`bags::fittings_layout`), so the window is barely bigger than the grid. They are not places on the doll (`data/slots.json` has none, and there is no `Slot` for them): the delver opens the pack or belt, by a click on it as for any bag, and fills and empties the slots like any other. Dropping a bedroll from the pack's main grid onto its lashing slot ties it on (`Target::Fitting`, a drop target by the same `ui_cursor` path as a grid).

- **What each slot takes** (`fb_items::fitting_grid`, `Item::fits_accessory`, the one place that decides). A slot holds one whole item of its kind whatever its size, with no footprint fit at all: the hidden grid behind it is large enough for anything accepted (10 by 10; 6 by 4 for the pouch, `fb_items::fittings` tests it), so a put never refuses for room. Lashing: one bedroll or packed tent (`Item::lashable()`, from `fb_items::camp::Camp`: the kind decides, never size, and never one laid out or pitched or another camp good; add a bundle to `Camp` and it can be lashed). Strap: one long, thin weapon (`Item::strappable()`: at least four cells long by its footprint, `STRAP_CELLS`, so a staff, spear, bow or long sword and a polearm, never a dagger, club, hand axe or crossbow; the user chose long and thin only, though a slot could now take a crossbow). Each frog: one one-handed weapon (`Item::frogable()`: a dagger, sword, mace, club or axe at most five cells long by its footprint, `FROG_CELLS`, so a greatsword is refused and a longsword or mace taken). Potion pouch: three slots for vials or small potions (`fits_potion_pouch`), one piece to a slot; a stack dropped on one gives one piece and keeps the rest where it was. A slot takes only its own kind ("That does not go in there."). Dropping on a full slot swaps (`Inventory::fit`, as the doll's `equip`): the held item goes back where the dragged one came from if it fits there, else is stowed in a carried bag, else set down underfoot; while bartering a full slot refuses, and a shelf stack is not split into the pouch.
- **They stay with the pack or belt.** Each fitting is a hidden container item that the inventory makes when it adds the pack or belt (`Inventory::fittings_of`, `fitting`, `fitting_host`; `fb_items::fitting_item`), so what it holds is kept as a bag's contents are: wherever the pack or belt goes (worn, in another bag, set down underfoot, in a chest, on a body, on the floor of a level) its tent stays tied, its spear strapped, its sword in its frog and its potions in the pouch. Taking the pack or belt off, setting it down, handing it over or looting it moves nothing out of its fittings, so there is no unfitting, and no line about a weapon slipping from the strap. The grids are not items: `Inventory::ids` leaves them out, and `forget` forgets them with the pack or belt. A fitting's grid counts as carried when its pack or belt does (`is_carried`, `holds`).
- **Load.** What hangs on a pack or belt counts in its contents' weight (`Inventory::contents_weight`), so in the load ("... on the strap", "on the lashing", "in a frog", "in the potion pouch" in the breakdown, `Character::load_explained`), but gives none of its stats, affixes or light, and cannot strike: it is carried, not held. `Inventory::holding` counts what is in a container and in its fittings, which is what "empty" means for selling, scrapping and the card ("Holds 2 things").
- **Drawing.** Drawing a weapon from a strap or frog (a quickslot, or dragging it to a hand) puts the hand's old weapon where the new one came from if it fits, else into a bag with room, else underfoot. A lashed bedroll or tent is laid out or pitched where it hangs (F in the inventory, the same verbs and checks as one in a grid; `camp::lay_out`), and when packed up again it is tied back on the lashing it came from if that is free, else packed into a bag (`Inventory::lashed_out`, `Inventory::lash`, saved with the camp as `SavedDelve::lashed`). There is no sleeping on the pack itself.
- **Quick drinking.** A potion in the pouch is carried: a quickslot binds it, and H drinks from the pouch first (`eat::nearest_healing`), through `Inventory::use_one`, which frees the place of the last piece.
- **On the body.** `fb_body::fit_lashed`, `fit_strapped` and `fit_frogged` draw what is in the worn pack's and belt's fitting grids (`Inventory::worn_hung`, passed to `Figure::dress_hung` and `fb_body::fit_worn_hung` as `(Accessory, Item)`): the lashing's bundle scaled to fill the hoops across the top of the pack, the strap's weapon upright along the pack's side, leaning outward, its butt a little below the lower band, and a weapon in each frog hung through its sleeve (a blade hilt up, a mace head up, leaning back, and further on a short body so the tip clears the floor), with a worn cloak lying over it. They ride the trunk or pelvis and turn with it, on the doll and on folk; the potions in the pouch are not drawn. Nothing is drawn in first person (the pack is behind the view). A pack lying on the floor or packed in a bag is drawn plain, without what hangs on it: the Armory's pack takes no bundle, so a tent tied to a pack set down shows only in the pack's window (a gap, not a rule). In the inventory a thin long item's icon lies on its grid's diagonal. A pack's card ends its contents line with what it has: "with a lashing, a weapon strap".
- **Bartering.** A pack or belt with anything on its fittings is refused for sale ("Empty it first.", `Refusal::HoldsThings`) like a bag with things in it, rather than priced with what hangs on it: the trader's shelf has no place to price a tent as part of a pack. Emptied it is a ware like any pack, and cancelling a deal puts back a pack or belt that was moved with its fittings (`barter::end`); nothing is moved out of the fittings while a deal is open.
- **Saves and the level record.** What a pack or belt holds in its fittings is recorded with it, after its main grid's contents: a `Stowed` item carries `fitting` (the accessory) and its cell in that grid (`record::record_contents`, `restore_contents`), so it goes wherever the pack goes in the record (worn, in a bag, set down, in a chest, on a body) and comes back where it hung. A thing whose fitting is gone, or that finds no place, is kept: it goes into the container's main grid, else to the spill, set down underfoot, never lost. `save::walk` numbers them in record order, so a quickslot bound to a potion in the pouch is found again. There is no migration: the fittings were never saved as worn slots outside the branch that introduced them, and a save without them loads as before.
- **Bodies.** A body's pack and belt open as windows with their fitting grids (a click on the pack in the body's window), so the tent and spear on a dead pack can be taken. A fallen body is drawn with what hangs on its pack and belt while they are on it (`remains::hung_kit`, `Person::hung`), and what hangs on a pack taken from the body goes with it from the picture.
- **Small sack.** A small sack hung behind a belt's left hip adds a row of four cells to the belt's grid, a 2 by 2 worth (`profile::BELT_SACK_ROWS`). It is not a fitting grid of its own: the inventory keeps one grid for each container, so the sack widens the belt's main grid, and capacity affixes add their rows to it.
- **Frogs and the Hip.** The Hip is not a frog. It stays the lantern's, a place any worn belt gives with no fitting at all (`Slot::Hip`, above), and the frogs are weapon grids only a belt rolled with them gives, so a lantern at the hip and a sword in a frog are both carried.
- **Captures.** The turntable shows them with `--family bags --seed 8` (a lashing; 4, 9, 13 and 14 carry a strap) and `--family belt --seed 91` (91 and 95 carry all four; 1 has two frogs and a pouch). `FB_PACK=strap|lashing|both|plain` and `FB_BELT=frogs|potions|sack|all` pick the pack and belt `FB_EQUIP="Pack,Belt"` puts on the delver, and `FB_HANG="Tent,Spear,Mace,Vial"` hangs one of each from the first fitting that takes it, for an inventory capture with `FB_OPEN_BAGS=1`. The doll view takes `--wear back=Pack,lash=Tent,strap=Spear,belt=Belt,frog1=Sword,frog2=Mace` (`lash=empty` for a pack with a lashing and nothing tied).

## The inventory screen

I opens the plate's inventory leaf over the level (`game/src/inventory/`): a parchment ledger with each carried container as its grid, items drawn as icons at their footprints, dragged between cells and grids and turned with R or the right mouse button, as in the plate's `renderGrids` and drag code. The level keeps drawing behind it; the mouse is freed and the body stops. The grid rules (`inventory::grid`) and where every item is (`inventory::items::Inventory`: worn, in a container's grid at a cell and turn, or loose on the floor) are plain Rust. Where it departs from the plate:

- X drops the carried or worn item under the mouse (rebindable, "Drop" in the options): it is set down underfoot as dragging it to the underfoot grid's first free spot does, a bag with what it holds, and at the delver's feet if that grid is full. The item's card lists the key.
- What each hand holds is drawn in the level's view model, the main hand's low on the right and the off hand's low on the left, fitted as on the doll (`docs/body.md`, "In first person"); equipping something in the inventory swaps it at once.
- Torches and lanterns are lit and put out by the player (`armory::kindle`, `game/src/kindle.rs`). L lights or puts out the carried one, the main hand's, else the off hand's, else the hip lantern; Shift+L the hip lantern alone; and with the inventory open, L acts on the torch or lantern under the mouse, wherever it lies, its card saying "Burning \u{b7} L puts it out" and the like. A fresh or doused torch lights and burns; put out, it is doused; a spent torch will not light, and the toast says so. A lantern is lit or unlit. The state is the item's own, so it goes wherever the item goes: a lantern put out and set down is still out when picked up. Whether it is lit decides whether it gives light in a hand, at the hip or on the floor.
- A flask of lamp oil refills lanterns (`fb_items::oil`, `kindle::refill_and_say`), the goods table's "six hours of lantern light", which the plate never used. Its fill is its oil, and a full flask is one full lantern's worth, so it fills any lantern from empty whatever its `BurnTime`, which decides how long that oil then burns. F on the flask in the inventory, or its quickslot key, pours into the lanterns in the main hand, the off hand and at the hip, in that order, each as full as the oil goes; a lantern in a bag or on the floor is not filled. The verb is offered only while one of them has room ("F Refill"). Refilling does not light the lantern, but an empty one lights again. Dregs too little to pour again go in with the rest, and the emptied flask is kept, as a drained waterskin is; a quickslot moves to the next full flask carried, or empties. Every lantern takes oil, the candle lanterns included: all of them burn the same six hours of fuel for now (our own).
- Every lit thing carried gives its own shadowed light in the world (`carry::carried_lights`): a lit lantern at the hip shines beside a lit torch in hand, and the header's light is the farthest reaching of them. Three carried lights cost about 3 ms a frame over one in the start room (11.7 ms to about 14.5), which holds well above 60 fps; each keeps its shadows, so none reaches through rock.
- The carried light in the level follows the hands (`carry::carried_light`): the lit torch or lantern in the main hand, or in the off hand if the main hand holds none lit, is drawn in the view and is the light the delver carries. Its reach is the Armory shape's light scaled by the item's light stat against its base's (`Item::light_factor`), so Gleaming carries half again as far and Smoky a quarter less; a lit thing on the floor is scaled the same. Torches and lanterns are made for the off hand in `data/bases.json`, so it is usually the off hand's, drawn low on the left; the plate's doll also lit from the off hand (`light: oh ? oh.light : 0`). With nothing lit in hand there is no carried light: the level is lit only by its braziers, glowcaps and lit things lying on the floor, and is black elsewhere (darkvision is not drawn yet). The delver starts with a burning torch in the off hand; the "Freshly fallen" outfit's own spare torch is unlit and goes in the pouch.
- The delver (`game/src/character.rs`) is made in the title's character creator (`game/src/title/creator.rs`), or continued from the last delve. The creator rolls as the plate's `rollSpec` rolls a character (a folk, a body seed, a name from `data/syl.json`, a class, the ability scores and their dirt), and the player can change the folk, class and name and roll the scores and the look again. The scores are kept as nudges on the folk's own, so a new folk keeps the roll. A delve begun without the title (`--seed`, a debug switch) rolls its delver from the level's seed as before. They start with almost nothing: the "Freshly fallen" outfit's tunic and trousers (rolled through `fb_items::outfit`), a belt whose 4×2 pouch is the first grid, and a burning torch in the off hand, all common. The outfit's spare torch and bread are not given to the delver, though folk in that outfit still carry them. The plate started a character in their class's outfit; here the class outfit is only what `FB_DEBUG_KIT` dresses them in.
- Carried grids are the containers worn on the back, at the satchel slot and on the belt, in that order (the plate's `containersOf`). Picking up a bag whose slot is free puts it on, so a pack found on the floor becomes a grid at once; anything else goes into the first grid with room for it, upright first and then turned (the plate's `stow` and `place`). Something with no room for it, or too long to pack, stays on the floor. Wearing a bag on pickup is this build's own rule.
- The carried list in the corner of the HUD, and choosing from it with the number keys and the wheel and setting down with G, are gone: the screen does all of that.
- Underfoot is a grid, where the plate's was a loose list of 58 px tiles (`.ground`): the items on the floor within reach of the eye (the 2 m pickup reach), nearest first, packed six columns wide at their first free spots, deeper if they need it. With nothing in reach it still shows its empty six by three grid, under "Bare stone. Drop things here.", so there is always a place to set things down. Its layout is only a view of the floor, so dropping one floor item on another cell does nothing. Each bag among them opens as its own grid beside it, "Sack · underfoot", as the plate did. Every item lying in the level is kept track of in the `Inventory` as loose, so a set-down bag's grid keeps its contents under its id.
- The left column of grids (every carried bag, an open container and underfoot) scrolls on its own, under the header, when it is taller than the screen (`ScrollColumn`): the wheel over it moves it, and an item held near its top or bottom edge scrolls it, faster the closer to the edge, so something can be carried from a top bag to underfoot below the fold. The doll, header and quickslots stay put, and nothing scrolls, and no scrollbar shows, while everything fits. The screen is rebuilt whenever the inventory changes, so the offset is kept in `ColumnScroll` and put back on the new column. Drop targets are read from each grid's laid-out rectangle, which follows the scroll, and a grid only counts where it is visible in the column, so a cell scrolled out beneath the header is not a target.
- A container opens as a window (`inventory::bags`). Double-clicking a bag the delver carries (the pack, the satchel, the belt pouch worn on the doll, or a sack inside one) opens its grid as a window on the right, and double-clicking it again, or the cross on its title bar, closes it; a double-click on a worn bag no longer takes it off (drag it off). A bag set down underfoot or standing in the level still opens on a single click, counted once the double-click interval has passed with no second click, so taking it up with a double-click never opens a window. The windows lie in the right region (`bags::regions`, below), which `column_layout` lays out in columns, never over the doll or each other: oldest first, each under the one before, a new column to the right when the next would pass the bottom, and when they cannot all fit their cells shrink (`fit_windows`: 1, .85, .75, .65, .55, .45 of the screen's), and only when even the smallest do not all fit is the oldest hidden. A hidden window stays in `BagWindows.order`, the remembered set, and shows again by itself when room frees (a search window closes, another bag is closed). The region runs the screen's full height less a margin. Hit-testing and the drag ghost use the shrunk cell for a grid in a window. The screen's layout size is that of the view the interface is drawn in under `UiScale`, so it is 1920 by 1080 at any 16:9 window size. Items drag between windows: a window is tested first, before the doll, the quickslots and the grids behind it, so it takes any drop over it (a grid in it, else the page) and a press on its title bar starts no item drag underneath. What is open is kept in `BagWindows` for the delve (reset in `leave_the_delve`, not saved), so closing the inventory and opening it again shows the same windows, and every bag worn opens on its own the first time the inventory opens in a delve (`BagWindows.seen` remembers which bags have been offered, so a bag the delver shut stays shut when the screen opens again, and a bag put on or picked up later opens when it is first carried); where each sits is derived from the open order and the screen each time, except a window the delver dragged. The delver's own windows are dragged by their title bar (`drag_windows`) anywhere on their side (`drag_area`): the whole right region, so never over the doll, quickslots and help whose drops they would swallow, nor onto the world side; the other party's windows cannot be dragged. A dragged window is kept at the corner it was dropped at (`BagWindows.moved`, for the delve, reset in `leave_the_delve`, not saved) whether it is open or not, so it opens there again, and is pulled back inside the area each time the screen is laid out (the stored place is kept, so a window comes back to it when the screen grows). It leaves the columns: those not dragged lay out as before, and a newly opened window takes the first free place in them. A dragged window overlaps others freely and the one taken last is in front (moving one past a click raises it; a plain click on a title bar leaves the order alone), and where a window was left is forgotten once its bag is out of reach; it takes no part in the fitting of `fit_windows` but shrinks to the same scale, and more if it alone would not fit the area. A title bar pressed and let go without moving is a click and pins nothing. While it is dragged the window's node is moved directly and the screen is rebuilt only when it is taken and let go. `FB_DRAG_WINDOW=satchel:300,200` drives such a drag for captures. A window stays open only while its bag is in reach: carried, underfoot, in the container opened in the level, or inside one of those at any depth. Set a bag down and walk off, give it in a trade, or leave the level, and its window closes. `FB_OPEN_BAGS=1` opens every carried container as a window, for captures.
- Items lying in the level are drawn 1.15 times their size about where they rest (`carry::FLOOR_SCALE`, the user's call), so they read from standing height; they still rest on the floor, and their pickup box and floor radius grow with them.
- Items lying in the level lie flat on their largest face, thinnest extent up (`carry::lie_down`): a sword or a bow on its side, a crossbow on its stock and prod, a shield on its back, face up, a tunic or a hood laid out. Things with a base of their own stand as made (`fb_items::profile::stands_on_base`): helms, hats, boots, bags, chests, lanterns, food and drink, and candles. Earlier only things more than twice as tall as wide were laid down, which stood crossbows and shields on edge. Our own rule; the plate lays nothing on a floor.
- Dragging an item from underfoot into a grid picks it up from the level; dragging anything onto the underfoot grid, or off the page onto the level, sets it down in front of the delver, to the left or right of straight ahead by the underfoot column it was let go on (up to 50 cm either way, so it stays within reach). Putting something into a bag lying underfoot leaves it in that bag on the floor.
- **One layout** (#851, #861). Every inventory screen is the same three regions (`bags::regions`, laid out in 1920 by 1080 logical pixels whatever the window): the left, `LEFT_PX` wide, is the world side; the middle, `MIDDLE_PX` (the doll's frame, 600), is the delver's header, tabs, doll, quickslots and help; the right, the rest (668 pixels less margins at 1920 by 1080), holds the delver's windows. The doll is never scaled and the quickslots are always one row of ten, so opening a barrel or starting a barter fills only the left and nothing in the middle or on the right moves or resizes. Alone, the left holds the floor underfoot as a window of its own (`bags::FLOOR`) and any bag set down. In a barter, search or open container it holds the other party: a trader's barter (the card with Accept and Cancel over the wares, in one window), a body being searched, or a container standing in the level, then the floor under it (no floor in a trader's stall) and any bag of theirs opened from them (the body's satchel), laid out automatically by the code that lays out the delver's (`bags::fit_sizes` in the left region, shrinking cells to fit, with the shelf, container or body and the floor never the ones left out). Nothing scrolls but a trader's wares in their window. The cross on a container's window shuts it; a trader's has none (Accept or Cancel ends the trade). The ledger is the same on every tab and takes the screen's full height, so the whole doll, the header and the strip fit and the header and tabs never move when switching: the character and factions tabs draw their page in the middle column under the same header, with the doll to its right. A bag set down shows on the left by itself; closing its window (the cross, or a click on the bag) records it in `BagWindows.shut` and it stays shut while it lies there, and a click opens it again.
- The screen scales with the window, where the plate's cells were a fixed 46 px (`--cs`) and ours were 52: its cells are 72 logical pixels apart in a 1920 by 1080 window (`[interface] cell_size`), and the whole screen, text and doll too, is drawn at the window's height over 1080 or width over 1920, whichever is tighter, through Bevy's `UiScale`, never so small that a cell is under 36 px (`min_cell_size`), so it keeps one layout at any resolution and all of it fits at 1280 by 720 (`InterfaceTuning::scale`). Every other screen scales the same way (`ui::InterfaceScalePlugin`). The quickslot strip and the help line sit under the doll, leaving the left leaf's height to the grids. Our own choices.
- Over the left leaf is the plate's header (`inventory::header`, after `renderIdent` and `.ident`): a portrait of the doll's head in a gold frame on the Stygian gradient, the name in the display face, a line of folk, height in feet and inches and class, and a line of armour class, load over capacity in pounds and the carried light's reach in feet (darkvision, or "No light", without one) in Plex Mono with the values bright, all from `Character::derive`. The portrait is drawn by a second camera on the doll's stage, a child of the doll so it turns with it and always sees the face, framed as the plate's `drawPortrait` frames the head from `(.32, .04, 1)`. Under it are the plate's three tabs, the inventory's lit; the outfit and character leaves are not built yet, so those tabs are labels. The plate's header line named the outfit; this one names the class.
- The hover card (`inventory::card`) is the plate's `itemInfoHTML` in its `.tip`: the item's icon on top where the plate turned a live 3D view, then the title in its rarity's colour, a legendary's old name under it, a line of rarity, noun, weight, armour, warmth, damage, light and footprint, a container's contents, each affix's line with its name (flaws in red), the remaining stats in total on one line, and a legendary's history. It sits beside the item, flipping and clamping to stay on screen as `showTip` does. The plate's buttons on the card (Wear, Drop) are left out; dragging does both. In the level the same card replaces the paper tag under the crosshair for the item in reach.
- Aiming in the level (`carry::nearest_aimed`) need not put the crosshair exactly on a thing, the user's call ("there should be a buffer around it"). Floor items, containers and folk are all aimed at by one rule: anything whose box the crosshair's line passes through, or whose nearest point to that line is within 2 degrees of it (`aim_buffer_degrees`) or 6 cm of it (`aim_min_radius`, so a ring at the feet is easy to catch), ahead of the eye and within the 2 m reach. Of several, the one nearest the line by angle wins, and of those the line passes through, the nearest. The plate's crosshair needed an exact ray hit. The numbers are our own tuning.
- F equips the item under the crosshair straight from the floor, without opening the screen (`inventory::equip_from_floor`), the user's call: into the slot it is made for (`Inventory::target_slot`, as dropping it on the doll does), so worn gear replaces what is in its slot, a weapon goes in the main hand (or the off hand, while only that is free), a torch, lantern or shield in the off hand, and a ring on whichever ring slot is free. What it replaces is stowed in the first carried bag with room, or else set down where the new item lay, so the two trade places on the floor (our own rule; a drag sets it down in front of the delver). A line low in the view says what is now worn or held and where the old one went, or why nothing happened ("That is not something you can wear."). The plate had no such key. F also opens close-ups in the turntable, which is a separate mode, so the two never meet.
- The right leaf is the plate's doll in its runic seal (`inventory::doll`, `inventory::seal`, after `renderRing` and `renderSlots`), in a 600 by 700 frame where the plate's filled the leaf. Bevy's UI draws no vector paths, so the seal's lines are rasterised once into three images, one per band, anti-aliased by distance to each stroke, with the blurred star glow as wider faint strokes; its runes are text from the same `mulberry32(7)` stream, one node per glyph round its circle, turning with its band at the plate's 300, 420 (backwards) and 200 seconds a turn. The breathing of the glow is left out. The slots sit where `data/slots.json` puts them (`fb_items::tables::slot_places`), labelled, a worn item's icon in a filled one. Dragging onto a slot equips the item if it is made for that slot; dragging onto the doll equips it in the slot it is made for. Rings fill whichever ring slot is free (`Inventory::target_slot`, the plate's `targetSlot`). Something already in the slot is swapped out, as in the plate's `commitDrop`: it goes back where the new item came from if it fits there (the slot it was worn in, or its cell in its grid, upright), and otherwise is set down underfoot, where the plate stowed it in the first bag with room; the slot under a dragged item turns green or red as the plate's `drop-ok` and `drop-bad` do. A worn item drags out like any other. Double-clicking (two clicks within 500 ms, Windows' default) wears or takes off, as the plate's help line says: something worn is stowed, or set down if there is no room; something wearable is equipped; anything else underfoot is picked up. Worn and held items add to the delver's armour class, load, carrying capacity, warmth, light and darkvision (`Character::derive`, the plate's `derive`).
- The doll is the delver's own body, dressed in what is worn and holding what is in the main and off hand (`game/src/figure.rs`: `fb_body::fit_hands`, then `fit_worn` on the body posed for both), drawn in the toon style by its own orthographic camera into the doll's image, as the icons are: on a stage far below the level on its own render layer, lit by the icons' far point key and the Armory ambient. It is framed full height (or the width it sweeps as it turns, if wider), faces the viewer, and turns about its own axis when dragged across, where the plate's doll was orbited. It is redressed whenever what is worn changes. A lit thing in either hand puts a warm point light at its flame and the warm glow on that hand's side of the seal; with none, there is no glow.
- Worn gear shows as it is worn wherever its icon is drawn: lying unturned in a grid, in its slot round the doll (whatever way it lay in the grid) and on the hover card. R turns a dragged item between the two ways its footprint lies, so it always comes back upright. Taken off by dragging it from its slot onto a grid, it lands upright where it fits there and turned where only that fits; swapped out by equipping something from a grid, it goes back to that cell upright, or turned, or else underfoot. Our own rules; the plate laid everything along its longest side.
- Icons are rendered from each item's Armory mesh in the toon style, one camera per icon drawing into an image the shape of its footprint at 144 px a cell (twice the screen's 72 px cell at 1080 lines, so an icon is sharp on a display of twice the density and only ever shrunk on others), posed with its longest side along its footprint's rows (worn gear and chests instead stand upright as worn, front to the viewer, so a coif or a tunic never lies on its side), then turned and tilted as the plate's item viewer looks (`(.32, .3, 1)`). Each is drawn for a second and kept, keyed by base, seed and turn. The plate drew its icons the same way into a cached canvas (`iconFor`). Every icon the screen needs is drawn at once, each on its own stage, so all are in place within a moment of the screen opening; until an icon first shows (its image is read back from the GPU each frame until any pixel is drawn), the item's initial stands faintly in its place. The plate drew its icons synchronously and needed no placeholder.

## Classes

`data/outfits.json`: Fighter, Mage and Cleric (the Ranger was folded into the Fighter, whose kit now carries a bow and a satchel), plus Freshly fallen and Scavenger. Each slot lists candidate bases, optionally with a leading probability. A class's set is rolled once per character, so the outfit you preview is the one you get.

`fb_items::outfit` reads the table and `Outfit::roll(seed, folk)` rolls one into whole items as the plate's `preparedSet` does: each slot filled at its chance from a picked base, a gnome's pointed hat at 60% when nothing is on its head, and an extra ring (22%), amulet (14%) and charm (14%). Each item's seed is drawn from the outfit's own stream, seeded as the plate's is; the plate rolled every item from one global stream.

**Liveries.** A faction member rolls with `Outfit::roll_in(seed, folk, odds, Some(&Livery))`, `Livery { tincture, tincture2, field, armour_lean }` being plain data that `game/src/folk.rs` builds from the faction (`fb_items` does not depend on `fb_faction`; `ArmourLean` is mirrored). It is our own, the plate had no factions. The base stream is untouched: a separate stream seeded from the seed draws everything the livery decides, so `roll_in(.., None)` equals `roll_with`. Three pieces of cloth in four (tunics, shirts, robes, cloaks and mantles, gambesons, brigandines, hoods; not leather jerkins, mail, scale or plate, or mail coifs) wear the colours: the main colour (the livery colour, the metal only if both are metals) is the body of a garment and the second its pattern colour and its trim, which half of tunics, robes, cloaks and hoods also gain; a hood is the second colour, or the main when that is a metal. Tinctures become cloth through `Tincture::dye` (softer than the paint `Tincture::rgb`; Or is weld yellow, Argent undyed wool, Sable a soft black). Every shield takes the faction's field exactly: division, tinctures and charge. Where an outfit lists a body-armour base the lean favours (padded, Gambeson; leather, Leather jerkin; mail, Mail; scale, Scale coat; plate, Cuirass or Brigandine), three members in four wear it instead of the rolled pick, and for head slots a mail lean favours the Mail coif and a plate lean the Helm. Liveried gear keeps its colours, hems and shield field through records: an `ItemRecord` stores the Armory `shape`, which `restore_item` puts back over the plain roll.
